### LinkedIn (1,743 chars)

Central Ohio's housing market posted an unusual combination in July: closed sales up, prices up, and active inventory at the highest July level in more than a decade — all at the same time.

The July 2026 Columbus REALTORS report covers the full Central Ohio metro:

**Sales & prices**
- Closed sales: 3,083 (+6.3% YoY)
- Median sale price: $350,000 (+2.3% YoY)
- YTD closed sales: +3.0% vs. same period 2025
- YTD median price: +4.4%

**Inventory & supply**
- Active listings: 6,193 — highest July count in more than a decade
- Monthly supply: 2.4 months (seller's market threshold is typically 5–6 months)
- Year-over-year inventory growth: roughly 7%

Why does this combination matter? Supply has been building, but from a historically compressed base. In 2021–22, Central Ohio supply regularly dropped below one month. The "decade high" inventory reflects how tight things were before — not that the market has swung in buyers' favor. A 2.4-month supply is still firmly seller-side.

For multifamily operators: the rent-vs-own calculation still tilts toward renting for most households. A $350,000 home at current rates (6.65%–6.69%, 30-year fixed, Freddie Mac Aug 20) requires roughly $2,300/month in principal, interest, taxes, and insurance. That exceeds what median rents command in most Columbus submarkets.

For investors and property owners: the YTD price gain of 4.4% is steady, not accelerating. That's consistent with a market absorbing new supply rather than one where demand is running unchecked.

August data releases in mid-September. The metric to watch: whether months-of-supply edges from 2.4 toward 3.0. That would be the first real signal of buyer leverage returning.
