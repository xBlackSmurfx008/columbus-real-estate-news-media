#### 1. Columbus Approves $3M Eviction Prevention Fund — City First ⚠️ NEGATIVE LEADS
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Mixed (relief it exists / anger it took this long)
**Frame:** Threat + Accountability Story

Mayor Andrew Ginther introduced nearly $3 million in housing stability investments, and Columbus City Council subsequently approved the funding. The package includes $1.5 million for Legal Aid of Southeast and Central Ohio to provide free legal representation to income-eligible tenants facing eviction — and $965,000 for the Resilient Housing Initiative, directed through a network of seven community partners: Broad Street Presbyterian Church ($202,500), Jewish Family Services ($180,000), Columbus Literacy Council ($157,500), The Homeless Families Foundation ($155,250), Gladden Community House ($90,000), St. Stephen's Community House ($90,000), and the YMCA of Central Ohio ($90,000). City officials stated this is the first time Columbus has used city general fund dollars directly for emergency rental assistance and eviction legal counsel. Context: Columbus recorded more than 25,000 evictions last year, not counting those served by mail.

**Why it leads:** Per playbook, negative sentiment about the project's subject community goes first. The community conversation is not celebrating — it is asking why a city of Columbus's size is only now funding tenant legal representation, and whether $3M scales to 25,000+ annual evictions.

#### 2. Matter News Investigation: Code Enforcement Displacing the Tenants It's Meant to Protect ⚠️ NEW SIGNAL
**Volume:** Light | **Sentiment:** Alarmed / Frustrated
**Frame:** Threat

Matter News published an investigation — confirmed indexed and being shared on Substack — examining how Columbus code enforcement actions against deteriorating affordable properties can inadvertently displace the vulnerable tenants living in them. The piece centers on a Hilltop resident (Todd Selbert) paying $650/month on Sullivant Avenue who received an eviction notice just days after the city filed a code violation lawsuit against his building. As of the article's publication, Selbert remained unable to find replacement housing and was staying in a hotel. The investigation notes the timing: the same day those evictions were filed (November 4, 2025), Columbus voters approved $500 million in affordable housing bonds. That juxtaposition — new money for new supply, tenants being pushed out of existing affordable stock — is the editorial core of the piece.

**Why it matters here:** The Matter News piece and the $3M eviction fund (Theme 1) are the same story seen from opposite sides. The fund exists precisely because enforcement-linked displacement is accelerating; the investigation documents why. No other Columbus outlet appears to have synthesized this connection.
