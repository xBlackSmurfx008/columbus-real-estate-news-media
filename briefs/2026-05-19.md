The City of Columbus is offering a business assistance program to help retailers and restaurants along Gay Street weather construction impacts. The two-mile loop — the full Capital Line — is projected to complete in 2028, with spurs connecting to German Village, Near East Side, and Franklinton to follow.

The city's business relief program is available but operators should confirm enrollment terms. Long-term, the Capital Line transforms Gay Street into a premier bike/pedestrian destination — the upside is real, but so is the two-year gap to get there.

OhioHealth is one step closer to putting Columbus's most prominent suburban corporate headquarters vacancy to work. On May 13, 2026, Columbus City Council voted to rezone the former Big Lots headquarters campus at 4860–4900 E. Dublin-Granville Road — clearing the path for the health system to develop the 25-acre property for hospital, outpatient, or other medical uses.

The vote was the final major local regulatory hurdle for OhioHealth, which purchased the northeast Columbus campus for $36 million in early 2025 — roughly a year after Big Lots vacated the property during its Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings. What gets built there, and when, remains an open question. But for the operators and investors who have been watching this ghost campus sit idle in one of Columbus's most active suburban submarkets, the rezoning is a meaningful signal.

## What Columbus City Council Approved

The May 13 vote formally enacted rezoning application Z25-036, reclassifying the campus to allow "hospital and associated accessory uses," including medical offices and outpatient services. The Columbus Development Commission had first recommended the change at its February 12 meeting; the full council vote completed the local entitlement process.

The rezoning is not a construction permit and not an approved development plan. It establishes what OhioHealth is legally allowed to build. Before any ground breaks, OhioHealth would still need to file detailed site plans, secure building permits, and clear technical reviews — including traffic, stormwater, and parking — through the city's standard development pipeline.

OhioHealth's planning facility director Doug Scholl told city commissioners that the health system is "currently studying use for the future of the site." No design concept, no program commitment, and no construction timeline has been publicly disclosed.

## What's on the Site

The Big Lots campus consists of two buildings constructed in 2017 as part of the Hamilton Quarter mixed-use development on Columbus's northeast side:

- **The office building**: a four-story, 273,000-square-foot Class A structure that housed Big Lots corporate employees
- **The support building**: a 329,348-square-foot structure that included distribution space and a structured parking garage

Both buildings were built to modern suburban specifications. Their 2017 construction dates matter: a health system evaluating adaptive reuse has a fundamentally different starting point with a 9-year-old Class A office building than with a 40-year-old suburban campus. Less structural remediation, modern MEP systems, and a clear loading infrastructure all lower the barrier to a phased conversion.

The campus sits near the intersection of E. Dublin-Granville Road and New Albany Road, adjacent to the New Albany International Business Park and roughly two miles from the heart of New Albany's data center and tech corridor. [Internal link: 2026-05-15 Ohio data center committee coverage]

## The Big Lots Arc: How OhioHealth Ended Up Here

Big Lots moved to this campus in 2017 as its corporate headquarters, relocating from its longtime Downtown Columbus offices on E. Broad Street. As the retailer's financial position deteriorated through 2023 and 2024, the company entered Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings and began exploring a sale of the property.

NBC4 first reported OhioHealth as the likely buyer in early 2025. The sale closed later that spring for $36 million — a figure that reflected both the campus's modern construction and the seller's bankruptcy-driven motivation. OhioHealth's acquisition gave it a large suburban campus on the northeastern edge of Columbus at a per-square-foot cost well below replacement cost.

The property sat largely vacant for nearly two years as OhioHealth completed the acquisition and navigated the rezoning process. The February 2026 Development Commission recommendation and the May 13 council vote close out that period. The campus now has a use entitlement. The next chapter is what gets planned.

## Why This Matters for Northeast Columbus CRE

**The ghost campus discount has tightened.** A 25-acre vacant campus in a submarket that otherwise absorbs new development aggressively — the New Albany corridor, Hamilton Quarter, the I-270/Route 161 interchange area — carries a meaningful uncertainty discount as long as its future use is unresolved. Institutional ownership plus a cleared rezoning narrows that discount, even without a committed program. Adjacent parcel owners, retail operators in Hamilton Quarter, and investors evaluating nearby land opportunities are now working with a different risk profile.

**Healthcare is the highest-and-best-use argument for this site.** The campus's scale (25 acres), parking infrastructure (the support building includes a garage), suburban location with regional road access, and modern construction align well with OhioHealth's criteria for a hospital or large outpatient campus. The northeast Columbus area — encompassing New Albany, Gahanna, and the eastern 270 ring — is among Columbus's fastest-growing residential markets. OhioHealth does not currently have a significant inpatient presence in this geography, which is precisely the kind of market gap that drives healthcare real estate acquisitions of this type.

**The data center adjacency creates a utility intersection.** The former Big Lots campus sits in a geographic corridor that is simultaneously processing a massive data center build-out in New Albany and Licking County. [Internal link: 2026-05-15 data center committee] Columbus is already evaluating a reclaimed-water infrastructure program specifically to serve data center cooling demand. A healthcare campus activation adjacent to the data center corridor means OhioHealth and hyperscale tech operators could become neighbors — a dynamic that will affect utility planning, traffic infrastructure, and land use in this submarket for years.

**The contrast with Capitol Square is instructive.** Across town, Edwards Companies shelved the $600 million Capitol Square Renaissance Project in April 2026, citing market conditions — and the May 3 CRE News brief covered what that pause means for downtown Columbus operators. [Internal link: 2026-05-03 Capitol Square shelved] The northeast Columbus story runs in exactly the opposite direction: an institutional buyer with a health system's balance sheet, modern buildings, and a cleared entitlement is a fundamentally different risk profile than a market-rate developer facing debt service pressure on a ground-up downtown megaproject. Same metro, same spring — very different trajectories.

**The "plans unsettled" caveat is not a red flag but it is real.** Health systems routinely complete real estate acquisitions and rezonings before committing to capital programs. OhioHealth is running standard process. But it does mean the campus could remain quiet for another twelve to twenty-four months while the organization works through internal planning, capital allocation, and design development. Operators underwriting adjacent opportunities on a short timeline should factor in that activation is not imminent.

## What Comes Next

OhioHealth now holds the zoning entitlement it needs. The visible next milestones in the public record:

1. **Site plan filing**: OhioHealth submits a development plan to Columbus Building and Zoning Services, triggering the city's technical review process including traffic, utilities, and stormwater.
2. **Building permit application**: Follows site plan approval; individual permits for demolition, renovation, or new construction.
3. **Capital program announcement**: OhioHealth has not disclosed whether this will be a new hospital building, a major outpatient campus, a phased mixed-use health district, or some combination. That announcement — when it comes — will be the most consequential news peg for operators watching this submarket. A hospital groundbreaking in northeast Columbus would be among the largest healthcare real estate events in Central Ohio this decade.

CRE News will track site plan filings and OhioHealth capital announcements through the city's development pipeline as they emerge.
