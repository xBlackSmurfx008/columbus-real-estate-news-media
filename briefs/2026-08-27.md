### 1. Mercy on Main, Olde Towne East — **★ COVER TODAY (Neighborhoods slot)**

**Coverage signal:** Cover today — Neighborhoods slot; OHFA LIHTC award confirmed (monitoring trigger from Aug 25 brief)
**Confidence:** 4/5
**What:** The Ohio Housing Finance Agency conditionally awarded 9% Low-Income Housing Tax Credits to Mercy on Main, a proposed 65-unit affordable apartment building at 907–917 E. Main St. in Columbus' Olde Towne East neighborhood. The award — confirmed in the OHFA's May 26, 2026 news release — is one of 25 Ohio projects sharing $39.1 million in annual federal 9% tax credits, selected from 62 statewide applications. The full financing package also includes a $4 million permanent mortgage and $3.25 million in City of Columbus HOME funds, covering the $22.8 million project budget.
**Why it matters:** The OHFA LIHTC award is the primary financing milestone that confirms Mercy on Main can move toward closing and construction. The Zone In Phase 1 connection (by-right 5-story height on E. Main St.) is the original CREN editorial angle — this is the first confirmed case where Zone In Phase 1's by-right corridor allowances have attracted an out-of-market LIHTC developer to a site that previously would have required a variance. Zone In Phase 2 is currently in public comment (through Oct 24), making this a live example of Phase 1's effects.
**Developer:** Kingsley + Co. (Chinedum Ndukwe, founder — Notre Dame grad, former Cincinnati Bengals/Oakland Raiders defensive tackle, retired 2012, central Ohio roots). Project named for Mercy Okoronkwo, Ndukwe's grandmother.
**Unit mix:** 8 studio, 29 1BR, 18 2BR, 10 3BR. Income targeting: 50–80% AMI. Projected rents: $867–$1,522/month.
**Transit:** Site is on planned East Main BRT route; Near East Transit Center nearby.

## Social posts

### LinkedIn (~1,250 chars)

Ohio's Housing Finance Agency has awarded 9% Low-Income Housing Tax Credits to Mercy on Main — a proposed 65-unit affordable apartment building planned for 907–917 E. Main St. in Columbus' Olde Towne East neighborhood.

The $22.8 million project by Kingsley + Co. (Cincinnati) would bring 8 studio, 29 one-bedroom, 18 two-bedroom, and 10 three-bedroom units to the E. Main corridor, with rents targeting households at 50–80% of Area Median Income ($867–$1,522/month).

The Zone In connection is the story under the story: before Zone In Phase One, a five-story building on E. Main St. required a zoning variance. After the July 2024 reform established by-right height allowances on main street corridors, Mercy on Main could proceed as-of-right — no hearing needed. Kingsley + Co.'s press release cited the Zone In designation as a direct reason for choosing the East Main site.

With Zone In Phase Two now open for public comment (through October 24), this is a real example of what Phase One already made possible.

The developer, Chinedum Ndukwe, is a Notre Dame graduate and former Cincinnati Bengals and Oakland Raiders defensive tackle. He grew up in central Ohio. The project is named for his grandmother, Mercy Okoronkwo.
