**1. Ohio Data Center Ballot Campaign Goes Live — 413K Signatures Due July 1**
Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / alarmed**
Frame: **THREAT** (data center land rush competes with housing in New Albany; power grid concerns are a live political fight with a hard deadline)

The most time-sensitive signal in today's scan. As of April 30, 2026, advocates launched a petition drive for the *Ohio Prohibition of Data Center Construction Amendment*, a proposed November 2026 constitutional ballot measure that would ban new data centers exceeding 25 MW of aggregate power demand. They need 413,488 valid signatures by July 1 — six weeks out. Simultaneously: ~18 Ohio municipalities have enacted or are considering moratoriums; the Ohio House unanimously passed a bill creating a data center study commission (now in Senate); and Ohio Capital Journal, ABC6, Signal Ohio, and State House News Bureau have all published in the past week on community backlash.

This is a direct Columbus real estate story: Meta's Prometheus data center (the world's first 1GW facility, New Albany), EdgeConneX's 524K sq ft warehouse conversion (April 2026 completion), Vantage's $2B/192MW campus (70 acres), and Google's 85-acre land acquisition in New Albany are all in or adjacent to submarkets where residential land is being competed for and utilities are under pressure. Columbus is projected to become the #2 data center hub in the Great Lakes region after Chicago per a January 2026 WOSU report.

> "Many have seen the industry's impact in rising power and water bills, and data centers have drawn criticism for years — namely, that they use too much water, and don't deliver many permanent jobs in exchange for the generous tax breaks they've traditionally received." — Signal Ohio, *"Ohio's data center boom is running into political resistance"* (signalohio.org, 2026)

> "Ohio data center ban advocates are trying to get 413,000 signatures by July 1." — Ohio Capital Journal headline, April 30, 2026 (ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/04/30/ohio-data-center-ban-proposal-advocates-are-trying-to-get-413000-signatures-by-july-1/)

> "Proposed data centers are roiling otherwise sleepy public hearings and raising difficult questions about electricity, water, and noise." — The Statehouse News Bureau, *"Ohio towns are pushing back against data centers"* (statenews.org, February 2026)

Sources: Ohio Capital Journal (April 30, 2026), Signal Ohio, ABC6, Statehouse News Bureau, Axios Columbus (March 4, 2026), Ballotpedia, Electropages — multiple distinct outlets.

**2. Ohio Legislature's Dueling Housing Bills — Votes Expected Mid-May**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Partisan / mixed**
Frame: **Opportunity** (both bills affect Columbus supply pipeline; CRE News can translate the political fight into what operators actually need to track)

Ohio's General Assembly returns from recess in mid-May and is expected to consider two competing housing affordability bills. The Democratic proposal (Rep. Karen Brownlee) would create a $100M revolving loan fund for single-family homes, duplexes, and small apartment buildings — framing it as capital for the missing-middle supply gap. The Republican counter (HB 361, Reps. Brian Lorenz R-Powell and Tex Fischer R-Canfield) would require local governments to streamline approval processes for new housing developments, reducing friction without direct state spending. Ohio Capital Journal, WOWKTV, NBC4, WOUB, and multiple editorial boards all covered the collision in early April; the legislative calendar now puts it live in Columbus conversation this week.

**6. ADU Legalization Is Live — But Practical Questions Are Going Unanswered**
Volume: **LIGHT** | Sentiment: **Curious / underserved**
Frame: **Content gap** (high search interest, low-quality local coverage; CRE News can own this)

Columbus legalized ADUs by right in all residential and apartment residential zoning districts in November 2025 (Columbus City Council vote; WOSU November 24, 2025). The rule change is significant: no additional off-street parking required, units up to 1,000 sq ft or 65% of the primary unit's floor area, max height 25 ft. This is now law, but online conversation reveals a gap between what's permitted and what homeowners and small investors can actually execute. Historic districts (German Village, Italian Village) still require separate commission approval. Lender appetite for ADU appraisal comps is thin. Permitting timelines in practice are not yet documented locally.

> "Columbus City Council votes to remove red tape for accessory dwelling units." — WOSU, November 24, 2025 (wosu.org/politics-government/2025-11-24/columbus-city-council-legislation-could-remove-red-tape-for-accessory-dwelling-units)

> "No additional off-street parking is required when an ADU is added to a property in Columbus — a change that has a major effect on feasibility, particularly on small or alley-loaded lots." — Steadily (steadily.com/blog/adu-laws-regulations-columbus, 2026 guide)

> "Over 80% of respondents support converting commercial buildings to housing, allowing more apartments near job and transit centers, allowing churches and nonprofits to build homes more easily, adding apartments to business areas, and giving homeowners the right to build an Accessory Dwelling Unit." — AHACO (ahaco.org/overwhelming)
