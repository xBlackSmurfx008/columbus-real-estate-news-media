# Poindexter Village Museum Breaks Ground in King-Lincoln

Ohio History Connection broke ground yesterday — May 6, 2026 — on the Poindexter Village African American Museum at 290 N. Champion Ave. in the King-Lincoln Bronzeville neighborhood on Columbus's Near East Side.

It is Ohio History Connection's 59th historic site and its first in Columbus. The $25.9 million project will convert two surviving buildings from the original 35-building Poindexter Village complex into a museum featuring exhibition halls, community gathering spaces, an oral history studio, a research lab, and an art gallery. Target opening: early 2028.

Architect: **Moody Nolan** (Columbus-based; the nation's largest Black-owned architecture firm). Construction manager: **Smoot Construction** (Columbus-based). Both are among Central Ohio's most active institutional project firms.

For Columbus real estate operators: this is a neighborhood-catalyst story in a corridor that has been absorbing meaningful investment — and it comes with named Columbus firms on both the design and construction sides.

---

## What Broke Ground Yesterday in King-Lincoln

The May 6 groundbreaking brought together Ohio History Connection, the Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA), and representatives of the James Preston Poindexter Foundation — the advocacy organization that spent years pushing for preservation of the site before CMHA ultimately sold the buildings to Ohio History Connection.

CMHA announced an additional $500,000 donation at the ceremony, raising its total commitment to $1.5 million [needs source — confirm prior donation amount with Ohio History Connection]. Total funds raised as of the groundbreaking: $14.5 million — approximately 56 percent of the $25.9 million goal. Ohio History Connection is continuing to pursue the remaining $11.4 million through philanthropic, state, and federal sources.

---

## The Buildings Being Saved — and What They Represent

Poindexter Village was dedicated in 1940 by President Franklin D. Roosevelt as the first public housing project in Columbus and among the first federally developed public housing communities in the United States. At its peak, the complex stretched across 27 acres with 35 buildings in the King-Lincoln Bronzeville neighborhood — a historic center of Black cultural and commercial life in Columbus.

Named after Rev. James Preston Poindexter — a Columbus abolitionist, civil rights activist, and Baptist minister — the village housed generations of Near East Side families. Beginning in 2013, CMHA demolished most of the original buildings and replaced them with contemporary housing.

Two structures survived. The James Preston Poindexter Foundation, formed by community members and descendants of original residents, advocated for their preservation for years. Ohio History Connection acquired both buildings and is now adapting them into the museum.

## $25.9M Project: Where the Money Stands

At $25.9 million, this is a full adaptive reuse and program buildout — not a preservation stabilization. The museum will include:

- Exhibition halls covering public housing history, urban renewal, and African American heritage in Central Ohio
- An oral history studio
- A research laboratory
- An art gallery
- Community gathering and programming spaces

With $14.5 million in hand, the project has crossed the majority threshold. The remaining $11.4 million will likely require a combination of Historic Tax Credits, state capital budget appropriations, and private philanthropy. Ohio History Connection has not announced a timeline for completing the capital campaign.

For local investors tracking Historic Tax Credit opportunities in Columbus: this project's HTC use — if confirmed — would be a reference transaction in a neighborhood that is not yet saturated with HTC-financed adaptive reuse.

## The Firms Behind the Build: Moody Nolan and Smoot Construction

The architectural firm is **Moody Nolan**, headquartered in Columbus and recognized as the nation's largest Black-owned architecture firm. Moody Nolan's portfolio includes significant institutional work at Ohio State University, Nationwide Children's Hospital, and across Central Ohio's public and cultural sectors. Their selection here is both a civic signal and a practical one — deep familiarity with Columbus's institutional approval and community engagement processes.

The construction manager is **Smoot Construction**, another Columbus-based firm with a strong institutional track record including OSU medical center work and civic projects throughout the region. Smoot's involvement as CM from the groundbreaking stage suggests the construction phase is actively being planned — not deferred.

For contractors and subcontractors active in the Columbus market: this project is moving. Watch for subcontractor RFP activity through summer 2026 as Smoot Construction mobilizes.

## What the Museum Means for the King-Lincoln Corridor

Cultural institutions are neighborhood anchors. Columbus has local precedent: Italian Village and Franklinton both absorbed waves of residential and retail investment that tracked behind institutional and arts investment by five to ten years. The Poindexter Village museum brings that dynamic to King-Lincoln Bronzeville.

The city has cited the Near East Side as part of nearly $765 million in public and private investment flowing into historically disinvested Columbus neighborhoods over the past decade. A $25.9 million cultural anchor at 290 N. Champion Ave. — backed by Ohio History Connection and CMHA — adds institutional credibility to that investment narrative and gives the corridor a concrete 2028 milestone.
