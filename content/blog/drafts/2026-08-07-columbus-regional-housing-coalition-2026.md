# Columbus Region Needs 20,000 New Homes a Year, Leaders Say

Columbus civic and business leaders made housing their top priority this week. The message: central Ohio needs to build 200,000 new homes by 2034.

That is roughly 20,000 new units every year. Right now, the region is not hitting that number.

The push came through a coordinated effort by the 83-member Columbus Partnership, reported by Columbus CEO on August 4, 2026. The effort revives and amplifies the Regional Housing Coalition launched by Mayor Andrew Ginther in November 2024.

## Why 20,000 Units a Year?

The math comes from one projection. The Mid-Ohio Regional Planning Commission (MORPC) expects the Columbus region to reach 3.1 million residents by 2050. More people need more homes.

Developer Brent Crawford, a Columbus Partnership member, said the region needs "housing of all types and price points" to bring costs down. That means affordable units. Workforce housing. Market-rate apartments. For-sale homes. All of it.

The coalition's goal is 200,000 new homes over ten years across the entire Columbus metro — not just inside city limits, but across Franklin County and surrounding areas.

## The Coalition: Two Years In

The Regional Housing Coalition is a nonprofit formed under the city's umbrella. Its job is to coordinate housing supply, affordability, and equity efforts across multiple jurisdictions.

Mayor Ginther launched the coalition in November 2024. More than a year later, the effort had not yet hired staff or released a public work plan, according to earlier reporting by Hoodline. The August Partnership push is an attempt to regain momentum with private-sector backing.

That private-sector angle matters. Columbus can only control what happens inside city limits. Much of the projected growth will happen in suburbs — Westerville, Dublin, Gahanna, Grove City, and beyond. Getting those municipalities to build more housing requires regional coordination. An April 2026 Hoodline report noted that Mayor Ginther's earlier push to expand housing in the suburbs "hit a wall." The Columbus Partnership brings corporate clout to a conversation that political pressure alone has not moved.

## What the Market Shows Right Now

The supply gap is real. Homes for sale in central Ohio recently reached about 5,497 — a 19.5 percent increase from the same period in 2024, according to Columbus REALTORS® data. Days on market climbed to 40 days, up 29 percent year-over-year.

That sounds like a market loosening. But context matters.

The months' supply of inventory edged up to only 2.0. A balanced market typically needs four to six months of supply. Columbus is still supply-constrained, even with the improvement. For buyers, more homes mean more choices and more room to negotiate. For sellers and landlords, it means more competition. For developers and operators, it confirms: the demand is there. The gap is real.

## What This Means for Columbus Operators

If you own or manage rental property in central Ohio, the coalition's work will shape your market over the next decade.

More supply tends to moderate rent growth. That is the stated goal. For investors counting on appreciation through scarcity, that is a headwind. For renters and residents, it is relief.

For builders and developers, the coalition's push could unlock policy changes — faster permitting, rezoning support, infrastructure funding — that help projects pencil out. The Zone In zoning overhaul is already moving toward Phase 2, which will cover 43 percent of Columbus's commercial and industrial land. Phase 3, which addresses single-family zoning, is set to begin in 2027. Those changes will determine whether a given parcel can support 2 units or 20.

One existing model worth watching: Fairfield County added 1,666 new residences from January 2025 through March 2026 — affordable, workforce, and market-rate units combined. That pace, replicated across the metro, would make a meaningful dent in the 200,000-unit target.

## What to Watch

Three things to track over the coming months:

**Coalition staffing and work plan.** The coalition has not yet published a public roadmap. When it does, the specifics will reveal how serious the commitment is — and which suburbs are at the table.

**Zone In Phase 2.** New zoning districts for commercial and industrial areas are being drafted now. Community input rounds are expected in late 2026. The rules set here will govern what gets built on nearly half the city's commercial land.

**Suburban alignment.** The Columbus Partnership's backing adds private-sector credibility to the regional push. Whether suburban municipalities move on permitting and zoning reforms in response is the deciding variable.

The Columbus region has the growth to need 20,000 homes a year. Building them is the hard part.
