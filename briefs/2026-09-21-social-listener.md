**2. Rental Registry Passed — Landlord Litigation Threat Active**
**Volume: LOUD | Sentiment: MIXED (tenant advocates positive; landlords negative/hostile)**
**Frame: Threat (for landlord audience) + opportunity (for tenant-focused coverage)**

Columbus City Council passed the residential rental registry ordinance on approximately April 20, 2026. The law requires all rental property owners to register annually ($15/unit, max $1,500/complex) and submit to triennial common-area inspections. The Columbus Apartment Association retained outside counsel and has signaled litigation. The conversation is still active — landlords citing state law preemption; tenant advocates and BREAD celebrating.

> "It doesn't fit with state law, so it creates more administrative burden. The city is then going to create administrative fees for that burden, which are not in the law. The city gets to decide what fees they're passing."
> — Attorney for landlord groups, quoted by NBC4/Columbus Apartment Association ([source](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-oks-rental-registry-to-hold-landlords-accountable/))

> "Many Columbus rental properties are owned by out-of-state investors, and having access to basic contact information is crucial to living in a safe home."
> — Legal advocates quoted in Columbus Today coverage ([source](https://nationaltoday.com/us/oh/columbus/news/2026/02/23/columbus-rental-registry-proposal-sparks-debate-between-landlords-and-tenant-advocates/))

**Editorial note:** The litigation threat is the live hook — if the Columbus Apartment Association files suit, that's a story. Content gap: a plain-language explainer on what the ordinance requires is among the most-searched questions in this space and doesn't exist yet in Columbus local media at scale.

**3. Zone In Phase 2 — 60-Day Public Comment Window Open, Far East Engaged**
**Volume: STEADY | Sentiment: MIXED (pro-housing curious; some neighborhood anxiety)**
**Frame: Content idea + opportunity**

The Zone In Phase 2 public review draft covers 70,000+ parcels (41% of city land). The 60-day public comment window runs August 25 – October 24, 2026 — it is open right now. Phase 1 drew 1,900+ comments; Phase 2 is larger. The Far East Area Commission held a discussion on how to shape the neighborhood's future under Phase 2 and separately endorsed the "Our City Our Say" (Issue 8) ballot initiative alongside Livingston and University Area Commissions.

> "Residents told the City that they wanted more affordability, more connectivity, more sustainability, and more homes."
> — City of Columbus Zone In FAQ ([source](https://zone-in-columbus.hub.arcgis.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions))

> "The city's planners turned their attention to mixed-use, industrial and warehouse areas, in addition to looking at several large business and institutional campuses."
> — Columbus Underground Zone In Phase 2 summary ([source](https://columbusunderground.com/next-phase-of-zoning-reform-will-cover-43-of-city-bw1/))

**Editorial note:** The active comment window is the news peg. A how-to-comment guide for Columbus residents — especially for Far East, Northland, and south Columbus neighborhoods where Phase 2 coverage is heaviest — is a clear content gap that serves CRE News's audience.

**4. "Our City Our Say" Issue 8 — Council Reform Ballot Fight, Housing Implications Debated**
**Volume: LOUD | Sentiment: MIXED (activists positive; council and some labor/Black community leaders negative)**
**Frame: Threat + content idea**

The Our City Our Say coalition submitted 23,000+ signatures to force a November 2026 ballot vote on switching Columbus City Council from citywide-elected to district-only representation. The competing measure — Council's own 13-seat hybrid proposal — is also on the ballot. Multiple area commissions, labor unions, and civic groups are taking public positions.

The real estate housing development debate is the least-covered angle: does district-only voting empower neighborhood NIMBYism at the expense of citywide housing goals?

> "Council President Pro Tem Rob Dorans cited transit, housing, and workforce development as examples of issues requiring the four at-large seats to preserve a citywide voice."
> — WOSU, Columbus City Council hybrid model announcement ([source](https://www.wosu.org/politics-government/2026-08-21/columbus-city-council-proposes-a-13-member-council-with-new-district-map-and-more-reforms))

> "District-only elections give neighborhoods a representative who answers to them directly... running in one district costs less than a citywide campaign — lowering the barrier to run."
> — Our City Our Say coalition, reported by WOSU ([source](https://www.wosu.org/politics-government/2026-07-16/our-city-our-say-submits-23-000-signatures-to-force-a-vote-on-how-columbus-elects-city-council/))

**Editorial note:** The governance story has a direct real estate angle that has not been written in local real estate media. Labor and Black community leaders raising concerns is negative community sentiment that must be represented with equal prominence. CRE News is well-positioned to cover this from a development and housing policy lens.

**6. Affordable Housing Gap — 53,000-Unit Shortage, CLT Model Getting Attention**
**Volume: STEADY | Sentiment: FRUSTRATED / CURIOUS**
**Frame: Content idea + opportunity**

The COHHIO 2026 Gap Report (published this year) found Columbus lacks 53,000 affordable units. Only 27 affordable units exist for every 100 extremely low-income renters; 73% of Ohio's ELI renters spend over half their income on rent. At the same time, the Central Ohio Community Land Trust's $199K East Side condos (Broad & Gould) and the Motherful co-housing project (100 Noe Bixby Rd.) are generating curiosity about alternative ownership models.

> "Columbus lacks 53,000 affordable units, with only 27 affordable units available for every 100 extremely low-income renters."
> — COHHIO 2026 Gap Report ([source](https://cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/))

> "The land trust develops housing and retains ownership of the land, while eligible families purchase the structures, allowing people to buy homes at a below market rate price in areas that they would otherwise be priced out of."
> — COCLT description, CW Columbus ([source](https://cwcolumbus.com/news/local/home-affordability-crisis-continues-as-city-announces-199k-condos-coming-to-the-east-side))

**Editorial note:** There is a strong unmet need for plain-language explainers on CLT homeownership and co-housing models. CRE News's Motherful story (stranded Sep 21) and Broad & Gould story (stranded Sep 16) are both relevant here.
