## The $430M Merchant Building Is Almost Open — Here's What It Changes for Downtown Columbus Real Estate

Three years ago, Rockbridge and Edwards Companies broke ground on the most ambitious mixed-use project Downtown Columbus had seen in a generation. By this fall, the Merchant Building should be open.

The 32-story tower at Columbus's North Market is tracking toward a Q4 2026 delivery. At $430 million — up from the original $345 million announced at project launch — it is the single largest active development in the city's urban core. When it opens, it will simultaneously reset Downtown apartment rental comps, introduce the first significant new Class A office supply in years, and more than double the footprint of one of Columbus's most beloved institutions.

Here's what Columbus operators, investors, and residents need to understand about what's coming.

---

## What the Merchant Building Actually Is

The Merchant Building is a genuine mixed-use project, not a multifamily tower with a coffee shop on the ground floor. The program is stacked:

- **174 rental apartments** — the residential component occupies upper floors of the 32-story tower
- **206-room boutique hotel** — brand not yet publicly disclosed, but positioned to serve business travelers and visitors to the National Veterans Memorial and Museum, COSI, and the Short North
- **65,000 sq ft of Class A office** — roughly equivalent to three full floors of a mid-size suburban office park, concentrated in Columbus's most walkable urban address
- **350-space parking garage** — structured parking serving all uses
- **15,000 sq ft North Market expansion** — a two-story addition to the 148-year-old public market, designed to accommodate eight to nine new vendor spaces

The developer team is Rockbridge — a Columbus-based investment firm with deep experience in hospitality and mixed-use — and Edwards Companies, a Columbus developer whose portfolio includes projects across the city's core. The project was designed by NBBJ, the Seattle-based architecture firm responsible for high-profile work for Amazon, Google, and a growing list of institutional developers nationwide.

Construction began in 2023. Four years from groundbreaking to opening for a 32-story urban tower is an aggressive schedule.

---

## The Cost Escalation: $345M Became $430M

When Rockbridge and Edwards announced the project, the price tag was $345 million. It is now $430 million — an $85 million increase, roughly 25% over the original budget.

Cost escalation on large urban construction projects has become a fact of life across Columbus and every peer market since 2021. Labor costs, structural steel pricing, and supply chain disruptions have added meaningful percentages to projects that started budgeting in a different cost environment. The Merchant Building is not an outlier, but the scale of the number — $85 million in absolute terms — is worth operators noting as a benchmark when underwriting any Downtown mixed-use project today. ## What 174 Apartments at North Market Mean for the Downtown Comp Set

Downtown Columbus multifamily has not had a true luxury rental product delivered at this address before. The closest comps are the GreenHouse Short North, which opened earlier in 2026 — where studios are leasing around $1,240/month and large one-bedrooms with office space are reaching $3,658/month.

The Merchant Building's apartments carry a location premium over Short North: direct access to North Market's vendor hall, walkability scores that outperform nearly any other Columbus address, and views from upper floors that include the Scioto River, Franklinton, and the downtown skyline. [See our earlier coverage of Downtown multifamily trends for context.]

For Columbus multifamily operators broadly, the Merchant Building sets a new ceiling. Any Downtown apartment that was previously at the top of the comp set will be reset downward relative to 174 units that can legitimately claim to be the highest-quality new rental product in the city. That affects underwriting on everything from lease-up timelines to stabilized cap rate assumptions.

Columbus's overall rental market is averaging around $1,425/month as of May 2026, down approximately 2% year-over-year as new supply comes online. The Merchant Building will lean against that trend at the luxury tier while adding to the aggregate supply pressure at midrange price points.

## The Office Play in a 19% Vacancy Market

Downtown Columbus's office market is carrying an 18.46% vacancy rate as of Q1 2026 — below the overall Columbus market average of 19.18%, but still elevated by historical standards. The national conversation about office being "dead" does not map cleanly onto Columbus, where 21 tenants are currently searching for a combined 271,000 square feet specifically in the urban core.

The Merchant Building's 65,000 square feet of Class A office adds meaningful new product to a market where quality is increasingly concentrated in a small number of buildings. Columbus's office market is in a "flight to quality" environment: vacancy rates at Class B and C buildings are significantly higher than at Class A. New, amenitized, transit-adjacent Class A supply gets absorbed more predictably than the aggregate vacancy number suggests.

A Class A office tenant in the Merchant Building gets walking access to North Market's lunch and dinner vendors, the hotel's conference and hospitality amenities, structured parking, and a Short North address that helps with employee recruitment. That combination is hard to replicate at the suburban campus sites that still dominate Columbus's office stock.

## The North Market Expansion Is the Hidden Story

The Merchant Building's apartments and hotel get the most ink, but the 15,000 sq ft North Market expansion may be the component with the longest-lasting neighborhood impact.

North Market has operated continuously since 1876. It is a public-trust institution — governed by a nonprofit — and one of Columbus's only year-round public markets. On any given Saturday, it draws thousands of shoppers, tourists, and residents to the neighborhood. It has historically operated at or beyond capacity, with vendor waitlists stretching for years.

The two-story expansion will add space for eight to nine new vendor stalls. That is not a small increment for a market of North Market's size — it represents roughly a 10% increase in vendor capacity [needs source on current vendor count], and it means new food, specialty goods, and artisan businesses will establish roots in the district.

More foot traffic on more days creates compounding effects for every ground-floor retail operator within a three-block radius. When the North Market expansion opens alongside the hotel (which will drive weekday food and beverage demand) and the apartments (which will drive everyday neighborhood spending), the entire district's pedestrian environment will be meaningfully different from what it is today.

Operators considering ground-floor retail along Spruce Street, North Fourth Street, and the Short North's southern edge should be modeling what the Merchant Building's activation means for their own lease-up timelines. [See our earlier coverage of Short North ground-floor retail trends.]

## What Comes Next in the North Market District

The Merchant Building does not stand alone. The North Market District is becoming a defined node in Columbus's urban geography, and the Merchant Building's opening will accelerate that.

To the south and west, The Peninsula development on Scioto Peninsula — which has already delivered Phase I apartments — is preparing to break ground on Phase II: a $211M, 14-story building from Daimler Group and Flaherty & Collins that will include 250 apartments above a 20,000 sq ft Giant Eagle Market District grocery on the ground floor. [See our full story on Peninsula Phase II.] Construction is expected to begin in 2026, with completion targeted for 2027–2028.

The corridor between North Market and Scioto Peninsula — roughly along Spruce and Gay Streets — is quietly becoming one of the most active development stretches in Columbus. That is partly a function of Zone In Phase I zoning reform, which removed height caps that previously limited development in this area to 72 feet.

The Merchant Building is the anchor that validates the zone. Its opening this fall will be the signal that sends the next wave of capital into the district.

---

## What to Watch Before Q4

A few things Columbus operators and investors should track as the Merchant Building approaches its opening:

**Hotel brand announcement**: The operator and flag for the 206-room boutique hotel has not been publicly disclosed. Once named, it will give a strong indication of the rate tier and customer profile the Merchant Building is targeting. That determines the hotel's impact on neighboring hospitality assets.

**Pre-leasing velocity on apartments**: In Columbus, Class A apartment projects at this price point have been leasing quickly when they hit the market — GreenHouse Short North was at 30% leased shortly after opening. The Merchant Building's lease-up rate will be a useful barometer for how much runway the Downtown luxury tier still has.

**North Market vendor announcements**: New vendors selected for the expansion will tell you a lot about the market's positioning — farm-to-table, specialty food, or artisan goods each attract a different customer profile and generate different weekday vs. weekend traffic patterns.

**Opening timeline confirmation**: Q4 2026 is four quarters away from when construction started getting serious attention, and three months is a wide band. Watch for Rockbridge and Edwards to firm up the opening window over the summer.

The Merchant Building is the most consequential single delivery in Downtown Columbus real estate in this cycle. Its opening this fall will be a before-and-after moment for the district.
