### Top themes

#### 1. Rent crisis / affordability gap — LOUD | FRUSTRATED | **THREAT + CONTENT OPPORTUNITY**

Columbus's aggregate rent numbers look relatively affordable (~$1,366 average), but the gap between those numbers and lived reality is generating sustained, cross-platform frustration. The dominant community frame: "the city looks cheap on paper but we're still being left behind."

**Signal sources:** NewsBreak community commentary, COHHIO 2026 Gap Report (covered Spectrum News 1), NBC4 affordability segments, Matter News, Affordable Housing Alliance of Central Ohio, Axios Columbus.

**Volume basis:** Multiple distinct outlets and advocacy organizations; NBC4 editorial board and policy-facing orgs amplifying the same message in the same week. Not a single author.

**Key facts driving the conversation:**
- Columbus has a deficit of ~53,000 affordable rental units (COHHIO 2026 Gap Report)
- A renter needs to earn $27.50/hr to afford a two-bedroom; the average Columbus renter earns $19.59/hr — a $7.91/hr gap
- Median home price hit ~$304–341K in 2026 (range across sources), up 51% since 2020
- 320,000 homes short for buyers earning ~$75,000/year

**Quotes (verbatim, with sources):**

> "The numbers look affordable, but thousands of renters are still being left behind."
> — NewsBreak community contributor, "Columbus Rent Crisis 2026," [newsbreak.com](https://www.newsbreak.com/crafting-your-home-355087212/4808651217814-columbus-rent-crisis-2026-the-numbers-look-affordable-but-thousands-of-renters-are-still-being-left-behind)

> "The need for more affordable housing is clear, especially for low-income individuals."
> — Executive Director, Affordable Housing Alliance of Central Ohio, via COHHIO / Spectrum News 1 coverage (March 2026), [spectrumnews1.com](https://spectrumnews1.com/oh/columbus/news/2026/03/05/ohio-affordable-rental-shortage-report)

> "Columbus home prices have surged 51% since 2020, while rents continue to climb, squeezing middle-income buyers and renters."
> — CRE Daily brief, "Housing Affordability Worsens As Columbus Home Prices Surge," [credaily.com](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/housing-affordability-worsens-as-columbus-home-prices-surge/)

**Project frame:** This is the biggest content gap on CRE News right now. We cover development supply stories well (Stone Ridge, Second Baptist, INTRO Columbus, LOT) but have not published a dedicated explainer on who Columbus's affordability crisis is actually hitting — by income band, by neighborhood, with current numbers. The community is asking this question loudly.

The city approved the Columbus Growth Strategy over divided neighborhood feedback, and a Fall 2026 Council vote approaches.

**Signal sources:** WOSU, Columbus Underground, AOL/Dispatch, CitizenPortal, Columbus Growth Strategy documentation.

**Volume basis:** Area commission letters, city council proceedings, multiple editorial stances. Multiple authors on multiple platforms.

#### 3. Franklin County triennial property tax reassessment — STEADY | ANXIOUS/FRUSTRATED | **CONTENT OPPORTUNITY**

Tentative property values were mailed June 9, 2026. The September deadline for homeowners to challenge valuations has just passed or is passing now. Community anxiety is high — especially among owners who absorbed the 2023 reappraisal's ~43% average increase and are now facing another adjustment. Investor community watching board of revision appeal deadlines carefully.

**Signal sources:** NBC4, Fox28, Franklin County Auditor's website, Axios Columbus (May 2026), Samantha Cooper Realtor blog (frequently cited), AppealDesk.

**Volume basis:** Multiple homeowner-facing news segments, realtor advisories, and advocacy pieces from distinct authors.

**Quotes (verbatim, with sources):**

> "Scott Durant bought his Worthington home in 2014 for $205,000 and has seen his tentative property value increase year after year, with this year's evaluation listing his property value at just under $500,000."
> — Fox28 Columbus, "Local homeowners concerned over rising property values (and potential tax increases)," [myfox28columbus.com](https://myfox28columbus.com/news/local/homeowners-concerned-over-tentative-property-value-increases-franklin-county-wsyx-auditor-assessment-appraisals-taxes)

> "The 2026 Triennial Property Value Update has caused some genuine homeowner anxiety in Central Ohio, especially after the dramatic 2023 reappraisal that saw average residential values jump roughly 43% across Franklin County."
> — AppealDesk, "Franklin County 2026 Triennial Update: Appeal Your Value," [appealdesk.com](https://www.appealdesk.com/appeals/ohio/franklin)

> "A rise in property value does not automatically mean a proportionally equal rise in taxes, thanks to Ohio's tax reduction factors, however, with higher valuations as the starting point for calculations, an increase in the total tax amount is likely for many."
> — Ohio Property Tax guide, [propertytaxrates.org](https://propertytaxrates.org/blog/ohio-property-tax-guide-2026)

**Project frame:** CRE News covered the triennial review period opening (Sep 5 article). The gap is the *other side* of the deadline: what happens now that the comment period has closed? What do homeowners do next? When do final values get set? When do 2027 tax bills reflect this? That explainer hasn't been published locally with the clarity our audience needs.

### Content gaps (questions to answer)

1. **"How bad is Columbus's housing shortage for renters who aren't middle-income?"**
   The affordable-unit deficit (53,000 units, $7.91/hr wage gap) is being cited repeatedly but no local outlet has turned it into a neighborhood-by-neighborhood explainer.
   → Suggested format: **long-form article** using COHHIO 2026 Gap Report + ZORI rent data + neighborhood income profiles. Would serve renters, policy watchers, and civic investors alike.

2. **"What does Zone In Phase 2 actually change for my neighborhood?"**
   Community forums are asking this specifically. The city's communications are abstract; local media coverage defaults to policy frame, not parcel-level impact.
   → Suggested format: **explainer article with neighborhood table** — what each area commission is saying and what the proposed rezoning means in practice (more density, different use allowances, etc.).

3. **"My triennial property value went up — what do I do now, and when will my tax bill change?"**
   The question of what comes *after* the comment period is not answered clearly anywhere in Columbus coverage. Community frustration is high and the deadline just passed.
   → Suggested format: **timeline explainer** — what happens between now and when the 2027 bill reflects the new value; Board of Revision appeal process; investor implications.

4. **"Is mass timber going to change how Columbus builds?"**
   INTRO Columbus is getting industry attention but the residential/community question — "will this make apartments cheaper or greener?" — hasn't been answered for a general audience.
   → Suggested format: **follow-on explainer or Q&A**, possibly pegged to the ULI tour.

5. **"How do other Columbus neighborhoods compare on rent prices?"**
   The rentcafe.com data (Indianola Terrace at $2,886 vs. Forest Park East at $749) is striking but not contextualized with neighborhood investment/quality-of-life factors. Readers are asking where to rent and where to invest.
   → Suggested format: **neighborhood rent comparison chart article** (connects to our market data tables).
