## Briefs

### 1. Rickenbacker Industrial Center Phase III — 960K SF Spec Industrial Breaks Ground
- **What**: Trident Capital Group and O'Connor Capital Partners closed a joint venture with Clarion Partners and broke ground on Phase III of the Rickenbacker Industrial Center — a 959,579 SF spec industrial building at 1669 Rohr Road on the south side of Columbus. The 57.30-acre project sits within a Qualified Opportunity Zone, carries a 15-year, 75% property tax abatement, and targets Q1 2027 delivery. Building specs: 40-foot clear heights, 104 dock doors, 238 trailer parking stalls, 336 car spaces. JLL (Dan Wendorf, Brian Marsh, Joseph Davis) is handling leasing.
- **Why it matters**: Spec industrial (no signed tenant) at this scale signals institutional confidence in Columbus logistics demand. The Clarion Partners entry brings core-fund capital into the Rickenbacker campus. The 230-acre master-planned complex is now in its third phase, confirming long-term commitment. Opportunity Zone designation adds a tax-incentive layer that broadens the investor pool.

### 2. Let Me Roll It — Vinyl Cocktail Bar Opens in Weinland Park
- **What**: Conor Stratton (co-owner of Ace of Cups) and partner Sarah Fleming opened Let Me Roll It at 132 E. 5th Ave. in Weinland Park. Soft open mid-July, grand open late July 2026. The bar is built around a 1970s aesthetic: vinyl records, cassette tapes, no TVs. Most drinks are under $12. The space (formerly a hookah lounge) is built around conversation. A partnership with Used Kids Records lets customers trade records for drinks or gift cards.
- **Why it matters**: Weinland Park sits between Italian Village and the OSU campus area — a corridor seeing steady commercial reinvestment. An established local operator choosing Weinland Park over Short North is a neighborhood-confidence signal. Below-Short-North pricing and an anti-algorithm concept are intentional counter-programming to the city's higher-rent districts.

### LinkedIn (1,498 chars)

A nearly 1-million-square-foot industrial building just broke ground in Columbus.

Trident Capital Group and O'Connor Capital Partners closed a joint venture with Clarion Partners to start Phase III of the Rickenbacker Industrial Center — 959,579 square feet of spec industrial space at 1669 Rohr Road.

This is significant for a few reasons.

First, it's spec. No anchor tenant is signed. Developers building spec at this scale believe Columbus will fill the space before they finish it. The evidence is on their side: the city's logistics corridor along Rickenbacker has attracted Amazon, Target, Honda logistics, and a long list of national 3PLs over the past decade.

Second, Clarion Partners is a core-fund manager. When core capital enters a project, it means institutional investors — pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, insurance companies — are now underwriting Columbus industrial as a stable, long-term hold. That's a different class of capital than development equity. It's a vote of confidence in the market's durability, not just its growth.

Third, the building specs are modern-big-box ready: 40-foot clear heights, 104 dock doors, 238 trailer stalls. These are numbers that appeal to e-commerce fulfillment, pharmaceutical distribution, and advanced manufacturing.

What to watch: JLL (Dan Wendorf, Brian Marsh, Joseph Davis) is marketing the space. Q1 2027 delivery. The building sits in a Qualified Opportunity Zone with a 15-year, 75% property tax abatement — incentives that improve the economics for prospective tenants and investors alike.
