# Columbus's Olde Towne East Gets 65 Affordable Apartments at Mercy on Main

*Published: September 18, 2026 | Author: CRE News Staff | Category: Development*

*Tags: columbus-ohio, central-ohio-real-estate, olde-towne-east, affordable-housing, development, near-east-side*

---

The Ohio Housing Finance Agency has awarded 2026 Low-Income Housing Tax Credits to Mercy on Main, a 65-unit affordable apartment complex planned for 907–917 E. Main St. in Columbus's Olde Towne East neighborhood. Developer Kingsley + Co. values the project at $22.8 million; no groundbreaking date has been announced.

## About the Project

Mercy on Main would add eight studios, 29 one-bedroom, 18 two-bedroom, and 10 three-bedroom apartments to a currently underused site on East Main Street, [according to NBC4 WCMH-TV's coverage](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/retired-nfl-player-behind-affordable-housing-proposal-in-columbus-olde-towne-east/). The unit mix—weighted toward one-bedroom apartments but with a meaningful share of larger homes for families—reflects a pattern common to urban Low-Income Housing Tax Credit projects in Columbus. Income targeting for individual units was not stated in search-accessible records reviewed by CREN; federal 9% LIHTC projects typically restrict units to households earning at or below 60% of the area median income.

The $22.8 million budget covers the building itself plus parking, access roads, sidewalks, utility connections, and other site improvements, per the NBC4 description. No architect, general contractor, or construction timeline has been publicly announced.

## The Developer and the Name

Kingsley + Co. is a Cincinnati-based affordable housing developer founded by Chinedum Ndukwe, who grew up in central Ohio, played linebacker for the Cincinnati Bengals and the Oakland Raiders after graduating from the University of Notre Dame, and retired from professional football in 2012. He started Kingsley + Co. to develop below-market housing in Ohio cities.

The project takes its name directly from Ndukwe's grandmother. "She was extremely caring and generous and supportive, and a fearless individual," Ndukwe told NBC4. "It was a name I'd been holding onto for a while." [The company's own announcement](https://www.kingsleyandcompany.com/post/kingsley-co-expands-affordable-housing-efforts-into-columbus-with-mercy-on-main) names the project in honor of his grandmother, Mercy Okoronkwo.

Mercy on Main would be Kingsley + Co.'s first announced standalone Columbus project. The company has a separate role in Columbus: it is partnering with Borror Development on a site in the King-Lincoln District, [according to Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/borror-kingsley-selected-to-develop-site-in-king-lincoln-district/).

## Why This Site in Olde Towne East

Olde Towne East occupies a stretch of the Near East Side bounded roughly by the railroad tracks to the north, Bryden Road to the south, and South Champion Avenue to the east. Its Victorian-era residential core has attracted incremental reinvestment since the mid-2010s, but the Main Street corridor has remained a patchwork of older commercial buildings, surface lots, and underbuilt parcels.

Two infrastructure factors likely strengthened the project's OHFA application. The 907–917 E. Main St. site sits on the planned East Main Street Bus Rapid Transit corridor, a transit line the city is advancing to reduce commute times from the Near East Side to Downtown Columbus. The site is also within a few blocks of COTA's existing Near East Transit Center, [per NBC4](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/retired-nfl-player-behind-affordable-housing-proposal-in-columbus-olde-towne-east/), and close to Nationwide Children's Hospital—one of the Near East Side's larger employers.

Early community outreach, according to Ndukwe's NBC4 interview, produced positive initial feedback from the Near East Area Commission and the Olde Towne East Neighborhood Association. Neither body's formal endorsement or vote was confirmed in available records.

## How the Tax Credit Process Works

The 9% Low-Income Housing Tax Credit is the most competitive federal subsidy for affordable rental construction, administered statewide by [the Ohio Housing Finance Agency](https://ohiohome.org/news/releases/2026/9percent-taxcredits.aspx). Ohio receives a limited annual allocation from the federal government, which OHFA distributes through a scored application process that evaluates site suitability, financing structure, developer experience, and other criteria. Projects that win credits sell them to equity investors to raise the upfront capital that makes below-market rents feasible.

OHFA announced $39.1 million in total 2026 9% tax credit allocations across Ohio. Mercy on Main was among the central Ohio recipients; the specific credit amount awarded to this project was not stated in search-accessible records reviewed by CREN. The full award list is available at the OHFA news release page linked above.

A tax credit award is not the same as a construction commitment. After an award, the developer must finalize equity syndication, close construction financing, and satisfy any remaining OHFA conditions—a process that typically takes six to twelve months before a groundbreaking can be scheduled.

## What to Watch

No permits for demolition or new construction at 907–917 E. Main St. were visible in publicly accessible Columbus building permit records as of September 18, 2026—consistent with a project still in the financing phase. The next verifiable signal of progress would be a construction loan closing, a permit filing, or a groundbreaking announcement from Kingsley + Co.

For context, CREN has previously reported on the [Near East Side modular affordable housing](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/developers-2nd-modular-complex-taking-shape-on-near-east-side-bw1/) at Reserve at Maryland, which opened for residents in September 2026—a distinct project type but part of the same neighborhood-wide affordable housing push on Columbus's east side.
