**1. Housing affordability vs. rising inventory — the tension that won't resolve**
Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **mixed / frustrated**
Frame: **content idea + threat**

Columbus is posting its highest July inventory in over a decade (2.4-month supply, up ~7% YoY; closed sales +6.3% YoY to 3,083; median sale price +2.3% to $350K per Columbus REALTORS July report). Yet housing advocates simultaneously report a 53,000-unit affordable housing deficit and note that nearly half of Ohio renters pay more than they can afford. The same week inventory hit a decade high, Columbus CEO/Columbus Partnership published a piece headlined "Columbus' Civic and Business Leaders Aim to Move the Needle on Housing." Community conversation is stuck between two true things: inventory IS rising AND housing IS still unaffordable for a large share of residents.

**2. Zone In Phase 2 — YIMBY momentum vs. neighborhood resistance**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **mixed (pro-density vs. protective)**
Frame: **opportunity + content idea**

Columbus City Council approved Phase 2 of Zone In, covering 43% of city parcels (~66,000 parcels). Phase 2 targets areas near universities, hospitals, and job centers, enabling denser mixed-use development. Neighbors for More Neighbors (N4MN), Columbus's main YIMBY advocacy organization, supports the initiative and is publicly active on Facebook and Substack. The Columbus Free Press published a skeptical counter-perspective ("Be careful what you wish for...") citing concerns that density reform may benefit developers more than residents. WOSU reported a "rift between city leaders and neighborhoods" over the growth plan as recently as December 2025, and that tension continues.

Representative signals (sourced from search results / public articles):
- Columbus Underground (search snippet): Zone In Phase 2 "will cover 43% of city" — next phase focuses on areas "primed for enhanced mixed-use development, industrial and warehouse operations, and business and institutional campuses." - Columbus Free Press headline surfaced in search: *"Be careful what you wish for, as the City and Neighbors 4 More Neighbors push for more urban density"* — represents a distinct skeptical voice in the civic conversation. [https://columbusfreepress.com/article/be-careful-what-you-wish-city-and-neighbors-4-more-neighbors-push-more-urban-density]

**3. Luxury build-out vs. community character (Short North / Franklinton)**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **negative / anxious**
Frame: **threat + content idea**

Parkside on Pearl (Short North) is opening with rents from $2,495 (1BR) to $8,000 (3BR), tying the Peninsula penthouse in Franklinton for the city's highest apartment rent. This is landing in the same news cycle as reports of displacement of Franklinton artists and longtime residents. The phrase "Next Short North" applied to Franklinton energizes developers and worries residents. Multiple distinct community voices (artists, advocacy orgs, long-term residents) are on record expressing concern — not a single-person thread.

Representative signals:
- From search-snippet of Medium post by Massarah Mikati: describes artists and *"mom-and-pop shops"* being pushed out as Franklinton develops, noting *"displacing people that have been here for a very long time and made the place cool before the developers started to come in."* [https://medium.com/@sfmikati/bottoms-up-gentrification-in-franklinton-b9389ad04bcd]

**4. Intel's northeast-side price pressure — four years in, still compounding**
Volume: **LIGHT** | Sentiment: **mixed / concerned**
Frame: **content idea**

Intel's $28B New Albany campus (operational as of 2025) is still driving localized price pressure in northeast Columbus and Licking County. National coverage (Fortune, NBC4, OSU Extension) established the story in 2022-23; community conversation has moved from alarm to a lived reality. Investors are active; workforce housing near the plant is thin. The Columbus market is now being called the "Silicon Heartland" — with observers noting that the tech wealth influx is accelerating a widening of the affordability gap.

Signal: OSU Extension research (Ohioline CDFS-4117) and NBC4 coverage still surfacing in search as the most-cited sources on Intel's housing impact, suggesting no major new reporting in the last 24 hours — the story is baked in but the consequences are ongoing.

**5. Regional Housing Coalition forming — civic action, not yet community conversation**
Volume: **LIGHT** | Sentiment: **cautiously positive**
Frame: **opportunity**

The Columbus Partnership (83-member CEO/civic leader coalition) published its housing-crisis action plan on August 4, 2026. A Regional Housing Coalition is forming with the explicit goal of coordinating housing production at scale. Target: 200,000 new units regionwide over the next decade, with Columbus committing ~half. This is leadership conversation, not yet community conversation — it hasn't broken through to resident-level social channels yet, but it will when specific neighborhood plans land.

Signal: Columbus CEO piece (paywalled; confirmed via search snippet) — Brent Crawford of Crawford Hoying publicly quoted agreeing "the region needs more housing of all types and price points." [https://www.columbusceo.com/story/business/briefs/2026/08/04/columbus-region-leaders-unite-to-tackle-the-local-housing-crisis/91170007007/]
