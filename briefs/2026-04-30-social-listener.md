**4. Affordable Housing Gap: Columbus Is 53,000 Units Short — Advocates Pushing Loudly**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / urgent**
Frame: **Threat + Content idea** (the gap between CRE News's investor audience and the crisis affecting renters is worth naming)

The 2026 COHHIO/NLIHC Gap Report (released March 5, 2026) found Columbus has a 53,000-unit affordable rental deficit. Only 27 affordable units exist for every 100 extremely low-income renters. Nearly half of Ohio's 1.58 million renters are cost-burdened. The median age of a first-time buyer has risen to 40 (from 33 six years ago). MORPC held a housing fair on April 25, 2026 where residents sought help.

> "A $200,000 home is $2,000 a month in mortgage, interest and insurance, and that is almost impossible without some kind of assistance to help families buy that home." — City official quoted in coverage of Columbus down payment assistance programs, nationaltoday.com (April 8, 2026)

> "Columbus lacks 53,000 affordable units, with only 27 affordable units available for every 100 extremely low-income renters." — COHHIO 2026 Gap Report, cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/ (released March 2026; covered by Spectrum News, NBC4, WTRF — multiple distinct outlets)

> "Ohio's 422,000 extremely low-income renters are especially precarious, with 73% of these households spending over half of their income on rent, up from 66% five years ago." — COHHIO 2026 Gap Report (ibid.)

Average Columbus rent: ~$1,341/month (+2.24% YoY per Zumper/Rent.com). 1BR avg ~$1,150; 2BR avg ~$1,370.

**7. STR License Cap Hits Short North & German Village — Hosts Navigating New Rules**
Volume: **LIGHT** | Sentiment: **Mixed / compliance-focused**
Frame: **Content idea** (investor-audience piece: what the new rules actually cost and how to stay compliant)

A mandatory Short-Term Rental Operator License took effect January 1, 2026. Columbus added license caps in Short North and German Village specifically. Requirements: $150 annual fee, $1M liability insurance, biennial safety inspections, 15.5% lodging tax remittance. City documents indicate this is intended to protect housing availability and neighborhood character.

No inflammatory social reaction found in indexed content — discussion appears muted and procedural. This may be because enforcement is new and hosts are in compliance mode rather than protest mode.

Sources: Columbus Department of Building and Zoning (columbus.gov STR FAQ), steadily.com, jakenfinancegroup.com — light but verified via official city documents.
