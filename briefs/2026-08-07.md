### LinkedIn
Columbus needs 20,000 new homes a year. Right now, the region isn't building that.

This week, civic and business leaders in the Columbus Partnership — 83 organizations representing many of the region's most powerful employers — rallied behind the Regional Housing Coalition's goal: 200,000 new homes across central Ohio by 2034.

That number comes from the growth curve. MORPC forecasts 3.1 million people in the Columbus region by 2050. More people need more housing. Without supply, rents and prices keep climbing.

Here is where the market stands today:

→ Homes for sale in central Ohio: ~5,497 (up 19.5% year-over-year)
→ Days on market: 40 days (up 29% from last year)
→ Months of supply: 2.0 (a balanced market needs 4–6)

Inventory is improving. But Columbus is still supply-constrained.

The Regional Housing Coalition is a nonprofit spanning multiple jurisdictions. It can't build houses. But it can push for policy: faster permitting, rezoning, regional coordination with suburbs that have resisted density.

Zone In is moving toward Phase 2 — covering 43% of Columbus's commercial and industrial land. Phase 3, tackling single-family zoning, is set for 2027.

For Columbus operators: this coalition's work will shape your market over the next decade. More supply tends to moderate rent growth. For renters, that's relief. For investors, it's a headwind — or an opportunity, depending on where you're positioned.
