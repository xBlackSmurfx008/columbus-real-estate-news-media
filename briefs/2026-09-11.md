### LinkedIn (1,200–2,200 chars)

Columbus just hit a construction milestone worth paying attention to.

INTRO Columbus — the 13-story mass timber student housing tower going up at High Street and 9th Avenue near Ohio State — topped out on September 8. That's ten weeks ahead of its November target, and exactly one year to the day after crews broke ground in September 2025.

At 158 feet, it's the tallest mass timber student housing building in the United States. It's also the first major mass timber project in Columbus.

Mass timber uses engineered wood — in this case cross-laminated timber (CLT) panels — in place of concrete and steel for the structural frame. Twelve of INTRO Columbus's thirteen floors are timber. The carbon math is significant: DLR Group, the project architect, calculates the wood superstructure will save approximately 1,128 metric tons of CO2 equivalent compared to a conventional frame.

The development stats: 186 units, 493 beds, 242,000 square feet. Developer is Chicago-based Harbor Bay Ventures. Residents are expected for the 2027–28 academic year.

For Columbus operators watching the construction pipeline: mass timber is faster to erect than poured concrete and increasingly competitive on cost. If INTRO Columbus delivers on schedule, it will be a proof point that could accelerate adoption in the city's multifamily and mixed-use pipeline.

ULI Columbus organized a Hard Hat site tour on September 8 — a sign the local development community is watching closely.

Worth watching: Does Harbor Bay announce a second Columbus project? Does a local developer commission a mass timber building next? The OSU-adjacent corridor at High and 9th is setting a benchmark.
