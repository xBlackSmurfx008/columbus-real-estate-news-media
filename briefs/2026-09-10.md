### LinkedIn (for publishing queue — needs human authentication)

Columbus is finishing a five-year, $267 million project under the Near West Side — and most residents have never heard of it.

The Lower Olentangy Tunnel is in its final construction weeks. Granite Construction broke ground in March 2021. The boring machine finished its 17,000-foot run and reached Tuttle Park in June 2025. Now the system is in commissioning, and the City says it should begin accepting water this month.

What it does: the LOT captures combined sewer overflows — the surge of stormwater and raw sewage that shared pipes can't hold during heavy rain — before they reach the Olentangy River. The overflows discharge at seven Near West Side locations: First Avenue, Third Avenue, King Avenue, Indianola Avenue, Frambes Street, Doe Alley, and Hudson Street.

For Harrison West, where homes from the early twentieth century sit above aging combined sewer lines, basement backup from CSO events has been a persistent problem. The tunnel intercepts that surge. What it doesn't do: stop river flooding from upstream precipitation, or fix lateral connection issues on individual parcels.

This is the largest single project in Columbus's Long Term Control Plan — a federally mandated program to bring the city into Clean Water Act compliance. Utility customers have been paying for it in their monthly bills, financed through an Ohio EPA loan.

The practical test comes with the next heavy rain.
