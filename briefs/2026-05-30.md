### 1. Demolition Begins on Livingston Avenue for 170-Unit Mixed-Use Near Nationwide Children's Hospital

- **What**: Crews this week began demolishing five vacant buildings at 475 E. Livingston Ave., clearing a 1.5-acre site between Ninth Street and Washington Avenue for a planned five-story mixed-use development from Northstar Realty LLC and Schiff Capital Group. The project will deliver approximately 170 apartments and 19,000 sq ft of ground-floor retail on a section of Livingston that has been transformed by Nationwide Children's Hospital's ongoing expansion. The project was originally proposed in 2018, rezoned by City Council in 2022, and received Board of Zoning Adjustment sign-off in 2024; demolition marks the first physical construction milestone after an eight-year development journey. Project budget reported at approximately $45M [one source; needs cross-confirmation].
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: The Nationwide Children's Hospital expansion corridor on Livingston Ave has become one of the most active development sub-markets in Columbus — institutional hospital investment anchors demand for adjacent housing and retail. This is the largest mixed-use project to break ground in that corridor in 2026, and the eight-year timeline from proposal to demolition illustrates the real cost of Columbus's pre-Zone In entitlement process.
- **Source(s)**: [Livingston Avenue Buildings Demolished, New Development Planned](https://columbusunderground.com/livingston-avenue-buildings-demolished-new-development-planned-bw1/) (Columbus Underground, canonical), [Five-Story Building Proposed for Livingston Avenue](https://columbusunderground.com/five-story-building-proposed-for-livingston-avenue-bw1/) (Columbus Underground, background), [Developer proposes $45M mixed-use building near Nationwide Children's Hospital](https://www.brickergraydon.com/DevelopOhio/Developer-proposes-45M-mixed-use-building-near-Nationwide-Childrens-Hospital) (Bricker & Graydon DevelopOhio)

### 3. Tommy's Pizza Site on Lane and Neil Targeted for 45-Unit Apartment Building

- **What**: Dublin-based Stavroff Land and Development, Inc. has submitted a zoning application to the City of Columbus for a six-story apartment building at the corner of Lane Avenue and Neil Avenue — the site of the former Tommy's Pizza, a Columbus institution that closed its doors in 2024 after 45 years (Upper Arlington and Dublin locations remain open). The proposed building would have 45 units across the top five floors (9 units/floor, 130 total beds), with the ground floor dedicated to a 22-space parking garage and approximately 3,300 sq ft of leasing and resident amenity space.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: The Lane/Neil corner is in the heart of the University District/Short North-adjacent corridor, where Zone In's new 12-story by-right allowances on major corridors are reshaping development economics. Stavroff's proposal tests the market for purpose-built multifamily on what was a low-density commercial site — and will draw significant neighborhood attention given Tommy's Pizza's iconic status with Ohio State alumni.

### 5. Zone In Phase 3 — Single-Family Residential Rezoning Pushed to 2027 Start

- **What**: The City of Columbus has confirmed that Phase 3 of its Zone In zoning reform initiative — which will tackle single-family residential districts, the most politically sensitive and geographically extensive phase — is now scheduled to begin in 2027. Phase 2 (commercial, mixed-use, and industrial parcels covering 43% of city lots) is currently in the community engagement and code-drafting stage for 2026 passage. Phase 3 will set by-right density, setback, and height rules for Columbus's vast single-family neighborhoods, and is expected to face significant political resistance.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Single-family investors and small multifamily operators in Columbus neighborhoods have been waiting for Phase 3 to understand their future development envelope. The 2027 start pushes that clarity further out — meaning current single-family lot values remain locked in pre-reform assumptions for at least 18 more months.
- **Source(s)**: [Timeline, Strategy Laid Out for Next Phases of Zoning Reform](https://columbusunderground.com/timeline-strategy-laid-out-for-next-phases-of-zoning-reform-bw1/) (Columbus Underground, canonical), [Next Phase of Zoning Reform Will Cover 43% of City](https://columbusunderground.com/next-phase-of-zoning-reform-will-cover-43-of-city-bw1/) (Columbus Underground), [Zone In Columbus](https://zone-in-columbus.hub.arcgis.com/) (City of Columbus official)

### 6. AspireColumbus — CMHA's 82-Unit Downtown Project Approaching Summer Groundbreaking

- **What**: The Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority (CMHA) is preparing for a summer groundbreaking on AspireColumbus, an 82-unit mixed-income apartment building at 360 S. Third St. — the former headquarters of United Way of Central Ohio. The $29M project will include 17 studio, 33 one-bedroom, 23 two-bedroom, and 9 three-bedroom units. Of the 82 apartments: 31 (38%) will be subsidized via CMHA Housing Choice Vouchers at 30% AMI (≈$21,700/yr individual income for Columbus metro), and 12 (15%) at market rate ($1,211–$2,280/month). At least 5% of units will be fully accessible. Demolition of the former United Way HQ was approved by the city and has been completed.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: AspireColumbus is a downtown Columbus mixed-income model — subsidized and market-rate units in a single building, steps from City Hall, from the region's largest public housing authority. The CMHA developer role signals a broadening of public housing beyond traditional project-based sites and into mixed-income infill. Worth tracking for Columbus operators watching how public-sector development activity fills the gap left by stalled private projects.
- **Source(s)**: [AspireCOLUMBUS Is On The Rise](https://cmhanet.com/aspirecolumbus) (CMHA official, canonical), [Affordable housing coming to downtown Columbus](https://cbustoday.6amcity.com/affordable-housing-coming-to-downtown-columbus) (CBUStoday), [CMHA receives demolition approval of former United Way headquarters for housing development](https://www.columbusjewishnews.com/news/local_news/cmha-receives-demolition-approval-of-former-united-way-headquarters-for-housing-development/article_d0b3de94-e7e3-11ef-bd66-43acb7390b01.html) (Columbus Jewish News)
