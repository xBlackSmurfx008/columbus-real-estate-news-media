### Opportunity Pointe Demolition Wraps on Cleveland Ave — Construction Starting This Week
- **What**: Columbus State Community Partners and Woda Cooper Companies demolished two buildings at 145 and 159 Cleveland Avenue this week, clearing the site for Opportunity Pointe — a $54 million, 186-unit affordable housing complex. Construction is expected to begin immediately after demolition wraps, with delivery targeted by end of 2027.

### Edwards Companies Shelves $600M Capitol Square Renaissance
- **What**: Edwards Companies announced in April 2026 that it is shelving its Capitol Square Renaissance Project, citing "current economic and market conditions." ### Opportunity Pointe Adds 186 Units to Downtown Columbus

*Columbus State Community College and Woda Cooper Companies are breaking ground on a $54 million affordable housing complex that will reshape a key block along one of the city's busiest transit corridors.*

---

**Demolition has wrapped this week** at 145 and 159 Cleveland Avenue in downtown Columbus, clearing two older buildings to make way for Opportunity Pointe — a $54 million affordable housing development that will bring 186 apartments to a block directly adjacent to Columbus State Community College's downtown campus.

Site construction is expected to begin immediately, with the full project targeting completion by the end of 2027.

---

## The Project: What's Coming to Cleveland Avenue

Opportunity Pointe is a two-phase development built as a partnership between Columbus State Community Partners — the nonprofit real estate arm of Columbus State Community College — and Woda Cooper Companies, one of central Ohio's most active affordable housing developers.

The 186-unit complex will span the block bordered by Cleveland Avenue, Grant Street, Long Street, and Spring Street. Of the total units, 20 are set aside specifically for Columbus State students. The remaining units will serve households earning between 30% and 80% of Area Median Income (AMI), with rent tiers ranging from $365 per month for a one-bedroom at the lowest AMI threshold to $1,295 per month for a three-bedroom at the higher end.

Unit mix across phases will include one-, two-, and three-bedroom apartments. Communal amenities have not been fully disclosed, but the project follows Woda Cooper's typical portfolio standard of on-site property management and resident services.

---

## How the Financing Came Together

The project's financing backbone is a dual award of 9% Low Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTCs) from the Ohio Housing Finance Agency (OHFA) — one for each phase of the development. The 9% credit is the most competitive and most valuable pool in Ohio's annual LIHTC cycle, awarded through a competitive scoring process. Securing it for two phases in back-to-back award cycles is an uncommon achievement that reflects both the strength of the application and the project's policy alignment with OHFA's statewide priorities.

Under the LIHTC structure, private equity investors purchase the tax credits upfront, providing capital that covers the majority of construction cost without relying solely on public appropriations. The OHFA credit awards were announced in May 2025, followed by a financing close and the construction start now underway.

"The ability to advance affordable housing for our students and community members in the heart of downtown Columbus is transformational," said a Columbus State spokesperson at the time of the 2025 announcement. ## Why Cleveland Avenue, Why Now

The site sits at the geographic center of Columbus State's downtown footprint — a location that gives future residents walkable access to the college's academic buildings, library, and campus services. But the choice of Cleveland Avenue is also strategic beyond the campus connection.

Cleveland Avenue is one of Columbus's most heavily ridden COTA bus routes, running north from downtown through Linden, Northland, and beyond. The corridor has been a focus of city investment in recent years, and transit-oriented affordable housing along it addresses a persistent gap: workers who rely on COTA for commutes have had limited access to affordable units on the route.

The neighborhood has also seen recent market-rate development pressure. New projects and renovations along the Short North–to–downtown stretch have pushed rents upward. Opportunity Pointe's income-tiered structure will provide a degree of affordability stabilization in a corridor that would otherwise be fully exposed to that market pressure.

## What Columbus Operators Should Know

**The college-anchor model is scaling.** Columbus State Community Partners is building a replicable playbook for community colleges using their real estate capacity to address workforce housing shortfalls. Columbus State has signaled plans to expand this model, which could generate additional development opportunity for mission-aligned developers in the region.

**LIHTC capital is active in downtown.** The dual 9% award for Opportunity Pointe demonstrates that OHFA is willing to allocate its most competitive credits to high-cost urban sites — a signal that affordable development in downtown Columbus is financeable even as market-rate projects face headwinds.

**Near-term supply addition where it counts.** With demolition completing this week and a 2027 delivery target, Opportunity Pointe is real supply in a neighborhood with real demand. For Columbus property managers and investors tracking the downtown rental market, 186 income-restricted units entering the pipeline over the next 18 months is a meaningful data point.

**Displacement questions remain open.** The demolition of two occupied buildings along Cleveland Avenue is typical in urban affordable redevelopment but raises standard questions about tenant relocation. Columbus Underground has reported the project proceeded through demolition, but public documentation of tenant relocation assistance is not confirmed in available sources. [needs source]
