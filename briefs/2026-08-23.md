**Coverage signal:** Cover today (Aug. 23) — real estate slot
**Confidence:** 4/5
**What:** Mayor Andrew Ginther announced on August 20, 2026 that the City of Columbus has released the formal Phase 2 Zone In draft zoning proposal. The proposal introduces nine new zoning districts covering roughly 40 percent of the city's land — targeting three categories left untouched by Phase 1: mixed-use corridors not in Phase 1, industrial and warehouse districts, and business and institutional campuses (hospitals, universities, major employment centers including Tuttle Mall and Polaris). Specific corridors newly in scope: Summit Street and Fourth Street north of Downtown; Whittier Street and Thurman Avenue on the South Side. The Northland/SR-161 corridor (160 parcels, "community activity centers") was addressed in a prior Council action and is now part of the Phase 2 framework. A 60-day public comment period opens August 25 and runs through October 24. Phase 2 is projected to enable ~13,000 new homes over 10 years in the newly covered areas; the full Zone In initiative (all three phases) has capacity for up to 88,000 new units city-wide. Phase 3 will address single-family residential neighborhoods.
**Why it matters:** Zone In Phase 2 rewrites the rules for roughly 40% of Columbus parcels, including close-in industrial land and underutilized commercial corridors. For investors and operators, it could lower the approval cost for certain building types and change what neighbors can build by right. Public comment opens Tuesday — operators and property owners have 48 hours to prepare before the comment window opens.

Columbus just released the formal Zone In Phase 2 zoning draft — and the 60-day public comment period opens this Tuesday, August 25.

Phase 2 covers roughly 40 percent of the city, targeting three categories that Phase 1 (passed by City Council in summer 2024) left untouched:

• Mixed-use corridors outside the Phase 1 boundary
• Industrial and warehouse districts

What this means for operators and property owners: Zone In doesn't approve projects — it changes baseline entitlements. If your land falls in a newly covered area, the new districts could lower the approval cost and time for certain building types, or change what a neighboring owner can legally build next to you.

City projections: Phase 2 could enable ~13,000 new homes over 10 years in the affected areas. The full Zone In initiative (all three phases) has been described as capable of enabling up to 88,000 new units city-wide.

Phase 3 — the final stage — will address single-family residential neighborhoods. No timeline has been set.

## 🚨 SITE IS DOWN — root cause found (post-run investigation)

**Symptom:** columbusrealestatenews.com returns HTTP 200 but every public page renders zero articles. `/blog` shows the empty-state message "Articles are being loaded. Check back shortly." All 79 live articles are invisible to the public. Individual article URLs 404.

**Root cause:** the NeonDB project has **exceeded its data transfer quota**. Every query now returns `HTTP 402: "Your project has exceeded the data transfer quota. Upgrade your plan to increase limits."` Confirmed persistent across repeated attempts, not transient. The Next.js site queries Neon at request time, so with the DB refusing all queries the pages fall back to their empty state.

Note this quota was NOT exhausted at the start of this session — `recent-articles.mjs`, `publish-batch.mjs` and `refresh-market-data.mjs` all ran successfully earlier in the run. It tipped over partway through.

**This requires owner action to restore service:** raise the Neon plan (or wait for the quota window to reset). No code change can bring the site back while the database refuses queries.

### What was burning the quota (fixed in this commit)

`lib/public-data.ts` was transferring every live article's full markdown body far more often than needed:

1. **`getArticleBySlug()` — the dominant cost.** Public URLs are `/blog/<slug>`, but article ids carry a `YYYY-MM-DD-` prefix. So the "fast path" `WHERE id = ${slug}` **never matched on a normal request**, and every single article page render fell through to `SELECT * FROM articles WHERE status='live'` — pulling *all* live articles including full bodies, just to find one. With ~79 article pages each revalidating on a 300s ISR timer, that is on the order of gigabytes per day of pure waste. Now resolves the slug against `id, title` only, then fetches the one matching row.

2. **List/card queries.** `getPublicData()` and `getArticles()` both used `SELECT *`, pulling every article body for the homepage, blog index, topic pages, area pages, and related-article cards — none of which render the body. Verified by grep that `article.body` is read in exactly one place (`app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx:237`), which still gets it via `getArticleById`/`getArticleBySlug`. Both list queries now select explicit columns and omit `body`.

Verification: `tsc --noEmit` clean; 29/29 unit tests pass.

**Important:** this fix does not restore the site on its own — it prevents the replacement quota from being burned down the same way once the Neon plan is raised.
