### LinkedIn (1,200–2,200 chars)

A Columbus startup is building affordable apartments in a factory — and its second Near East Side complex opens this week.

Connect Housing Blocks, a subsidiary of Connect Real Estate, manufactures apartment units at its 630,000-square-foot plant in Linden on Westerville Road. Steel-framed modules leave fully finished — drywall, plumbing, wiring, fixtures, the works — and get trucked to the site, where a crane stacks them into floors. The company says the approach cuts construction time and cost by roughly a third versus traditional methods.

Their second project, Reserve at Maryland at 1728 Maryland Ave., opens Building 2 on September 16. All 84 units across the four-building complex are reserved for households earning up to 60 percent of the area median income, with rents from $1,078 to $1,464 per month. Building 1 is already fully leased. Buildings 3 and 4 open October 16.

This matters for Columbus's affordable housing pipeline for a few reasons:

- The Linden factory employs up to 165 people — it's not just producing units, it's producing jobs in a neighborhood that needs them.
- The 60% AMI band covers working households across healthcare, logistics, and education — exactly the people being priced out of the neighborhoods they work in.
- If the Reserve at Maryland leases up at this pace, it validates factory-built production as more than a pilot.

The company's first project, The Station at Trolley District, opened in 2025. The Reserve at Maryland is the second. What's the third?
