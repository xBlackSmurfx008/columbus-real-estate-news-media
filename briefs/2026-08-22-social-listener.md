**1. $600M Capitol Square Renaissance Project Shelved by Edwards Companies** — Loud, Mixed/Negative
*Frame: Threat (to downtown revitalization narrative) + content opportunity (investor confidence story)*

Edwards Companies has officially shelved the Capitol Square Renaissance Project, a roughly $600 million overhaul spanning about 10 acres south of East Broad Street in downtown Columbus. The plan would have replaced surface parking lots and underused buildings with 1,000+ residential units, office space, restaurants, and public green space. Edwards cited "current economic and market conditions" after a "thorough evaluation."

This is circulating in commercial real estate and investment circles with mixed reaction: some view it as a market-cycle setback, others see it as a referendum on downtown Columbus fundamentals. The project had already received $33 million in state tax credits through Ohio's Transformational Mixed-Use Development Program — a significant public commitment that is now in question. Edwards Companies has completed portions of the broader vision (Preston Centre redevelopment, The Pembroke apartments) and said it will continue work on Gay/High streets and Capital Line projects.

**Verbatim (Jeff Edwards, CEO, Edwards Companies, via NBC4/ConstructConnect):**
> "While we remain confident in the long-term trajectory of downtown Columbus, today's market conditions indicate that now is not the right time for this project."

**Verbatim (ConstructConnect, trade press):**
> "Edwards Companies is shelving a $600 million Capitol Square Renaissance Project in downtown Columbus, Ohio, citing unfavorable economic and market conditions."

**Volume signal:** Loud — covered by NBC4, AOL, ConstructConnect, Columbus Underground; referenced in commercial RE trade press. Multiple outlets and commenters.

**CRE News angle:** A $600M project with $33M in state credits going on hold is the biggest downtown Columbus RE story of the summer. The tension between the simultaneous successes (Merchant Building opening, Bridge Park Ellis condos under construction) and this failure reveals fault lines in the downtown investment thesis. A piece explaining what the cancellation means for the E. Broad St. corridor would be shareable and useful for investors.

**3. Franklinton Flooding + Investment Confidence Question** — Steady, Concerned/Negative
*Frame: Threat (to buyer/investor confidence in Franklinton) + content opportunity*

The August 19–20 flooding event — 4 to 7 inches of rain in Franklin and Licking counties, the Franklinton floodwall activated, five water rescues, major road closures — is generating sustained social conversation about Franklinton's flood risk. The conversation is intersecting with the neighborhood's active development boom: East Franklinton has a $250M Phase I redevelopment underway, a local developer is building 50 new homes, and an 11-story building has been proposed for a small lot.

First Street Foundation data surfaced in multiple search results: **41% of South Franklinton properties face major flood risk over the next 30 years**, and the neighborhood carries a FEMA Zone AE designation despite the floodwall. The question circulating in real estate forums and buyer searches: "Does the floodwall actually protect me, and what does this mean for my property value?"

**Verbatim (WOSU Public Media, Aug 20, 2026):**
> "Franklinton floodwall activated after recent floods in Columbus" — city crews installed floodgates on Greenlawn and Harmon avenues as heavy rain caused water levels to rise.

**Verbatim (Spectrum News 1, Aug 20, 2026):**
> "Heavy rain floods Columbus roads, prompts rescues" — NWS reported 4 to 7 inches in heaviest-hit parts of Franklin and Licking counties.

**Verbatim (First Street Foundation / Franklinton flood risk profile, via search):**
> "South Franklinton has a major risk of flooding, with 41% of all properties at risk of severe flooding over the next 30 years."

**Volume signal:** Steady — NBC4, ABC6, 10TV, WOSU, Spectrum News 1, Columbus Navigator, ABC6 all covered the Aug 19–20 flooding. Multiple distinct organizations and distinct audience segments (buyers, investors, current residents) engaging.

**CRE News angle:** A data piece combining the floodwall's protection limits, FEMA zone designations, First Street projections, and the current development wave would be high-value and genuinely protective journalism. Buyers need to understand the distinction between "floodwall is working" (which it is) and "no flood risk" (which isn't accurate).
