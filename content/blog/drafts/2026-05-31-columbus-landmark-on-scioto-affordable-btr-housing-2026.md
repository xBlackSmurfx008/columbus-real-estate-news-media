In April 2026, a 44.5-acre site in Columbus received its first shovels.

The project is Landmark on Scioto — 321 purpose-built affordable homes from Lincoln Avenue Communities (LAC), a national affordable housing developer making its first ground-up construction investment in Ohio. What breaks ground here is not a standard apartment complex. It is something Columbus has not had before at this scale: a build-to-rent (BTR) community designed from the ground up for families earning up to 60 percent of the area median income.

U.S. Representative Mike Carey (OH-15), Ohio Housing Finance Agency Executive Director Bill Beagle, and OCCH President and CEO Catherine Cawthon attended the ceremony. That lineup — a federal legislator, the state's affordable housing finance chief, and the CEO of Ohio's largest affordable housing intermediary — reflects how much institutional weight this project carries.

## What Build-to-Rent Affordable Housing Is — and Why Columbus Doesn't Have It

The phrase "build-to-rent" has been in the Columbus real estate vocabulary for several years. Several Columbus-area developers and national operators have built or acquired scattered-site rental homes across central Ohio's suburbs. The BTR market in central Ohio is real.

But affordable BTR — purpose-built for income-restricted households — is a different product, and Columbus has essentially none of it.

A standard LIHTC (Low-Income Housing Tax Credit) apartment development in Columbus looks like this: a four- to seven-story building, elevator access, 80 to 200 stacked units, a parking garage, and shared amenity floors. It is an apartment complex with income restrictions. [Internal link: Columbus affordable housing pipeline — Opportunity Pointe, Kingsley East Main, AspireColumbus]

Landmark on Scioto looks like this: one- and two-story homes, arranged like a neighborhood, with 2-, 3-, and 4-bedroom floor plans, private space between units, and shared community areas. Amenities include a fitness center, pool, clubhouse, and outdoor grilling and gathering areas. Think of a new-construction rental subdivision — built affordable.

For Columbus families earning between 30 and 60 percent of AMI, the standard affordable apartment product does not always fit. A family of five needs bedrooms, not efficiency studios. They want space, some separation between units, and a place for kids to play. Homeownership at Columbus's April 2026 median of $346,500 is not reachable at 60% AMI. [Internal link: Columbus April 2026 Housing Market] Affordable BTR fills that gap — if you build it.

Lincoln Avenue Communities is building it.

## 321 Homes. 44.5 Acres. What the Scale Means

The scale of Landmark on Scioto is worth pausing on.

321 homes on 44.5 acres yields a density of roughly 7.2 units per acre — low by urban infill standards, but consistent with what you need to deliver one- and two-story BTR homes with yards, setbacks, and community spaces. By comparison, Columbus's highest-density affordable housing projects — Opportunity Pointe (166 units across two five-story buildings), Kingsley East Main (65 units, five stories), AspireColumbus (82 units, five stories) — run 30 to 40 units per acre. Landmark on Scioto is an entirely different land-use model.

The 44.5-acre footprint also puts Landmark on Scioto among the largest single affordable housing land commitments in Columbus in recent years [needs source: specific address and neighborhood for geographic context].

Approximately 7 acres of the site will be dedicated to the City of Columbus for a public park — a land donation that signals LAC is building a neighborhood, not just a housing development. In a city where park access is unequally distributed across income levels, a 7-acre public park adjacent to 321 affordable homes is a meaningful community investment that outlasts any single lease cycle.

## Lincoln Avenue Communities Enters Ohio

Lincoln Avenue Communities is a national affordable housing developer with a portfolio concentrated in tax-credit housing across the eastern and midwestern United States. Landmark on Scioto is LAC's first ground-up construction project in Ohio — a market entry that reflects deliberate underwriting, not opportunism.

What makes a market viable for a developer like LAC? The factors they underwrite include: documented income-qualified demand, strong LIHTC allocation competition (Ohio's 9% award cycle has been oversubscribed for years), supportive local and state government, and population growth that drives ongoing housing need.

Columbus checks every box. The Central Ohio metro grew by 30,348 residents between 2023 and 2024, bringing the region to 2.2 million — the second-largest metro in Ohio and one of the fastest-growing in the Midwest. Columbus permitted only 10,474 new homes in 2024 against an estimated 19,000 needed annually. The supply gap at every income level is documented, and OHFA's competitive LIHTC allocation makes state financing available to developers who can execute at scale.

LAC's entry into Ohio through Landmark on Scioto is the market signal that Columbus's affordable housing sector has graduated from local operators and regional nonprofits to national institutional capital — a transition that has implications for every Columbus developer working in the affordable space.

## Who the Homes Are For: The 60% AMI Calculation

Landmark on Scioto targets residents earning up to 60 percent of area median income.

For the Columbus, Ohio metro area in 2026, 60% AMI is approximately:

- 1-person household: ~$37,260/year
- 2-person household: ~$42,600/year
- 3-person household: ~$47,940/year
- 4-person household: ~$53,280/year

These are households that earn too much for the deepest rental subsidies — the 30% AMI units that serve incomes around $20,000 annually — but far too little to compete in Columbus's ownership market or its rapidly rising rental market. A 3-bedroom market-rate apartment in Columbus was running $1,800–$2,200/month in early 2026 [needs source: current 3BR rental average]. A 60% AMI 3-bedroom unit would rent at approximately $1,100–$1,200/month.

The 2-, 3-, and 4-bedroom unit mix at Landmark on Scioto is not accidental. These are family-sized homes designed for the households that Columbus's standard affordable apartment portfolio has consistently underdelivered. LIHTC towers built for efficiency rarely offer 4-bedroom units; Landmark on Scioto leads with them.

## The Public Land Component: 7 Acres for Columbus

LAC's commitment to dedicate approximately 7 of the project's 44.5 acres to the City of Columbus for a public park is one of the most operationally significant elements of the Landmark on Scioto deal.

Public park dedication in affordable housing development is unusual at this scale. Most affordable developers — working under tight LIHTC yield constraints — maximize every acre for units. The fact that LAC is dedicating 16 percent of their site to public green space reflects either a strong land basis (the site was available at a price that accommodated the dedication) or a strategic community engagement decision (or both).

The park creates a permanent public amenity for the surrounding area, regardless of what happens to the residential component. For Columbus's affordable housing advocates and neighborhood organizations, a 7-acre park is a legacy investment in the community's long-term quality of life.

## The Columbus Affordable Housing Pipeline in May 2026

Landmark on Scioto does not exist in isolation. Columbus's affordable housing pipeline is more active in 2026 than at any point in recent history:

- **Opportunity Pointe** — Near East Side, 166 units (two five-story buildings), Columbus State Community Partners + Woda Cooper Companies, demolition underway, completion targeted by end of 2027 [Internal link: Opportunity Pointe coverage]
- **AspireColumbus** — Downtown, 82 units, Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority, summer 2026 groundbreaking pending
- **Kingsley East Main St.** — Near East Side, 65 units, Kingsley and Company, 9% LIHTC award from OHFA in May 2026
- **Whitechapel Development** — Franklinton, 103 units supportive housing, Arch City Development + Bianchi, spring 2026 start targeted
- **Landmark on Scioto** — Columbus, 321 units BTR affordable, Lincoln Avenue Communities, April 2026 groundbreaking

Taken together, these five projects represent approximately 737 new affordable or income-restricted units entering the Columbus pipeline — one of the largest single-year cohorts in recent memory.

It is still not enough. Columbus needs an estimated 19,000 new homes annually; these 737 units address the income levels the private market consistently undersupplies, but they do not close the gap. What they represent is an acceleration in institutional commitment to the problem — from local nonprofits to national platforms, from individual LIHTC towers to 44.5-acre BTR communities.

## What to Watch

Landmark on Scioto's summer 2026 leasing launch is the next news hook. When the community opens and families move in, the story moves from groundbreaking to realized product. Columbus Real Estate News will cover the leasing launch and — if and when it is scheduled — the public park dedication.

Until then, Landmark on Scioto represents a threshold Columbus crossed in April 2026: a national institutional developer committing 44.5 acres and 321 affordable homes to the city's housing supply, using a product model that the Columbus market had so far left to other cities.

Columbus is now one of those cities.
