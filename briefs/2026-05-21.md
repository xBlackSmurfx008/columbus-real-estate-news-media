## 419 Apartments Would Replace Ohio State Library in Columbus

The State Library of Ohio locked its Italian Village doors on April 15 and may never reopen them.

The library's closure at 274 E. First Ave. wasn't a routine relocation — it was cleared specifically to make way for a large-scale apartment proposal from Cincinnati-based developer Merus. Working with local architecture firm BBCO, Merus has submitted plans to tear down the 122,000-square-foot commercial building that has housed the State Library and the Ohioana Library and replace it with **419 apartments** spread across five buildings on the nearly 7-acre site.

For anyone tracking infill development in Columbus's Italian Village, the proposal marks a significant potential shift: the largest single residential development proposal the neighborhood has seen in years, on a site that many longtime Columbus observers had assumed would remain institutional indefinitely.

---

## What Merus Is Proposing

The application, submitted to the City of Columbus by Merus and BBCO, calls for five apartment buildings of varying heights — three at three stories and two at four stories — arranged across the site, which is bounded roughly by East First Avenue, East Second Avenue, and Sixth Street. A 3.5-level structured parking garage and surface parking lots are also included in the plan.

That's 419 units across buildings that top out at four stories, meaning Merus is betting on spread-out density rather than a single tall tower. For Italian Village, a neighborhood defined by low-rise Victorian and Craftsman housing stock, that massing approach may be strategically intentional — but it will still face scrutiny.

The current site is zoned for manufacturing, which reflects its industrial heritage. Merus is seeking a rezone to mixed-use, arguing in its application that the change would align with the city's stated goals of "promoting density, walkability, and a diverse building typology within a predominantly residential program."

Merus has described the plans as being in an early, conceptual stage. No construction timeline has been announced. [Project cost and delivery timeline need source]

---

## The Library Leaves — And What It's Carrying With It

The State Library of Ohio is one of the state's oldest public institutions, serving researchers, legislators, and libraries across Ohio. Its Italian Village location has been its longtime home for physical collections and public-facing operations.

Under the relocation plan, library staff offices will move to 35 E. Chestnut St. in Downtown Columbus. The library's collections — including the Ohioana Library's materials on Ohio authors and literature — will transfer to a specialized archival facility in Gahanna. The library's current lease on the Italian Village site runs through June 30, 2027, which means Merus will not be able to move forward with demolition until at least that point unless the lease is terminated early.

The closure has been described as indefinite. Whether the State Library returns to a public-facing facility — or whether the Gahanna archival model becomes permanent — appears to remain an open question. [Library's long-term service model post-relocation needs source]

For Italian Village neighbors and residents who have used the library, the closure is a concrete loss in the near term. For the developer, the library's move-out is the prerequisite that starts the clock.

---

## Why Italian Village Is a Contested Battleground

Italian Village is among the most investment-active neighborhoods in Columbus right now. Bounded by Short North to the south, Interstate 670 to the north, and the Olentangy River corridor to the west, the neighborhood has seen steady residential and mixed-use investment over the past decade, with rising median rents and significant new construction along North Fourth Street and East Fifth Avenue.

The 274 E. First Ave. site sits on the eastern edge of that activity, close to the boundary where Italian Village's older industrial and warehouse buildings meet its Victorian-era residential blocks. The "mixed-use" rezoning request at this specific location is not unusual — but the scale of the ask (419 units, five buildings, structured parking) will need to hold up in front of two separate review bodies.

Because the site sits within the **Italian Village Historic District**, the proposal must receive approval from the **Italian Village Commission** before demolition can proceed. The commission held an initial conceptual feedback session for this project on March 10, 2026. [Outcome of March 10 Italian Village Commission meeting needs source — whether the commission was supportive, skeptical, or requested revisions has not been confirmed.]

After the Italian Village Commission, the application must clear the **Development Commission** for the rezoning itself. The Development Commission's May 14, 2026 agenda included at least one urban center mixed-use application, though it has not been publicly confirmed whether the Merus proposal appeared on that specific agenda. [Confirm whether 274 E. First Ave. was on the May 14 DC agenda — needs source]

Even if both commissions sign off, the project will still require standard building permits and plan approvals before construction begins. This is a multi-year process at minimum.

## What Columbus Operators and Investors Should Watch

**Supply signal for Italian Village:** 419 units is a meaningful pipeline addition for a submarket that has absorbed new supply steadily but rarely sees proposals of this scale. If the project moves through approvals, it would rank among the larger multifamily deliveries in the neighborhood's history. Investors tracking rent trends and occupancy rates in Italian Village should log this as a five-to-seven-year forward supply signal.

**The affordability gap:** The Merus application, as submitted, does not appear to include an affordable housing component. [Needs source — confirm whether any affordable set-aside is included in the proposal.] In a neighborhood with increasing displacement pressure, the absence of an affordable component will likely be a point of contention during community review.

**Historic preservation as leverage:** The Italian Village Historic District designation gives the commission broad authority to require modifications — massing, materials, setbacks, design language — that could affect the project's financial model. Developers who have successfully navigated Columbus historic district reviews have generally come to the Italian Village Commission with design flexibility and a willingness to engage. Whether Merus, as a Cincinnati-based operator entering the Columbus market, has built those local relationships remains to be seen.

**Who is Merus?** Merus is a Cincinnati-based residential development firm. [Additional background on Merus's project history and scale needs source — this is their first known project submission in Columbus.] Their use of BBCO, a local Columbus firm, as architect suggests an intentional approach to local market credibility.

## The Broader Context: Columbus's Infill Moment

This proposal arrives at a moment when downtown Columbus's large-scale development ambitions have taken a public hit. In April, Edwards Companies shelved its $600 million Capitol Square Renaissance project — a 10-block, 1,000-plus-unit mixed-use development — citing market conditions, rising construction costs tied to tariffs, and what at least one local real estate expert described as a downtown market that "needs to absorb what's already been built."

*[Internal link: "[Edwards Companies Shelves $600M Capitol Square Renaissance](../2026-05-03-edwards-companies-capitol-square-renaissance-shelved.md)" — May 3, 2026]*

Italian Village, a step removed from the downtown core, may not face the same absorption dynamics that are stalling downtown towers. The neighborhood's walk-to-Short North positioning, its existing tight rental market, and its strong demand from young professionals and OSU-adjacent renters all suggest a different risk profile than large downtown projects. But the timing is a reminder that no Columbus project is insulated from broader market conditions.

*[Internal link: "[Columbus April 2026 Housing Market Report](../2026-05-16-columbus-april-2026-housing-market-median-price.md)" — May 16, 2026]*

## What Happens Next

The next formal milestone for the Merus proposal is continued review by the Italian Village Commission, where detailed design plans will need to address the historic district's standards. Separately, the Development Commission rezoning process must run its course — including public notice, community input periods, and commission deliberation.

If both approvals are granted, Merus would then move into detailed permitting and design development. Given the library's lease running through mid-2027, the earliest realistic construction start would likely be late 2027 at the earliest.

CRE News will continue to track commission hearings, community feedback, and any design revisions as this project moves through Columbus's development review process.
