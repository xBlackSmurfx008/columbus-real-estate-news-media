### 1. COTA Signals West Broad BRT Construction Delay — Up to 16 Months

- **What**: COTA confirmed on May 27, 2026 that the West Broad Street BRT construction timeline is "fluid," with a potential delay of up to 16 months from the originally projected 2026 start. A one-mile section in Franklinton (Princeton Ave to May Ave) may require redesign after ODOT issued doubled traffic volume projections in May 2025. The West Broad corridor design was reportedly approaching 90% completion as of April 2026, but federal coordination with the FTA remains unresolved. COTA says the dedicated bus-only guideway lanes planned for Franklinton will not be removed.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Franklinton has attracted significant development capital premised on BRT corridor proximity and transit-oriented development; a 16-month construction slip pushes service to 2028–2029 at the earliest, directly affecting hold periods and exit assumptions for operators who baked transit access into their underwriting.
- **Source(s)**: [WOSU — May 27, 2026](https://www.wosu.org/news/2026-05-27/cota-says-timeline-for-west-broad-street-bus-rapid-transit-line-construction-remains-fluid) (canonical), [WOSU — April 23, 2026](https://www.wosu.org/politics-government/2026-04-23/west-broad-street-transit-corridor-could-see-16-month-delay-in-projected-timeline), [Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/cota-linkus-construction-delay-changes-to-west-broad-design-possible-bw1/), [LinkUS Columbus official](https://linkuscolumbus.com/westbroad/), [COTA TOD Study](https://www.cota.com/blog/cota-and-city-of-columbus-continue-public-meetings-this-spring-for-west-broad-transit-oriented-development-study/)

### 2. Nationwide Realty Investors Converts Downtown Tower to 148 Apartments — "The Centennial on High"

- **What**: Nationwide Realty Investors received Downtown Commission approval on February 24, 2026 to convert the 18-story Two Nationwide Plaza (280 N. High St.) into a mixed-use building renamed The Centennial on High, celebrating Nationwide's 100th year in business. The redevelopment creates 148 upscale residential units across 15 floors — including a new penthouse level — with units ranging from one to three bedrooms and each floor featuring five apartments with balconies and a sunken outdoor courtyard pool. Approximately 75,000 sq ft of office space is preserved; anchor tenants include Carpenter Lipps LLP and Ohio Farm Bureau. First residents expected mid-2027.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Two Nationwide Plaza is the largest confirmed office-to-residential adaptive reuse project in downtown Columbus this cycle; it sets a template for other underutilized CBD towers, and the fact that the developer is converting its own former corporate HQ sends a strong downtown residential confidence signal.
- **Source(s)**: [NRI official](https://www.nationwiderealtyinvestors.com/apartments/new-name-timeline-for-downtown-tower-conversion/) (canonical), [REBusinessOnline](https://rebusinessonline.com/nationwide-realty-investors-to-redevelop-18-story-office-tower-in-columbus-into-mixed-use-destination/), [NBC4](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/nationwides-old-headquarters-to-become-next-columbus-apartment-building/), [Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/downtown-commission-approves-plan-for-office-to-residential-conversion-bw1/), [Yield Pro](https://yieldpro.com/2026/03/nri-unveils-renovation-to-280-high-in-columbus/)

### 3. The Pointe at Polaris: $60.2M Luxury Apartment Sale + DFW LAND Buys Commercial Component

- **What**: Two transactions closed April 29, 2026 at The Pointe at Polaris. The two luxury residential buildings at 8890 and 8922 Lyra Drive — together comprising 473 units — sold for $60.2M (~$127,273/unit) to entities named Polaris Lyra Holdings LLC and Polaris Exchange LLC. Simultaneously, Dallas-based DFW LAND acquired the commercial component — 212,366 sq ft of Class A office and 33,071 sq ft of retail (both fully leased) — from a VanTrust Real Estate / NP Limited Partnership joint venture. Commercial sale price was not disclosed. Newmark Capital Markets arranged both the sale and acquisition financing for the commercial buy.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: DFW LAND CEO Vijay Borra explicitly called this deal "entry into the Columbus market" — an out-of-market, Texas-based buyer making a trophy-asset bet on fully leased suburban Columbus office signals renewed investor appetite for the Polaris submarket at a time when office sentiment nationally remains depressed.

### 4. Lincoln Avenue Communities Breaks Ground on 321-Unit Affordable Development — Landmark on Scioto

- **What**: Lincoln Avenue Communities broke ground April 8, 2026 on Landmark on Scioto, a 321-unit affordable housing development on a 44.5-acre Columbus site. The project consists of 257 low-rise buildings with two-, three-, and four-bedroom units, all serving households at or below 60% AMI with LIHTC financing. Leasing begins summer 2026; full completion expected 2028. LAC is dedicating roughly 7 acres to the City of Columbus for a public park. U.S. Rep. Mike Carey (OH-15), OHFA Executive Director Bill Beagle, and OCCH CEO Catherine Cawthon attended the groundbreaking.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: At 321 units on 44.5 acres, Landmark on Scioto is among the larger affordable family-housing projects to break ground in Columbus this year; the family-sized unit mix (2–4 BR), 60% AMI cap, and community park dedication mark a replicable model for operators watching affordable housing pipeline.
- **Source(s)**: [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lincoln-avenue-communities-breaks-ground-on-new-affordable-housing-development-in-columbus-ohio-302737402.html) (canonical), [WhatNow Columbus](https://whatnow.com/columbus/real-estate/321-unit-affordable-housing-project-breaks-ground-in-columbus/), [Yield Pro](https://yieldpro.com/2026/04/scioto/)

### 5. Columbus BZA Approves Factory-to-Apartment Variance (110 Units) — May 26, 2026

- **What**: The Columbus Board of Zoning Adjustment approved a variance on May 26, 2026 to convert a former factory into a 110-unit apartment complex. The approval reduced the minimum front facade zone from 60% to 30% and allowed parking space widths reduced from 9 to 8 feet.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Columbus is showing willingness to flex design standards for adaptive reuse conversions, creating a regulatory signal for developers eyeing older industrial stock across Franklinton, Milo-Grogan, and other neighborhoods with pre-war manufacturing buildings.
