### 1. Ohio's $120M Brownfield Remediation Round Opens TODAY for Columbus Applicants

- **What**: The Ohio Department of Development opened its latest $120 million Brownfield Remediation Program application round today, August 11, 2026, at 10 a.m. Applications are accepted through September 11 at 5 p.m. The program funds site assessment and full cleanup of contaminated industrial, commercial, and institutional properties. No local match required. Governor DeWine announced the round on August 6. Since 2021, the program has awarded nearly $800 million to 904 projects in 87 of Ohio's 88 counties.
- **Columbus angle**: COCIC (Central Ohio Community Improvement Corp.) is Franklin County's lead entity for applications. Franklin County had a 6-for-6 success rate in 2024, winning nearly $29M (per Zimmer Development Co.). Prior grants funded Eastland Mall cleanup, Gahanna Creekside District, Weinland Park, and the former downtown Columbus YMCA building (Lofts at 40 Long — $1M to Woda Cooper). Eligible applicants include for-profit organizations, not only public entities.
- **Apply**: development.ohio.gov → For Community → Redevelopment → Brownfield Remediation Program. Franklin County coordination: cocic.org/grants.

### LinkedIn (1,312 chars)

Ohio just opened a $120 million brownfield cleanup grant round. Applications are open today through September 11.

This matters for Columbus operators and developers in ways that aren't always obvious.

A "brownfield" is any industrial, commercial, or institutional property where known or suspected contamination has left it idle or underused. The cleanup costs — and the uncertainty about what that cleanup might cost — are often what keeps a site on the shelf for years.

Ohio's Brownfield Remediation Program pays for that cleanup, with grants that cover the full project cost and require no local match. That's not a subsidy — that's a risk transfer that changes the development math.

Franklin County has been one of the most successful applicants in the state. In 2024, the Franklin County Land Bank and COCIC (the county's land bank) submitted 6 applications and won all 6, for a total of nearly $29 million. Prior rounds funded cleanup at Eastland Mall, Gahanna's Creekside District, a parcel in Weinland Park, and asbestos abatement at the former Columbus YMCA building on 40 Long Street.

Eligible applicants include counties, townships, cities, port authorities — and for-profit organizations. Private developers can apply directly.

The window is open now at development.ohio.gov. For Franklin County coordination, contact COCIC at cocic.org/grants before submitting independently.

Full breakdown at Columbus Real Estate News — link in comments.
