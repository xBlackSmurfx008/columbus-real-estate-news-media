### Top themes

**1. Renter cost-burden / affordable-housing gap — LOUD / negative-frustrated — THREAT + content opportunity**

The 2026 COHHIO Gap Report (released March 2026) and a wave of downstream local coverage have kept this conversation loud all year, with fresh peaks in July–August as Vindicator, NewsBreak, Columbus Underground, and ABC6 all republished variations of the finding:

- Columbus has a deficit of **53,000 affordable rental units**; only **27 affordable units exist per 100 extremely low-income renters**.
- A full-time Ohio worker must earn **$23.82/hr** to afford a two-bedroom without being cost-burdened; the average renter earns **$19.59/hr**.
- Nearly half of Ohio's 1.58 million renters are cost-burdened; 73% of extremely low-income renters spend more than half their income on rent — up from 66% five years ago.

Representative coverage:
> "Columbus Rent Crisis 2026: The Numbers Look Affordable, But Thousands of Renters Are Still Being Left Behind" — *NewsBreak / Crafting Your Home*, 2026 — https://www.newsbreak.com/crafting-your-home-355087212/4808651217814-columbus-rent-crisis-2026-the-numbers-look-affordable-but-thousands-of-renters-are-still-being-left-behind

> "Renting Has Become Unaffordable for Nearly Half of Ohioans" — *Columbus Underground*, reporting on COHHIO Gap Report — https://columbusunderground.com/report-renting-has-become-unaffordable-for-nearly-half-of-ohioans-ocj1/

> "Ohio's lack of affordable housing just getting worse" — *The Vindicator*, July 2026 — https://www.vindy.com/opinion/editorials/2026/07/ohios-lack-of-affordable-housing-just-getting-worse/

Frame: Threat to audience (renters) and content opportunity for CRE News. Average rents ($1,366–$1,410/mo, up 1.4–2% YoY) look benign in aggregate; the community is pushing back on that headline figure because it masks who gets left out. Multiple distinct authors/outlets driving this — not a single-source flare.

Note: Matter News specifically angles on displacement of tenants when LIHTC-backed affordable housing is redeveloped into market-rate. A distinct underreported sub-story: https://matternews.org/community/development/new-affordable-housing-investments-may-leave-columbus-most-vulnerable-properties-and-tenants-behind/

**2. Harrison West / Founders Park apartment swap — STEADY / negative-frustrated — content opportunity**

Thrive Companies has filed to replace the last planned piece of the Founders Park mixed-use development (531 W. 5th Ave) — originally 1 townhouse building + 20 condos — with a 60-unit apartment building. The request includes reducing required parking from 90 to 15 spaces. The Harrison West homeowners association is pushing back publicly via NBC4 and Columbus Underground.

> "This shift undermines the trust we, as residents, placed in the original development plan, which was intended to promote long-term stability and homeownership in our neighborhood and balance apartments with townhomes and single-family homes in the development." — *Harrison West HOA statement, via NBC4 WCMH-TV* — https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/real-estate/residents-concerned-by-developers-revised-plan-for-columbus-neighborhood/

> "Battelle Project in Harrison West Moving Forward" (background on the 21-acre Founders Park site history) — *Columbus Underground* — https://columbusunderground.com/battelle-project-in-harrison-west-moving-forward-bw1/

Context: Founders Park is 95% complete. This is the final parcel. The Columbus Division of Fire does not support the parking reduction. The dispute sits at the intersection of two big city-wide conversations — parking minimums and pro-density rezoning (Zone In) vs. neighborhood plan integrity.

Frame: Content opportunity. CRE News has not covered the Harrison West/Founders Park dispute. This is a live zoning/neighborhood-trust story with a named developer, a named HOA, city agency opposition, and a concrete address.

**3. Zone In Phase 2 — public comment open now — STEADY / mixed-curious — content opportunity**

Columbus's 60-year zoning overhaul is at its most consequential phase: a 60-day public comment period on Phase 2 is running through summer 2026, with City Council scheduled to consider the full package in Fall 2026. Phase 2 would rezone ~40% of the city (primarily transportation corridors), allow taller buildings, reduce parking minimums, and cut the variance-seeking required for site-by-site deals.

> "New gallery gives residents closer look at Zone In proposal" — *Axios Columbus*, April 2024 background — https://www.axios.com/local/columbus/2024/04/24/zone-in-zoning-ohio-affordable-housing-density

> "Columbus City Council resolution lays path for zoning changes to bring more urban density" — *Yahoo News / Columbus Dispatch wire* — https://news.yahoo.com/columbus-city-council-resolution-lays-100829171.html

Zone In FAQ resource actively being circulated: https://zone-in-columbus.hub.arcgis.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions

Frame: CRE News covered Zone In Phase 2 draft release (Aug 23 article). Community is now in the comment-period phase; the conversation is shifting from "what is this?" to "how does this affect my street?" — a second, distinct story angle. No single dominant sentiment: urban-density advocates and neighborhood-character preservationists are both loud.

**4. Columbus housing market: strong but tightening — STEADY / cautiously positive — investor-audience signal**

The Columbus REALTORS July 2026 report landed this month and is circulating through broker/investor social channels:

- Closed sales: 3,083 (+6.3% YoY)
- Median sale price: $350,000 (+2.3% YoY; +4.4% YTD vs. 2025)
- Inventory: 6,193 units, highest July level in over a decade
- Supply: 2.4 months (still a seller's market, but improving)
- Rents: up ~3.1% YoY, average $1,366/mo

> "Central Ohio's housing market showed balanced growth with increases in sales, prices, and inventory all occurring simultaneously — a sign of a healthy market." — *The Columbus Team, Central Ohio Housing Report July 2026* — https://thecolumbusteam.com/central-ohio-housing-report/

> "Investors are increasingly targeting homes that sit longer on the market, allowing room for negotiation and value-add improvements." — *The Mancini Group, 2026 Market Predictions* — https://www.themancinigroupsells.com/the-hub/2026-market-predictions

Frame: Investor-audience signal. The "balance returning" narrative is consistent with what multiple brokers are saying. This is a positive-leaning story for investors but could read as frustrating to buyers and renters who don't feel the balance in their price range.

**5. Franklinton gentrification / artist displacement — STEADY / negative-frustrated — watchlist**

WOSU, Matter News, and Medium-hosted writing continue to circulate on Franklinton's dual development track — high-end housing and business investment arriving without a grocery store or affordable-rent preservation.

> "In Franklinton, high-end business and housing developments are popping up while there is no major grocery store for its existing residents." — *WOSU Public Media, "A tale of two Franklintons"* — https://www.wosu.org/arts-culture/2024-02-12/a-tale-of-two-franklintons-development-and-affordability-spar-in-central-ohios-oldest-community

> "Some residents worry that as the neighborhood develops, the artists and mom-and-pop shops will get pushed out, with some artist and art spaces already having left the neighborhood because of pressure from developers or rising rent prices." — *Medium / Massarah Mikati, "Bottoms Up: Gentrification in Franklinton"* — https://medium.com/@sfmikati/bottoms-up-gentrification-in-franklinton-b9389ad04bcd

Frame: Watchlist. Not a breaking story — this conversation has been steady for 2+ years. But with New City Homes' 50-home project completing and the Zone In public comment period open, Franklinton could surface again as a focal point. CRE News has not published a dedicated Franklinton neighborhood piece recently.
