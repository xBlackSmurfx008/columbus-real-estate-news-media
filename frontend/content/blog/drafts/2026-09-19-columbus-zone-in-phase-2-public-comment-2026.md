# Columbus Zone In Phase 2 Opens 70,000 Parcels for Comment Through Oct. 24

Columbus released the public review draft of Zone In Phase 2 on August 20, covering roughly 70,000 parcels — about 41 percent of the city's land — under nine new zoning districts. Residents, developers, and property owners have until October 24 to submit comments before the proposal goes to City Council for a final vote.

## What Phase 2 covers

Zone In is Columbus's first comprehensive overhaul of its zoning code in decades. Phase 1, passed by City Council in 2024, rewrote the rules for downtown, the Short North, and the High Street corridor — areas characterized by dense commercial and mixed-use development. Phase 2, formally titled [Economic and Housing Opportunity](https://zone-in-columbus.hub.arcgis.com/pages/frequently-asked-questions), extends the reform to three additional categories of land that Phase 1 did not address.

The 70,000-plus parcels in scope represent roughly 41 percent of the city's total land area, according to [Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/next-phase-of-zoning-reform-will-cover-43-of-city-bw1/). The [Columbus Navigator](https://www.columbusnavigator.com/columbus-zoning-changes-zone-in-phase-two/) cited a similar figure of approximately 40 percent. A third and final phase covering single-family residential neighborhoods is expected to begin this year and conclude in 2027, according to city planning documents.

## The three district types

The draft proposal organizes the nine new districts into three functional categories:

**Mixed-use corridors.** Phase 2 adds a new tier of streets beyond those already revised in Phase 1 — corridors that either already blend housing with neighborhood-scale retail or that the city wants to push in that direction. New zoning here is designed to allow more housing near transit and employment centers without requiring a discretionary rezoning hearing for each project.

**Industrial and warehouse districts.** Columbus has substantial industrial land east, south, and west of the urban core. Phase 2 updates these districts to separate light and heavy industrial uses more clearly and to address conversion pressure as neighborhoods evolve near downtown.

**Campuses.** Hospitals, universities, and major employment centers — including the Ohio State University Medical Center, Nationwide Children's Hospital, and OhioHealth — generate concentrated demand and activity on their edges. The draft creates dedicated campus-adjacent districts intended to channel how those perimeter areas develop.

## What it means for Columbus real estate

For operators and investors, the practical impact of Phase 2 depends on where specific parcels land in the new map — details the city has published on the [Zone In website](https://zone-in-columbus.hub.arcgis.com/). The interactive map is worth checking against any acquisition, assemblage, or repositioning play in areas outside the High Street/downtown core that Phase 1 already addressed.

The broader effect, if the proposal is adopted as drafted, is a shift toward more by-right development options across a wider share of the city. By-right approval allows a project that meets written zoning standards to proceed without a discretionary hearing, reducing timeline, carrying costs, and approval risk. Phase 1 created those conditions along major corridors; Phase 2 would extend them to industrial, warehouse, and campus-edge parcels.

One caution: the proposal is still a draft and the public comment window exists precisely because the final text can and does change in response to input. Operators building underwriting assumptions from the current map should monitor the final ordinance, expected later this year.

## How to participate before October 24

The city is accepting written input through the [Zone In survey](https://zone-in-columbus.hub.arcgis.com/) through October 24. The Planning Division has also opened a walk-in gallery at **141 N. Front St.** in downtown Columbus, where planning staff walk through the proposal in person:

- **Weekdays:** 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. and 4 to 6 p.m.
- **Saturdays:** 11 a.m. to 1 p.m.

Written questions can be directed to zoningupdate@columbus.gov or 614-645-5343.

## What to watch next

After the comment period closes October 24, city planning staff will review every submission and prepare a revised, final proposal for City Council. [The Columbus Navigator reported](https://www.columbusnavigator.com/columbus-zoning-changes-zone-in-phase-two/) that a final Council vote is expected before the end of 2026. If Council approves, the new districts would take legal effect — the point at which Phase 2 becomes the operative code for those 70,000-plus parcels.

Phase 3, which addresses the largest remaining share of the city — single-family residential neighborhoods — is scheduled to begin in 2026 and is planned for completion in 2027, according to the [City of Columbus](https://www.columbus.gov/News-articles/City-of-Columbus-Proposes-to-Expand-Reach-and-Focus-of-Zoning-Code-Modernization).
