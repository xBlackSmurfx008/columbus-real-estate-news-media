## Lead 1 — Downtown Columbus Inc. Secures Emergency Historic Designation for 17 S. High St. (Huntington Bank Building), Enabling State Tax Credit Application for Adaptive Reuse with OSU Moritz College of Law

**Summary:** Columbus City Council passed an emergency ordinance on September 28, 2026 to list the 1926-era, 13-story Huntington National Bank building at 17 S. High St. in downtown Columbus on the Columbus Register of Historic Properties as CR #84 (Ord. 2569-2026). The emergency designation was needed to meet a September 29, 2026 state historic tax credit application deadline in connection with the building's planned "rehabilitation and adaptive use." Owner Downtown Columbus, Inc. purchased the building from Huntington Bank in January 2025 for $4,514,520. Background reporting (Yahoo Finance, September 14, 2026) confirmed that Ohio State University Moritz College of Law will occupy the former bank branch space once Huntington Bank completes its relocation to a new storefront within the same building, expected summer 2027. The OSU Moritz occupancy includes a permanent historical display preserving the original teller line, P.W. Huntington's office, the building clock, and other significant features.

## Lead 2 — Columbus Formalizes 279.5-Acre Western Annexation in Brown-Norwich Township, Adjacent to Pulte Homes Land Along Alton Darby Creek Corridor

**Summary:** Columbus City Council voted 9–0 on September 28, 2026 to formally accept the annexation of 279.5± acres in Brown and Norwich Township (Franklin County) to the City of Columbus (AN26-008, Ord. 2467-2026). The territory is located along Alton Darby Creek Road on the west side of Columbus, near the Columbus-Hilliard boundary. Petitioners are Tracy L. Zwayer and Richard L. Zwayer II (trustees) and related landowners, and the parcels include Alton Darby Creek Holding LLC-owned land. The annexation territory is directly contiguous to property owned by Pulte Homes of Ohio LLC, one of the largest national homebuilders. The petition was filed with Franklin County on May 14, 2026; Franklin County approved it June 23, 2026; the Columbus City Clerk received notice July 9, 2026. By formally accepting the annexation, Columbus extends its corporate boundary and municipal service obligations (water, sewer, police, fire) to this 279.5-acre tract, enabling future development under Columbus zoning codes.
