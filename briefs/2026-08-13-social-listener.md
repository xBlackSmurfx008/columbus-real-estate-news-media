### 1. Inventory Creep — Columbus Is Quietly Shifting Toward Buyers
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Mixed (cautious optimism from buyers; sellers nervous) | **Frame:** Content idea + opportunity

Central Ohio housing inventory climbed roughly 7–7.6% year-over-year to approximately 5,497 homes for sale as of early August 2026 — the highest level buyers have had to work with in years. The shift is real but not dramatic: supply stands at 2.2 months (six months is "balanced"), homes are still selling at 98.5% of asking price, and the median sale price has pushed past $335,000.

What has changed is pace and pressure. A Wayne Woods (local broker) market update published August 4, 2026 noted explicitly: "homes aren't flying off the market in 48 hours anymore, and for-sale signs are staying up a little longer." Columbus REALTORS President Gloria Alonso Cannon confirmed conditions remain competitive but are improving for buyers.

The public conversation is picking up as first-time buyers who have been waiting on the sidelines try to interpret the signals. No Reddit thread-level data was recoverable (site blocked), but broker commentary and market aggregator coverage reflects questions buyers are actively asking agents.

### 2. ADUs: Legal Since December — But Can You Actually Afford to Build One?
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Frustrated (cost gap) / Hopeful (legal permission) | **Frame:** Content idea + threat (to ADU-as-housing-solution narrative)

Columbus legalized ADUs across all residential districts in November 2025 (effective December 24), eliminating the multi-variance requirement that previously made them impractical for most homeowners. The conversation since has moved from "can we build one?" to "how much will it cost?"

The answer is sobering. ADU construction in Columbus runs $150–$400 per square foot in 2026, with typical detached units costing $230,000+. A current city code requirement — that an ADU connect to city sewer lines *independently* from the main home — adds tens of thousands to that cost by requiring excavation through driveways and yards.

A June 18, 2026 Greater Ohio Policy Center study (covered by WOSU, WYSO, and Ideastream) proposed letting ADU sewer lines route through the main home, potentially saving homeowners significantly. Columbus City Council and Columbus Water and Power have been in discussions about subsidizing sewer connections. The July 29, 2026 WOSU conversation on "How can ADUs help address housing needs in Columbus?" brought this friction to a public audience.

> "Columbus city code says an ADU must connect to city sewer lines independently from the main home on a property. The Greater Ohio Policy Center argues that letting an ADU's sewer line be routed through the main home would potentially save tens of thousands in construction costs." — *WYSO, June 18, 2026, confirmed in search index, source: wysu.org/ohio-news/2026-06-18/study-shows-how-columbus-other-ohio-cities-could-ease-costs-of-building-accessory-dwelling-units*

The gap between what ADU legalization promises and what homeowners can afford to execute is an active, unresolved public conversation.

**Content gap:** "Columbus legalized ADUs — but can most homeowners actually afford to build one?" — an explainer that walks through realistic cost ranges, the sewer connection problem, what the city is doing about it, and what a typical Columbus homeowner's realistic options are right now. High search value; no single local outlet has written it clearly.

### 3. Mercy on Main — NFL Player's Affordable Housing Project Advances in Olde Towne East
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Positive / Curious | **Frame:** Opportunity + content idea

Chinedum Ndukwe — a Powell, Ohio native who played safety for the Cincinnati Bengals and Oakland Raiders before retiring in 2012 — has proposed a 65-unit, five-story affordable apartment building at 907–917 E. Main St. in Olde Towne East through his firm Kingsley + Co. The project is called Mercy on Main, named for Ndukwe's grandmother Mercy Okoronkwo.

The $22.8M project received a reservation through Ohio's competitive 9% Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) program. Financing includes ~$14.7M in federal tax credits, a $4M permanent mortgage, and $3.25M in city HOME funding. Unit mix: 8 studios, 29 one-bedroom, 18 two-bedroom, 10 three-bedroom. Amenities include a community room, fitness center, outdoor patio, and secured building access. Eight parking spaces on the roughly half-acre site.

NBC4 and Columbus Underground both covered the development. It represents the "affordable housing + athlete gives back locally" narrative that generates broad community resonance across multiple audiences.

> "As a native of Powell, Ohio, just outside Columbus, the project allows Ndukwe to reinvest in the community that helped shape his values." — *Kingsley + Co. press release, confirmed via kingsleyandcompany.com/post/kingsley-co-expands-affordable-housing-efforts-into-columbus-with-mercy-on-main*

**Content idea:** "From Powell to the Bengals to Olde Towne East: Chinedum Ndukwe's bet on Columbus affordable housing" — a profile story that traces the athlete-to-developer arc, explains how LIHTC actually works for a lay reader, and places this project in Olde Towne East's larger affordability context. Strong candidate for social sharing and search traffic on both real estate and sports-crossover audiences.

### 4. NRP Group Breaks Ground on 336-Unit Market-Rate Community — Its First in Columbus
**Volume:** Light-to-Steady | **Sentiment:** Neutral-to-Positive | **Frame:** Content idea (market-rate supply push)

The NRP Group broke ground on OSU East on August 5, 2026 — a 336-unit upscale apartment community on a 27.5-acre site at West Dublin Granville Road in Northwest Columbus. This is NRP's first market-rate project in the city (the firm has previously built only affordable housing here). Development is supported by Carlyle Group equity and PNC Bank financing, with the City of Columbus as a key partner through entitlement.

The site will include 11 three-story buildings with one-, two-, and three-bedroom homes, a clubhouse, fitness center, coworking space, resort pool, volleyball and bocce courts. M/I Homes of Central Ohio will break ground on an adjacent 138-unit for-sale townhome community in spring 2027.

Coverage appeared in REBusinessOnline, Yahoo Finance, FinancialContent, RE Journals, and BizWire.

The project is notable as a signal of investor confidence in Columbus's market-rate rental demand — even as the city's affordable conversation dominates policy coverage.

> "OSU East marks The NRP Group's first market-rate community in Columbus." — *REBusinessOnline, August 5, 2026, source: rebusinessonline.com/nrp-group-breaks-ground-on-336-unit-apartment-development-in-columbus-ohio/*

### 5. All In Dublin — A New Model for Disability-Inclusive Affordable Housing Breaks Ground
**Volume:** Light | **Sentiment:** Positive / Supportive | **Frame:** Content idea (new housing model)

The Dublin Planning and Zoning Commission approved and construction is now scheduled (Summer 2026) for All In Dublin — a 75-unit, $24M disability-inclusive affordable housing development at Dublin Center Drive and West Dublin Granville Road. The project is a partnership among All Inclusive Living (All In), TFG Housing Resources, and CMHA.

The model deliberately mixes populations: 25% of units reserved for low-income people with disabilities; 40% for older adults; remaining units at higher-income "affordable workplace housing" requirements. The design, developed with direct input from disabled residents and older adults, incorporates shared community spaces intended to encourage mutual support rather than segregating disability housing from the broader building.

Coverage from ABC6, 10TV, NBC4, and Columbus Underground. The "inclusive housing" model is distinctive enough to generate reader interest beyond Columbus, and the public design process is itself a template worth documenting.

> "All In Dublin [uses] a neighbor-based housing model intended to encourage social connection and mutual support among residents." — *Dublin Ohio Economic Development, confirmed in search index, source: econdev.dublinohiousa.gov/all-in-dublin-a-new-model-for-inclusive-affordable-housing/*

**Content idea:** "Dublin's Housing Experiment: What Happens When People With and Without Disabilities Live as Neighbors?" — a feature on the All In Dublin design process and its challenge to the traditional segregation model in disability housing. Angle is both local and nationally timely.

**Volume:** Light | **Sentiment:** Positive (luxury market confidence) | **Frame:** Content idea

Construction is underway on The Ellis at Bridge Park, an 89-unit luxury condominium building in Dublin's J Block expansion at Bridge Park Avenue and Dale Drive. Developer: Crawford Hoying. ### 7. OHFA Draft 4% LIHTC Guidelines — Policy Pipeline Watching Today
**Volume:** Light (specialist audience) | **Sentiment:** Watchful | **Frame:** Policy signal / opportunity

The Ohio Housing Finance Agency held its Multifamily Committee Meeting today (August 13, 2026) in Columbus to discuss draft guidelines for 4% Low-Income Housing Tax Credits with Bond Gap Financing — a key financing mechanism for large affordable housing projects. The meeting was held publicly at OHFA's 2600 Corporate Exchange Drive location with virtual attendance available.

This is a policy-pipeline event that most Columbus residents don't follow — but the draft guidelines will shape which affordable housing projects can get built in the metro over the next 12–18 months. The NH&RA flagged the meeting ahead of time, meaning the affordable housing development community was paying attention.

No community quotes or reactions are available from today's meeting; this is flagged as an emerging policy thread to monitor.

**Content gap:** "Ohio's 4% tax credit rules are being rewritten. Here's why Columbus developers care." — a policy explainer written for an educated general audience (investors, civic advocates) who've heard "LIHTC" but don't know how the mechanism controls what gets built, where.
