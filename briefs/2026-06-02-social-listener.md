#### 1. Columbus Is Wall Street's #1 Single-Family Landlord Market — and Residents Are Asking Why ⚠️ NEGATIVE LEADS
**Volume:** Loud | **Sentiment:** Alarmed / Frustrated
**Frame:** Threat + Accountability Story

Columbus and Cleveland top every national ranking for institutional single-family landlord acquisition in the U.S. in 2025–2026. Six national companies — American Homes 4 Rent, Amherst, FirstKey Homes, Progress Residential, Starwood Capital, and Vinebrook Homes — now control more than 6,000 Columbus-area homes. Data provider Parcl Labs recorded institutional investors acquiring more than 600 Columbus properties in a single three-month window; Ohio overall accounts for 14.2% of all U.S. institutional single-family investment, with one in ten homes bought by institutional operators nationwide located in Columbus. In January 2026, the Trump administration issued an executive order ("Stopping Wall Street from Competing with Main Street Homebuyers") using federal financing and regulatory levers to limit large investor access to government-backed acquisition tools — but legal and policy analysts note the order's practical impact on existing ownership is limited. Community advocates are connecting the institutional ownership concentration directly to Columbus's affordable housing shortage and rising eviction rates.

**Why it leads:** Per playbook, negative sentiment about the project's subject community goes first. The community conversation is not asking whether Wall Street landlords exist in Columbus — it is asking why Columbus is the top market, whether the Trump EO actually helps, and whether tenant advocacy organizations have the resources to respond at the scale of 6,000 institutionally controlled homes.

**Volume basis:** NBC4 (two confirmed articles, distinct authors), Axios Columbus, ABC6 / WSYX, NLIHC (National Low Income Housing Coalition), Brookings Institution, AEI, The Regulatory Review — seven distinct sources across local, national, and policy channels. Parcl Labs cited as underlying data source across multiple outlets.

#### 2. Ohio's Renter Squeeze: 53,000-Unit Gap, 73% Income Burden — The Data Is Circulating ⚠️ NEGATIVE
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Alarmed / Frustrated
**Frame:** Threat + Content Idea

Two 2026 reports are actively circulating in Columbus housing conversations: the COHHIO *Gap Report 2026* (Ohio Housing Finance Agency data) and the OHFA FY2026 Housing Needs Assessment. Key figures being cited: Columbus has a shortage of 53,000 affordable rental units. Only 27 affordable and available units exist for every 100 extremely low-income (ELI) renters in Columbus. 73% of Ohio's ELI renters spend more than 50% of their income on rent. #### 3. Zone In vs. Area Commissions: The Map Fight Is Heating Up
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Skeptical / Resistant
**Frame:** Threat + Accountability Story

Multiple Columbus area commissions are declining to endorse the next phase of the City's Zone In zoning overhaul, which would consolidate individual neighborhood land-use maps into a single citywide map. The Greater South East Area Commission explicitly cited concerns about a 2018 neighborhood plan being overridden: *"one uniform map could disregard their map, which was just updated in 2018."* The Southeast Commission also recently released a competing document — the "Eastland for Everyone" community plan — setting a neighborhood-controlled vision for growth areas. The West Side and Milo-Grogan area commissions have surfaced similar resistance. Phase III of Zone In (single-family residential districts) is expected to reach public engagement in 2027, meaning the current conflict over commercial/mixed-use corridors is a preview of a sharper fight ahead.

**Volume basis:** WOSU Public Media (confirmed indexed, two articles), City of Columbus (columbus.gov Zone In page confirmed), Zone-In Columbus ArcGIS hub (confirmed), MA Design (search-indexed) — four distinct sources.
