### LinkedIn

Franklin County mailed new property assessments to every property owner in June — and the average residential value went up about 9% from the 2023 reappraisal.

Here's what that actually means and what you can do about it before the window closes:

📋 **The update:** Ohio requires county auditors to refresh values every three years. This is Franklin County's 2026 triennial update. The new values affect your 2027 tax bill, not your current payments.

💰 **The math:** A home assessed at $275,000 in 2023 is now roughly $299,750. Ohio taxes residential properties on 35% of appraised value, so your taxable base rises from about $96,250 to $104,913 — an increase of $8,663. Multiply that across a multi-property portfolio and the exposure adds up.

🗓️ **The deadline:** Informal review sessions with the Auditor's Office run only through September 2026. They're free, low-stakes, and the single easiest step if you think your new value is off. After that, you can file a formal complaint with the Board of Revision by March 31, 2027 — but the informal path is simpler for most cases.

🎯 **Who should act:** Owners of properties with known issues not captured by the automated assessment, investors who bought in 2024–25 at a price below the new assessed value, and anyone whose assessment exceeds what similar properties are actually selling for right now.

If your value looks right, no action needed. But if it's off, September is your window.
