The Roman Catholic Diocese of Columbus has applied to tear down its 1908 five-story building at 197 E. Gay St. and convert the site to surface parking. The Downtown Commission — the body that governs demolitions in the historic district — will hear the application at its September 22 meeting.

## September 22 Agenda

Columbus's Downtown Commission is scheduled to take up the Diocese's demolition application at its September 22 regular meeting, [according to Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/plan-to-demolish-downtown-buildings-to-be-heard-by-board-bw1/) and [the City of Columbus's 2026 Downtown Commission meeting calendar](https://www.columbus.gov/files/sharedassets/city/v/1/business-and-development/economic-development/planning/documents/2.-planning-docs/dc/dc-2026-final.pdf). The Diocese filed the application as a Certificate of Appropriateness — the form of approval required for any demolition within a designated historic district in Columbus.

A Certificate of Appropriateness (COA) is effectively a permit that must precede any demolition work on a historic property. Without the commission's approval, no demolition permit can be issued. The Downtown Commission is the city body empowered to grant or deny that approval for properties inside the downtown historic district.

## The Building at 197 E. Gay St.

The five-story building at 197 E. Gay St. dates to 1908, making it among the older surviving office structures in the immediate downtown core. The Diocese's offices occupy the building alongside [Catholic Social Services and the Our Lady of Guadalupe Center](https://columbusunderground.com/church-wants-to-tear-down-5-story-downtown-building-bw1/), which serves Spanish-speaking Catholic parishioners in central Ohio.

The property sits directly behind Saint Joseph Cathedral, adjacent to the Columbus Athenaeum, and across the street from the Neighborhood Launch residential condominiums — market-rate units built in the early 2000s to repopulate downtown Columbus's historic core.

The Diocese's demolition plan calls for replacing the structure with surface parking. A Diocese spokesperson told Columbus Underground that the organization had "nothing to add beyond what was stated on the application."

## Safety Order and the Application's Path

Columbus Underground reported that the city placed an unsafe order on the building following a brick wall collapse earlier this year. The unsafe order documents the building's condition but does not resolve the COA process — that determination belongs to the Downtown Commission.

As of this writing, no demolition permit has been pulled for 197 E. Gay St. The city's planning division received the Certificate of Appropriateness filing before the commission's review step. The Diocese's own website has since created a listing for the site under the heading "197 E. Gay St. Parking Lot," which appears to reflect internal planning rather than any approved outcome.

## How the Commission Has Ruled Recently

The Downtown Commission is not an advisory body — it exercises binding authority over Certificates of Appropriateness in Columbus's historic downtown district. Demolition proposals that pair building removal with surface parking have faced resistance from the commission in recent months.

In August 2026, [the Downtown Commission denied a demolition application at 195 S. Washington Ave.](https://myfox28columbus.com/news/local/downtown-columbus-commission-denies-demolition-ohio-dilapidated-buildings-commons-national-bank-building-south-high-street) that would have replaced a downtown structure with a parking lot. The commission's rejection came without an approved plan for a replacement building. Whether the Diocese's application — which includes an unsafe order and immediate adjacency to Saint Joseph Cathedral — is materially different is a question the commission will decide on September 22.

Commissioners weigh factors including the building's historical significance, the structural condition, the applicant's plans for the site after demolition, and consistency with the district's design guidelines. The commission can approve, deny, or table an application pending additional information such as a structural engineering report or a development plan.

## Community Opposition in Print

Columbus Underground published an opinion piece headlined ["The Catholic Church Can Afford to Fix Their Downtown Building"](https://columbusunderground.com/opinion-the-catholic-church-can-afford-to-fix-their-downtown-building/) ahead of the commission hearing. The piece argues the Diocese has the financial means to rehabilitate rather than demolish. Opinion columns do not bind the commission's decision, which turns on the application and the district's governing guidelines — but the piece signals that the application has drawn attention from people who care about preservation in a part of downtown where each building's fate shapes the character of the surrounding block.

The building's location in a visually prominent spot — adjacent to Saint Joseph Cathedral at one of downtown Columbus's busiest pedestrian corners — gives the outcome outsized visibility relative to most demolition cases the commission hears.

## What the Vote Means

If the commission approves the COA, the Diocese can pull a demolition permit and proceed. If it denies the application, the Diocese can appeal through the Board of Zoning Adjustment or revise and resubmit. A tabling — common when commissioners want additional documentation — would push any final decision to a later meeting.

The September 22 decision will be one of the more visible historic-preservation questions in downtown Columbus in 2026. The commission's ruling will also signal how it weighs an unsafe order — a safety designation the city places on buildings with documented structural deficiencies — against the district's long-standing preference for development over demolition-to-parking.
