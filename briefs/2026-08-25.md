### 2. Mercy on Main — 65-Unit Affordable Housing Proposed for Olde Towne East (cover later)

**Coverage signal:** Cover later — hold pending primary-source milestone
**Confidence:** 3/5
**What:** Cincinnati developer Kingsley + Co. (founder Chinedum Ndukwe, former NFL player) has proposed Mercy on Main, a $22.8 million, 65-unit affordable housing development at 907–917 E. Main St. in Olde Towne East. The five-story project would include 8 studios, 29 one-bedroom, 18 two-bedroom, and 10 three-bedroom units. It is Kingsley + Co.'s first LIHTC (Low-Income Housing Tax Credit) project in Columbus. The Zone In Phase 1 code already enables five-story buildings by right on E. Main St., removing the need for a zoning variance.
**Why it matters:** The Zone In connection is the editorial angle: a Columbus zoning reform passed in 2024 directly enabled an affordable housing proposal that would have required a variance before. The $22.8 million project at a specific Olde Towne East address is the first confirmed instance of Zone In Phase 1's by-right height allowances attracting an out-of-market affordable housing developer to E. Main. OHFA LIHTC financing is a competitive state program; the project is not yet awarded.

### LinkedIn (~1,100 chars)

One Hospitality is opening Sugar — a Latin, Spanish, and Mediterranean restaurant — at 504 Park St. in Columbus on August 27.

The group behind Forno Kitchen + Bar, Astra Rooftop, and Standard Hall has converted the former Howl at the Moon space at 504 Park St. into a two-floor restaurant concept with a full outdoor patio. The interior features a custom staircase, hand-drawn wallpaper, and greenery leading to the patio — which has fire pits, cabanas, lounge seating, and photo booths.

On the menu: chipotle chicken tacos, pan-seared salmon, blackened garlic shrimp pasta, and quesadillas, alongside an agave-focused cocktail program.

The name echoes Sugar Bar, a dance club that ran at 525 N. Park St. from 2005 to 2010.

For the Grand Opening, One Hospitality is hosting a ticketed street party on Park Street on August 27 from 5 to 11 p.m. — live DJ, local vendors, light bites, signature cocktails, and expanded outdoor seating.

From a commercial real estate angle: this is the reactivation of a prominent Park Street vacancy — a national chain entertainment concept turned into a locally operated, multi-floor neighborhood restaurant. That conversion pattern has been common in the Columbus core since 2020, and Sugar is the latest, and arguably most prominent, example on this block.
