### 1. Harrison West Founders Park — Residents Feel Betrayed by Rezoning to All-Apartments
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Negative / Frustrated | **Frame:** Threat + content idea

Thrive Companies filed to rezone 531 W. 5th Ave in Harrison West — part of the Founders Park mixed-use development along the Olentangy River — from planned townhomes and condos to a 60-unit apartment building. The original plan was built over a decade of community conversation; Thrive says the townhomes aren't selling and they've abandoned hundreds of thousands in architectural investment. Columbus City Council approved the rezoning. The Harrison West Society and neighboring homeowners are calling it a trust violation.

The Columbus Division of Fire did not support the parking changes, citing concerns about unauthorized parking blocking emergency vehicles.

> "This shift undermines the trust we, as residents, placed in the original development plan, which was intended to promote long-term stability and homeownership in our neighborhood and balance apartments with townhomes and single-family homes in the development." — **Harrison West Society** (homeowners association), via NBC4/Yahoo News, *source: nbc4i.com/news/local-news/real-estate/residents-concerned-by-developers-revised-plan-for-columbus-neighborhood/* [domain blocked from direct fetch; quote confirmed in search index]

Multiple distinct sources — NBC4, Yahoo News, CitizenPortal.ai — covered this with resident participation at the council hearing. Volume signal is based on cross-outlet coverage, not single-author claims.

**Content idea:** "How Founders Park Lost the Community's Trust" — an accountability piece that traces the decade-long resident-developer compact and what precedent this sets for rezoning in mixed-use master plans citywide.

### 2. German Village Cedar Square — Four Rejections and Counting
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Frustrated (split: neighbors oppose, housing advocates frustrated by blockage) | **Frame:** Content idea + threat (to Zone In narrative)

The Cedar Square apartment proposal near East Livingston Ave and South Sixth Street has now been denied approval by the German Village Commission at least twice in its current scaled-down form — the fourth iteration overall. The project started at 96 units with 47 parking spaces in early 2024; it is now down to 51 units in two three-story buildings with 61 parking spaces. Still blocked.

The commission's persistent denial sits in direct tension with the city's Zone In upzoning push, which is meant to streamline exactly this kind of in-fill development in transit-connected neighborhoods. No verbatim commissioner quotes were recoverable in this run.

The gap: German Village is a historic district with its own commission layer. That's the structural reason for the disconnect — but most Columbus residents don't understand why Zone In hasn't affected German Village's approvals.

**Content idea:** "Why Zone In Can't Touch German Village" — an explainer on how historic district commissions sit outside the normal upzoning pipeline, and what that means for residents hoping for neighborhood-by-neighborhood change.
