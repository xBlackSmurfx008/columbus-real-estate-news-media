### Top themes

**1. Diocese Gay Street demolition — community backlash is loud and fast**
- Volume: **loud** (two Columbus Underground opinion pieces published same day as news story; multiple distinct authors)
- Sentiment: **negative / outraged**
- Frame: **threat to historic downtown + content validation (CREN covered the news story this morning)**

The Diocese of Columbus COA filing to demolish the 1908 building at 197 E. Gay St. for surface parking has generated immediate community reaction. Columbus Underground published two opinion pieces within hours of its own news story:

> "The Roman Catholic Diocese of Columbus, which has operated their corporate offices out of a sturdy square brick building for the past sixty-some years, has suddenly decided that the structure must come down because they've failed to be good stewards to this historic building." — Columbus Underground opinion, "Goodbye, Gay Street" (https://columbusunderground.com/opinion-goodbye-gay-street/)

> "The Diocese currently pays zero property taxes and never will because they belong to an infinity-year tax abatement program." — Columbus Underground opinion, "The Catholic Church Can Afford to Fix Their Downtown Building" (https://columbusunderground.com/opinion-the-catholic-church-can-afford-to-fix-their-downtown-building/)

> "What was the norm in the 1950s and 1960s and 1970s and 1980s and 1990s has not carried forward into the 21st century... the Downtown Commission code does not allow for historic demolition to construct surface parking lots." — Columbus Underground, summarizing Downtown Commission precedent

**Key tension:** Columbus Underground also noted that Finance Fund is simultaneously replacing surface parking with low-income housing on the same street — a direct on-the-ground contrast. The 614NOW piece from earlier this year reinforced the commission's posture: *"'We don't tear down buildings to make parking spots'"* (https://614now.com/2026/hot-topics/we-dont-tear-down-buildings-to-make-parking-spots-downtown-commission-denies-demolition-of-historic-home).

**2. Historic preservation vs. demolition for parking — a downtown pattern, not just one story**
- Volume: **steady** (multiple distinct sources; pattern spans at least four proposals in 2026)
- Sentiment: **mixed / concerned** (preservationists alarmed; developers and some commissioners divided)
- Frame: **content opportunity — pattern story connecting Diocese, Beacon Building, and Downtown Commission precedents**

The Diocese proposal is the fourth case this year of a downtown owner seeking demolition for parking or a non-structural replacement, and the commission has denied the prior three:

- **171–191 S. High St.**: Harsax Management asked to demolish three century-old brick buildings for a grass lawn. Commission denied. Source: Fox28 Columbus (https://myfox28columbus.com/news/local/downtown-columbus-commission-denies-demolition-ohio-dilapidated-buildings-commons-national-bank-building-south-high-street)
- **195 S. Washington Ave.**: Healthcare Realty proposed demolition for parking. Commission stated "non-accessory parking lots are not an appropriate replacement use for a razed building." Source: Columbus Underground (https://columbusunderground.com/plan-to-demolish-downtown-buildings-to-be-heard-by-board-bw1/)
- **Beacon Building, 50 W. Gay St.**: City-owned, vacant since 2018, on Columbus Landmarks' 2026 Most Endangered list — directly across the street from the Diocese site. Source: Columbus Navigator / Axios Columbus (https://www.columbusnavigator.com/most-endangered-buildings-columbus-2026/)

> "Historic schools, Downtown tower among most endangered Columbus sites for 2026" — NBC4 WCMH-TV headline (https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/historic-schools-downtown-tower-among-most-endangered-columbus-sites-for-2026/)

The Beacon Building adds irony: one block from the Diocese, it has been on the endangered list for years, and the city itself has failed to act on it.

**4. $199K permanently affordable condos groundbreaking — East Side, fresh story, not yet in CREN**
- Volume: **light** (two TV outlets; breaking news; not yet in social circulation)
- Sentiment: **positive / curious**
- Frame: **content opportunity — first visible deployment of Columbus's $500M affordable housing bond**

The City of Columbus broke ground on 23 permanently affordable condos at East Broad Street and South Gould Road (Broad & Gould Condominiums), a Community Land Trust partnership with Finance Fund. At $199,000 for two- and three-bedroom units (2BR–3BR, 950–1,300 sq ft), it is among the city's first visible uses of the $500M bond voters approved last November.

> "Home affordability crisis continues as city announces $199k condos coming to the east side" — ABC6 WSYX (https://abc6onyourside.com/news/local/home-affordability-crisis-continues-as-city-announces-199k-condos-coming-to-the-east-side)

> "23 affordable-living condos under construction on Columbus' East Side" — NBC4 WCMH-TV (https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/23-affordable-living-condos-under-construction-on-columbus-east-side/)

Key details: buyers must be at or below 120% AMI; when resold, the unit stays in the land trust at an affordable price (perpetual affordability). City contributing $3M construction + $1.1M homebuyer assistance. Occupancy expected fall 2027. **Not covered by CREN in the last 30 days — potential article.**
