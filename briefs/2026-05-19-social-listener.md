**3. Google's $1 Billion Ohio River Data Center Battle — New Rural Community Angle**
Volume: **STEADY** (Hoodline + Statehouse News Bureau + LimaOhio.com + Hunterbrook + ABC6 — 5 distinct outlets covering the Franklin Furnace story) | Sentiment: **Frustrated / Opposed (rural residents)**
Frame: **Opportunity — New geographic angle on the data center story** — While CRE News has tracked the New Albany/Columbus metro data center story extensively, a parallel battle is emerging in rural southern Ohio. Hoodline published a story about Google's proposed $1 billion, 500,000 sq ft data center in Franklin Furnace (Scioto County, on the Ohio River), describing the community as having "erupted" over the plan. The Ohio EPA held a public hearing on a wetland permit for the project. Reported community concerns include: altered wetland hydrology from the data center footprint, heavy truck traffic on narrow rural roads, and changes to Ohio River habitat used by local anglers and recreationists. Multiple community meetings reportedly drew standing-room-only crowds. The Lima Ohio.com story confirms Google as formally behind the project. The Hunterbrook investigation ("Meta Secretly Behind $1B Ohio Data Center Facing Community Pushback") adds a parallel thread about opacity in data center ownership disclosure. Taken together, these stories document a pattern: rural Ohio communities are encountering data center projects with limited prior notice, large environmental footprints, and limited economic benefit transparency. The statewide Statehouse News Bureau February 2026 story confirmed that "Ohio towns are pushing back against data centers — to varying degrees of success," and that moratoriums are now in place in 12+ municipalities. This is the rural version of what Columbus metro residents have been debating since 2024.
