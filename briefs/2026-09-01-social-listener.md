### Top themes

**1. Rental Registry in Force — Landlord/Tenant Battlelines Drawn — STEADY / mixed-negative — content opportunity**

Columbus City Council passed its Rental Registry ordinance in April 2026 (Councilmember Nick Bankston's bill), requiring all landlords to register rental units at $15/unit, capped at $1,500 per apartment complex. Mandatory individual-unit inspections were removed before passage due to legal concerns; the final version allows preventative three-year inspections of building common areas only. The Columbus Apartment Association (CAA) opposed the ordinance and continues to push back post-passage, with landlord-side accounts arguing costs will roll to tenants.

> "The cost of registering will ultimately be passed on to renters, making housing less affordable." — *Don Brunner, Columbus Apartment Association President, via Columbus Rental Registry Proposal coverage* — https://nationaltoday.com/us/oh/columbus/news/2026/02/23/columbus-rental-registry-proposal-sparks-debate-between-landlords-and-tenant-advocates/

> "Columbus OKs rental registry to hold landlords accountable" — *Yahoo News / Columbus Dispatch wire* — https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/columbus-oks-rental-registry-hold-000000166.html

Frame: Content opportunity. The CAA's "costs passed to renters" argument is circulating on landlord-side channels; tenant advocates counter by pointing to the Latitude Five25 (2022) and Colonial Village (2023) evacuations as evidence that accountability mechanisms were long overdue. Multiple named parties, multiple outlets, multiple sides — confirmed active conversation, not a single-source flare.

**2. Latitude Five25 — Near East Side Towers Face Demolition Under New Ownership — STEADY / cautiously curious — content opportunity**

A new buyer is under contract to acquire the troubled Latitude Five25 property on the Near East Side. The plan calls for demolishing both 15-story towers and rebuilding a comparable number of apartments from the ground up. The towers were the site of mass evacuations in 2022 (hundreds of tenants displaced) and have been a recurring flashpoint in Columbus's conversations about corporate landlord accountability, habitability, and who bears responsibility when a large complex fails.

> "Near East Side Towers Could Come Down Under Redevelopment Proposal" — *Columbus Underground* — https://columbusunderground.com/near-east-side-towers-could-come-down-under-redevelopment-proposal-bw1/

Frame: Content opportunity and accountability angle. This connects directly to Theme 1 (Rental Registry debate: why Bankston cited Latitude Five25 in his floor remarks) and Theme 5 (institutional landlords). A demolish-and-rebuild plan for a 2022 housing crisis site is a significant development story. CRE News has not published a dedicated follow-up on Latitude Five25's status.

**6. ADU Wave — By-Right Policy Live, Community on a Learning Curve — LIGHT / curious-positive — content opportunity**

Following Zone In Phase 1 (by-right ADUs in all residential districts, effective December 2025), WOSU dedicated a full All Sides episode to ADUs on July 29, 2026. A Greater Ohio Policy Center study from June 2026 found that allowing ADU sewer lines to route through the main home — rather than requiring a separate lateral connection — could save Columbus homebuilders tens of thousands of dollars in construction costs, a barrier identified as suppressing actual uptake despite the new by-right rule.

> "expands housing choices, supports multigenerational living, and strengthens the fabric of our community." — *AARP Ohio state director, on ADU ordinance, via Citizen Portal* — https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6713989/Ohio/Columbus-Council-hearing-on-ADU-ordinance-draws-broad-praise-and-sharp-objections

> "How can Accessory Dwelling Units help address housing needs in Columbus?" — *WOSU Public Media, All Sides, July 29, 2026* — https://www.wosu.org/show/all-sides/2026-07-29/how-can-accessory-dwelling-units-help-address-housing-needs-in-columbus

> "Study shows how Columbus, other Ohio cities, could ease costs of building accessory dwelling units" — *WOSU / Greater Ohio Policy Center, June 2026* — https://www.wosu.org/politics-government/2026-06-18/study-shows-how-columbus-other-ohio-cities-could-ease-costs-of-building-accessory-dwelling-units

Frame: Light but policy-moment signal is high. The rule is live; the question is whether homeowners are actually building ADUs and what real-world costs look like. A permit-tracker or homeowner case study would fill a real gap — and September is when first-year results would be surfacing.
