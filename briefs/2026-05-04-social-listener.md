**1. Rental Registry — "We Plan to Challenge It in Court"**
Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Polarized** (landlords: defiant; tenants: cautiously victorious)
Frame: **Threat** (legal outcome uncertain for landlords) | **Content idea** (what the challenge is actually based on — state preemption vs. local registry)

*Continues from May 3 brief (Theme 1) — new development: legal challenge formally stated on record.*

Columbus Apartment Association attorney Dimitri Hatzifotinos has now made the legal challenge explicit in published record: "We plan to challenge it in court." The legal basis cited: (1) the information is already submitted to the Franklin County rental registry, creating redundancy; (2) the city creates "administrative fees for that burden, which are not in the law." Council voted 8-1 on April 20. Individual unit inspections were already stripped from the ordinance due to legal concern before passage — suggesting the city anticipated pushback. The Columbus Underground published a rental-registry opinion piece titled "A Foundation for Accountability" in support of the registry; the Buckeye Institute has a counter-report circulating. Multi-voice, multi-outlet fight with a real legal proceeding now on the record.

> "We plan to challenge it in court." — Dimitri Hatzifotinos, attorney for the Columbus Apartment Association (NBC4, nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-oks-rental-registry-to-hold-landlords-accountable/)

> "It doesn't fit with state law, so it creates more administrative burden. The city is then going to create administrative fees for that burden, which are not in the law." — Dimitri Hatzifotinos, Columbus Apartment Association attorney (NBC4, same source)

> "Opinion: A Foundation for Accountability — Why a Rental Registry is Necessary." — Columbus Underground opinion piece headline (columbusunderground.com/opinion-a-foundation-for-accountability-why-a-rental-registry-is-necessary/)

Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / resigned**
Frame: **Content idea** (the gap between "Columbus is affordable" trade coverage and what the rent-vs-buy math actually shows for residents)

*New to this run — not in prior briefs. Distinct from the general Ohio affordability theme.*

Stacker/Redfin analysis published in 2026 finds that a Columbus resident needs to earn 60.7% more income to afford buying a median-priced home versus renting a comparable unit — significantly above the national average gap of 46.3%. Ohio's 30-year fixed mortgage rate sits at 6.68% as of May 4. Median sale price in Columbus reached $290K in March 2026 (up 3.9% YoY), with 47-day average time on market. The median age of a first-time homebuyer in Ohio has risen to 40. This framing — "how much more do you need to earn to buy?" — is generating search traffic and social sharing on consumer finance aggregators.

> "In Columbus, the income premium to afford a typical home over a typical apartment is 60.7% — notably higher than the national average of 46.3%." — Stacker, citing Redfin data (stacker.com/stories/ohio/columbus/it-cheaper-buy-or-rent-columbus-2026)

Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / alarmed (resident side); cautious (industry side)**
Frame: **Threat** (July 1 deadline is now a hard news peg; New Albany land market directly exposed)

*Rolls over from May 3 brief (Theme 6) — updated: ABC6 confirmed Ohio Ballot Board unanimously approved the petition drive; OSU Farm Office confirms agricultural land-use angle is now in play.*

The ABC6 headline confirms the Ohio Ballot Board "unanimously" approved the petition to begin collecting signatures — a stronger signal of institutional legitimacy than earlier coverage suggested. **6. Latitude Five25 / Near East Side — $150M Affordable Redevelopment Advancing to Site Plan Stage**
Volume: **LIGHT** | Sentiment: **Cautiously positive**
Frame: **Opportunity** (one of the largest affordable housing infill projects in Columbus history; Near East Side equity narrative; institutional capital bet on Columbus)

*New to this run — not in prior briefs.*

NBC4 reports that site plans have been submitted to Columbus for the demolition of the vacant Latitude Five25 towers and replacement with a $150 million affordable housing complex — part of Nuveen's broader $370 million Central Ohio affordable housing investment targeting 1,200+ units over five years. The Latitude Five25 site would yield 393 units (100% affordable, most restricted to 60% AMI). Construction anticipated to start late 2026, complete by end of 2028; zoning approvals expected summer 2026. Franklin County Commissioners approved $25M in grants (December 2024) and a $12.5M loan (March 2025). Volume is light — NBC4 is the primary outlet — but the project scale and Nuveen's institutional backing make it structurally significant.

**7. Tariff-Driven Construction Cost Pressure Continues — Some Columbus Projects "On Hold"**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / worried (developers + buyers)**
Frame: **Threat** (cost pressure is compressing the new-supply pipeline just as Zone In tries to open the door)

*Carries forward from Apr 30 brief — updated with specific tariff rates now confirmed in indexed Columbus coverage.*

Columbus Underground and NBC4 have both published on tariff impacts on Columbus builders. Confirmed rates surfacing in local coverage: 10% tariff on imported timber/lumber; 25% tariff on imported kitchen cabinets, bathroom vanities, and upholstered furniture (effective October 14). Local developers on record: "tariffs translate to major cost increases that are enough to scuttle a project." The average cost to build a single-family home in Columbus is approximately $312,222, with labor averaging $38–58/hr and materials now running 5–10% above national average. The tariff pressure is a direct counterforce to Zone In's supply ambitions — even if zoning is liberalized, if build costs push projects off the ledger, the unit math doesn't close.

> "New Trump Tariffs Have Local Housing Developers Concerned." — Columbus Underground, headline (columbusunderground.com/new-trump-tariffs-have-local-housing-developers-concerned-bw1/)

> "It's challenging — tariffs making central Ohio home construction more expensive." — NBC4, headline (nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/its-challenging-tariffs-making-central-ohio-home-construction-more-expensive/)
