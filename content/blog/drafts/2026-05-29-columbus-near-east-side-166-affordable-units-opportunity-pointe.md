Two vacant buildings on Cleveland Avenue are coming down this week to make room for one of the most significant affordable housing projects to break ground in downtown Columbus this year.

Columbus State Community Partners — the real estate nonprofit arm of Columbus State Community College — is partnering with Woda Cooper Companies to build **Opportunity Pointe**, a 166-unit affordable apartment complex at the corner of Cleveland Avenue and East Long Street. Demolition of the structures at 145 and 159 Cleveland Ave. is expected to wrap by the end of this week, with construction set to begin shortly after.

Total project cost: **$54 million**.

---

## What Is Opportunity Pointe?

Opportunity Pointe will consist of two five-story residential buildings sharing an outdoor amenity space and surface parking lot at one of the Near East Side's most-watched intersections — directly across from Columbus State's main downtown campus. That location is no accident.

The development will offer one-, two-, and three-bedroom units. Because the project secured **Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC)** from the Ohio Housing Finance Agency, all rents are income-restricted based on household income:

- **1-bedroom:** $365–$995/month
- **2-bedroom:** $410–$1,175/month
- **3-bedroom:** $440–$1,295/month

At the lower end of those ranges, Opportunity Pointe would be among the most affordable new construction delivering to Columbus in 2026. Twenty of the 166 units are specifically reserved for Columbus State Community College students.

The project is expected to be complete by the end of 2027.

## Why Columbus State Is Getting Into Real Estate

Columbus State Community Partners, the nonprofit vehicle steering the deal, was created specifically to lead real estate development on behalf of CSCC. The model is part of a growing national trend: educational institutions acting as community anchor developers rather than waiting for market forces to solve the housing problems their own students and staff face.

The logic is direct. As Columbus State's enrollment has grown and central Ohio's cost of living has increased, the college identified housing instability as a student retention issue, not just a community-good problem. Reserving 20 units for CSCC students is a data-driven response: if students can't afford to live near campus, they don't stay enrolled.

## Woda Cooper's Columbus Footprint

Woda Cooper Companies is one of the most active affordable housing developers in the Midwest. The company specializes in LIHTC-financed projects and has deep experience navigating Ohio Housing Finance Agency credit allocations. The Opportunity Pointe partnership deepens the firm's footprint in Columbus's Near East Side, a neighborhood that has seen significant affordable housing investment alongside rising market-rate pressure over the past decade.

For Columbus operators watching the LIHTC development pipeline: Woda Cooper's track record in Ohio means this project is likely to deliver on schedule and on budget — a meaningful distinction in a development environment where cost and supply chain pressures have stalled projects elsewhere.

## The Near East Side Context

The intersection of Cleveland Avenue and Long Street sits at the working edge of downtown Columbus. In recent years, this corridor has absorbed both forces that define the Near East Side's current moment: sustained community development investment and rising market-rate pressure from Columbus's ongoing residential boom.

Organizations like the Near East Area Commission and Homeport have worked for years to expand affordable supply in this neighborhood. Market-rate apartment towers have risen just blocks away along High Street. And land values have climbed steadily as developers and investors have turned their attention east of downtown.

Opportunity Pointe's LIHTC structure is significant in this context. Unlike market-rate affordable housing — which is affordable only until ownership changes or rents reset — LIHTC-financed units carry income restrictions that run for decades. This project is not just adding units; it is adding *durable* affordable units at a corner that has long mattered to Near East Side residents.

## The May 2026 Columbus Housing Context

Columbus's affordable housing gap is real, and the numbers from April 2026 make it stark.

The median Columbus home sale price in April hit **$346,500** — an 8.3% year-over-year increase, according to Columbus REALTORS®. The for-sale inventory has risen too, up 13.4% YoY to 5,027 homes, and homes are sitting longer (47 days on market vs. 43 in April 2025). But that inventory build-up reflects the market-rate and move-up segments, not entry-level homeownership.

Meanwhile, average Columbus apartment rents are flat to slightly negative — Zillow data shows roughly a 2% YoY dip, with average rents around $1,341–$1,495/month depending on the source. That may sound like relief for renters, but it means the gap between the most affordable rental housing and what the market delivers is still enormous.

Projects like Opportunity Pointe don't fully close that gap. At 166 units, it's one building cluster in one neighborhood. But LIHTC financing at scale — the kind of deals OHFA funds each cycle — is the most reliable mechanism Columbus has for adding sub-market-rate housing that stays affordable long-term. And this project is the real thing: income verification, deed restrictions, state agency oversight.

## Construction Roundup: What Else Is Happening Near East Side

Opportunity Pointe is not the only project moving in Columbus's affordable and workforce housing pipeline. A few others worth watching:

**Landmark on Scioto** (Lincoln Avenue Communities): A 321-unit affordable family housing development on a 44.5-acre site broke ground April 8, 2026. All units serve households at or below 60% AMI. Leasing begins summer 2026.

**Flores Development scattered-site homes**: Local firm Flores Development has started construction on the first of 27 scattered-site single-family homes on the Near East Side and West Side, priced $450,000–$700,000. A different market segment, but the same bet on Near East Side land appreciation.
