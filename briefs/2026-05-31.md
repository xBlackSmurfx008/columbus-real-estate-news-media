### 1. Columbus Landmark on Scioto: 321-Unit BTR Affordable Community Breaks Ground

- **What**: National affordable housing developer Lincoln Avenue Communities (LAC) broke ground in April 2026 on Landmark on Scioto, a 321-unit build-to-rent (BTR) affordable housing community on 44.5 acres in Columbus — the firm's first ground-up construction project in Ohio. The development will provide 2-, 3-, and 4-bedroom homes (one- and two-story structures) to residents earning up to 60% of area median income (~$53,000/yr for a family of 4 in Columbus). Amenities include a fitness center, pool, clubhouse, outdoor grilling areas, and open spaces. LAC will dedicate approximately 7 acres to the City of Columbus for a public park. Leasing expected to begin summer 2026; full completion projected for 2028. U.S. Representative Mike Carey (OH-15), OHFA Executive Director Bill Beagle, and OCCH President and CEO Catherine Cawthon attended the groundbreaking. Street address [needs source].
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Columbus has no BTR affordable housing product at this scale. Landmark on Scioto applies the build-to-rent model — purpose-built rental homes at neighborhood density — to income-restricted housing for the first time in Columbus. For families earning 60% AMI who are priced out of homeownership ($346,500 median) and underserved by standard LIHTC apartment buildings, this is a genuinely new product type entering the market. LAC's first Ohio entry signals that institutional affordable housing capital now views Columbus as a viable market for scaled BTR affordable development.

### 2. 103-Unit Supportive Housing Building Approved for Franklinton's West Town Street

- **What**: A 103-unit, five-story supportive housing building called the Whitechapel Development received East Franklinton Review Board (EFRB) approval in December 2025 for construction at 640 W. Town St. in Franklinton. The project will house formerly unsheltered individuals. Developers are Arch City Development and Bianchi Development Company. Support partners include YMCA of Central Ohio, Mid-Ohio Board for an Independent Living Environment (MOBILE), Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority, Salvation Army, Community Shelter Board, and HEER to Serve. The project also renovates an existing single-family home at Chapel and Grubb Streets into a duplex. Construction start was targeted for spring 2026.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Franklinton's pipeline has been dominated by market-rate residential and arts/entertainment uses. A 103-unit supportive housing building at 640 W. Town St. represents the neighborhood's service infrastructure catching up with its residential investment. The coalition behind this project — CMHA, Community Shelter Board, Salvation Army — signals institutional backing that gives this project staying power. Columbus operators watching Franklinton's evolution should track how supportive and market-rate housing coexist in what is becoming a model mixed-use neighborhood.

Columbus just got something it's never had before.

In April 2026, Lincoln Avenue Communities (LAC) broke ground on Landmark on Scioto — a 321-unit, 44.5-acre build-to-rent affordable housing community for families earning up to 60% of area median income.

Not an apartment complex. Not a stacked tower. One- and two-story homes in a neighborhood layout — with 2-, 3-, and 4-bedroom floor plans designed specifically for the families Columbus's affordable housing stock has consistently underserved.

A few things worth noting for Columbus CRE operators and housing policy watchers:

**The BTR affordable model is new here.** Columbus has market-rate build-to-rent. It has LIHTC apartment buildings. Landmark on Scioto is the first project to combine the neighborhood feel of BTR with the income restrictions of affordable housing at this scale. 321 units. 44.5 acres.

**Who qualifies?** Residents earning up to 60% AMI — roughly $37,000–$53,000 per year depending on household size. These are working Columbus families who can't afford the $346,500 median home price but don't qualify for the deepest rental subsidies. The 2/3/4-bedroom layout is intentional: this is family-sized housing.

**National capital is paying attention.** LAC is a national developer entering Ohio for the first time through this project. You don't pick a state for your first ground-up project unless the fundamentals are compelling. Columbus's persistent supply shortfall — 10,474 homes permitted in 2024 versus an estimated 19,000 needed annually — made this market viable.

**7 acres is becoming a park.** LAC is donating 7 of the 44.5 acres to the City of Columbus for a public park. Community anchor beyond the housing itself.

U.S. Rep. Mike Carey, OHFA Executive Director Bill Beagle, and OCCH CEO Catherine Cawthon were on site for the groundbreaking. When federal, state, and intermediary capital all show up for your shovel ceremony, that's a signal.
