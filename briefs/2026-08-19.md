## Social posts

### LinkedIn

Columbus is turning surface parking into housing on a significant scale.

On June 29, city council approved an economic development agreement clearing the way for Pizzuti Companies and Encova Insurance to redevelop 47 surface lots in the Discovery District into roughly 518 apartments and 15,000 square feet of retail — a $96 million phased project.

The city isn't just a bystander here. Columbus is committing $1.65 million from its 2026 capital budget for street design work on Oak Street (between Ninth and Lester), with future years expected to fund full construction and a new 575-space public parking garage that would replace the surface parking being removed.

Why this matters:

The Discovery District sits between COSI, the Columbus Museum of Art campus, and Nationwide Children's Hospital — but that stretch of downtown has historically been among its least activated, defined more by surface lots and fencing than street life. Pizzuti, who built the Library Park apartments in phases along the district's western edge, is the same developer here, which matters: they've already shown they can execute phased projects in this corridor to completion.

At 518 units with 15,000 SF of retail (about 29 square feet per apartment), this is neighborhood-service retail — cafés, dry cleaners, pharmacy — not a destination draw. But the permanent residential population it would introduce is what makes those uses viable.

The city's willingness to fund streets and a parking garage is also the most important detail in the deal. Projects like this stall in the design phase all the time, often because city infrastructure doesn't follow. Here it's committed.

Full buildout across multiple phases will take years — likely a decade or more. But the June 29 agreement put the project on the public record, and the infrastructure commitment is real. 

Worth watching.
