# Columbus Livingston Ave: 170-Unit Mixed-Use Breaks Ground

Demolition started this week at 475 E. Livingston Avenue. Five vacant buildings are coming down on a 1.5-acre site between Ninth Street and Washington Avenue, cleared for a five-story mixed-use development that will bring approximately 170 apartments and 19,000 square feet of ground-floor retail to one of Columbus's most active development corridors.

The project — from Northstar Realty LLC and Schiff Capital Group — sits directly across the street from the Nationwide Children's Hospital campus. It has been in the pipeline since 2018. This week's demolition is the first physical milestone after eight years of proposals, rezoning, and regulatory approvals.

---

## Eight Years From Proposal to Dirt

Columbus real estate moves at a particular pace, and the Livingston Avenue project is a case study in pre-reform entitlement timelines.

Northstar Realty first brought the concept to the neighborhood in 2018, proposing a mixed-use building to replace surface parking and a cluster of aging one- and two-story commercial buildings along the south side of Livingston Avenue. The site, at 475 E. Livingston, had long been underutilized relative to its location — walking distance from Nationwide Children's Hospital's main campus, on a major east-west corridor, and adjacent to the German Village and Merion Village neighborhoods to the south.

Columbus City Council approved rezoning for the site in 2022. In 2024, Schiff Capital Group joined Northstar as a development partner, and the project returned to the Board of Zoning Adjustment for a design modification — approval of a planned pickup window for a first-floor retail tenant. The BZA signed off.

Demolition is now underway. All five buildings on the 1.5-acre site are being cleared this week. Construction on the five-story mixed-use building is expected to follow.

For Columbus developers and investors currently underwriting projects through Columbus's Zone In code ([Columbus Zone In Phase 2: what the commercial and industrial rezoning means for operators](/blog/columbus-zone-in-phase-2-commercial-industrial-rezoning)), this timeline — eight years from proposal to dirt — is a useful baseline for what pre-Zone In entitlement actually cost in time and carrying costs. Future projects on major Columbus corridors where 12-story by-right heights are now allowed may move materially faster.

---

## What's Being Built: Apartments, Retail, and a Ground Floor Designed for a Changing Corridor

The completed building will be five stories. The upper four floors will hold approximately 170 residential units; unit mix has not been publicly disclosed [needs source]. The ground floor combines approximately 19,000 square feet of retail space with building service uses.

The site runs along the south side of Livingston Avenue between Ninth Street and Washington Avenue — a stretch that faces the Nationwide Children's Hospital campus directly across the street.

Project cost has been reported at approximately $45 million in one legal publication; that figure has not been independently confirmed and should be treated as a working estimate until the development team provides public detail.

No project opening timeline has been publicly stated. Given the project's regulatory history and the scale of demolition underway, a 2027 or 2028 delivery is plausible based on comparable Columbus mixed-use timelines, though this is not confirmed.

## The Developers: Northstar Realty and Schiff Capital Group

**Northstar Realty LLC** is a Columbus-based real estate company with a track record of development and management in central Ohio urban corridors. The firm's address of record is 387 E. Livingston Ave. — one block from this project site — suggesting deep familiarity with the specific submarket.

**Schiff Capital Group** is one of Columbus's most active development firms in urban mixed-use corridors. The firm has navigated complex approval processes across multiple Columbus neighborhoods. Their involvement in the Livingston Avenue project adds execution credibility; Schiff Capital has the institutional capacity to manage a multi-phase entitlement process and close financing on a project of this scale.

Together, the two firms represent a model becoming more common in Columbus mixed-use development: a local operator with deep site knowledge paired with a capital-strong development partner capable of carrying multi-year entitlement timelines.

---

## The Nationwide Children's Hospital Effect on Livingston Avenue

The location of this project — directly across Livingston Avenue from Nationwide Children's Hospital — is not incidental. It's the primary demand driver.

Nationwide Children's Hospital is one of Columbus's largest employers and one of the nation's top pediatric medical centers. Its ongoing campus expansion has been one of the most significant anchors of Near East Side real estate activity over the past decade. Hospital employees, medical trainees, and visiting families create durable, non-cyclical demand for housing and services adjacent to campus.

The Livingston Avenue corridor south of the hospital has long been targeted for densification — close to the medical campus, connected to German Village and Merion Village, and with direct access to downtown via Livingston. The Northstar/Schiff project will be the largest new residential project to physically break ground in that corridor in recent years.

For operators tracking Columbus [apartment market data](/blog/columbus-apartment-rents-drop-2026-what-data-misses): hospital-adjacent submarkets have historically shown tighter vacancy and stronger rent performance than the Columbus market average, because tenant demand is driven by employment rather than purely by market conditions. Worth underwriting separately from the broader Columbus multifamily picture.

## What This Means for Columbus Operators and Investors

**If you own or operate in the Livingston Avenue corridor:** The arrival of 170 new units and 19,000 sq ft of retail signals accelerating investment in this submarket. Ground-floor retail demand for medical-service and food-and-beverage tenants on the corridor is likely to increase as the project moves toward delivery.

**If you're underwriting new Columbus mixed-use:** The eight-year timeline on this deal is your comp for what pre-Zone In entitlement cost. Projects on eligible corridors under the updated Zone In code should be modeled with meaningfully shorter entitlement windows — with the caveat that design review and neighborhood engagement remain real timelines even under by-right frameworks.

**If you're tracking Columbus supply additions:** 170 units is a meaningful addition to the Near East Side submarket, which has received significant affordable supply (Opportunity Pointe's 166 LIHTC units on Cleveland Ave, [Kingsley and Company's 65-unit OHFA-funded project on E. Main St.](/blog/columbus-near-east-side-166-affordable-units-opportunity-pointe)), and now market-rate or mixed inventory at Livingston. The corridor is receiving coordinated investment across income levels.

Columbus's [April 2026 housing market report](/blog/columbus-april-2026-housing-market-median-price) showed a median sale price of $346,500 — up 8.3% year over year — with inventory up 13.4% but still well below the levels needed to dampen price appreciation. More supply at all price points, across more Columbus corridors, is the only structural solution.

The Livingston Avenue project is one piece of that solution. It broke ground this week.
