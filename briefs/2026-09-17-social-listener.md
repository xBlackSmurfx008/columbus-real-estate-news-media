#### 4. Our City Our Say / Issue 8 — STEADY | Sentiment: SUPPORTIVE (coalition) / CAUTIOUS (city hall)
**Two competing Nov 3 ballot measures confirmed; grassroots vs. city-hall versions**

The compromise between the Our City Our Say coalition and Columbus City Council failed. Both a grassroots version and a City Council-authored version of district-based election reform may appear on the November 3, 2026 ballot. The grassroots coalition submitted 23,000 signatures (July 16). NALC Buckeye Branch 78 endorsed Issue 8 on September 12. The coalition includes Columbus Education Association, Columbus Stand Up, and Indivisible Central Ohio.

Real estate relevance: If district-only elections pass, political power shifts toward residential neighborhood coalitions and away from citywide developer-aligned majorities — directly reshaping zoning vote dynamics and development approval outcomes for years. Zone In Phase 2 and Issue 8 could interact if the council vote happens before or after the election.

**Frame: OPPORTUNITY — pre-election explainer is a content gap; no Columbus real estate outlet has framed the governance stakes for the investor/broker audience.**

#### 5. Construction cost escalation — STEADY | Sentiment: FRUSTRATED (developers) / CONCERNED (buyers)
**NEW framing since prior briefs — JLL report circulating**

Columbus Underground published a report citing JLL data: construction costs in Central Ohio are up 5% since the start of the year and projected to rise another 8%. Labor remains the most volatile input — skilled trades (electricians, plumbers, HVAC) in high demand. Modular and prefab components are a documented developer response. Cost-per-square-foot is 50%+ higher than 2016.

The cost pressure connects directly to the affordable housing gap: every point of cost escalation widens the gap between market-rate delivery costs and what affordable rents can support, tightening the LIHTC underwriting window.

**Frame: CONTENT OPPORTUNITY — a local explainer on what rising construction costs mean for Columbus rents, development timelines, and affordable housing capacity would be high-value for developer, investor, and policy readers.**
