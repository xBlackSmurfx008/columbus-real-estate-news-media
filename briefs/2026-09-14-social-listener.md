#### 1. "Our City Our Say" Issue 8 vs. City Council's competing measure — dueling November ballot fight — LOUD | FRUSTRATED/DIVIDED | **CONTENT OPPORTUNITY (NEW)**

The most active new signal today. The "Our City Our Say" (OCOS) coalition submitted 23,000+ signatures on July 16, 2026, placing Issue 8 on the November 2026 general election ballot — a charter amendment to switch Columbus's nine council seats from at-large voting to true district elections (only residents of each district vote for that district's council member). City Council responded by placing its own competing hybrid amendment on the same ballot: a 13-seat model with 9 district seats and 4 at-large seats. Two dueling measures will appear side-by-side in November.

The Far East Area Commission voted September 1, 2026 to formally endorse Issue 8. Columbus DSA publicly accused City Council of attempting to "co-opt" the OCOS initiative by proposing a diluted hybrid alternative. The NALC Buckeye Branch 78 (postal workers union, Columbus) posted an active OCOS endorsement on September 12, 2026.

This story connects directly to the Zone In Phase 2 conflict (same underlying dynamic: do neighborhood-level institutions or citywide bodies control growth decisions?) and to the $500M affordable housing bond accountability question (which council members represent which neighborhoods?). The reform question goes to November — real estate investors, residents, and neighborhood groups all have skin in the outcome.

**Signal sources:** WOSU Public Media (3 articles), Columbus DSA, NALC Buckeye Branch 78, Far East Area Commission (fareastac.org), Free Press Columbus.

**Volume basis:** Loud — 5+ distinct organizations actively publishing or endorsing on Issue 8 in the last 30 days, with fresh signal in the last 7 days from area commissions and labor unions.

#### 5. Construction costs rising — developer impact story — LIGHT | CAUTIOUS | **NEW SIGNAL**

Columbus Underground published a report on rising construction costs in Central Ohio: up 5% since January 2026, projected to reach 8% by year-end. Primary drivers: construction materials and a highly competitive skilled-trades labor market. Local labor rates for electricians, plumbers, and HVAC technicians range $38–$58/hr. Central Ohio is outpacing national averages (3.5–4.5% YoY nationally vs. higher locally). A separate WOSU article notes Ohio's 2026 construction season is "breaking records."
