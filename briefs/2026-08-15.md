### 1. Columbus Housing Inventory Up 7%, Market Rebalancing (August 2026)
**Coverage signal:** Cover today ✓
**Confidence:** 4/5
**What:** Central Ohio housing inventory rose roughly 7% year-over-year in August 2026, reaching approximately 2.2 months of supply — the highest level since early 2020. Despite more choice for buyers, prices have not buckled: the regional median is above $335,000, homes are closing at 98.5% of asking price, and the Franklin County median reached $352,000 in June (+0.6% YoY). Columbus's June closed sales rose 7.8% YoY even as national existing-home sales fell 1.7% in July per NAR, reflecting the region's relative affordability advantage (Franklin County median is ~19% below the national $434,100 figure).
**Why it matters:** Operators, brokers, buyers, and sellers in the Columbus market are navigating the first meaningful supply increase in years. The 7.8% Columbus sales increase against national softness is a story of local divergence that serves all four CREN audience segments. Citywide scope — no neighborhood slot consumed.

### LinkedIn (1,200–2,200 chars)

**Columbus's Housing Market Is Shifting. Here's What the Numbers Actually Say.**

Central Ohio's housing inventory climbed roughly 7% year-over-year in August 2026 — the most breathing room buyers have seen since early 2020. The local supply gauge now sits at about 2.2 months.

That sounds like a lot. It's not.

To put it in context: a "balanced" market — where neither buyers nor sellers have a clear edge — is typically defined as four to six months of supply. At 2.2 months, Columbus is still firmly in seller's market territory. Sellers are still closing deals at 98.5% of asking price. The Franklin County median sale price reached $352,000 in June (Columbus REALTORS® data), up from $349,000 a year earlier.

What has changed is the cost of getting the price wrong. Overpricing by 5–10% — a strategy that worked in 2022 because buyer desperation covered the gap — is now a real risk. Buyers have alternatives. Listings that sit past 30 days attract questions that are hard to answer later.

Meanwhile, Columbus is doing something the national market isn't: growing. Nationally, existing-home sales fell 1.7% in July per NAR. Columbus's June closed sales rose 7.8% year-over-year. The region's relative affordability — Franklin County's $352K median is roughly 19% below the national $434K figure — continues to attract buyers that higher-cost markets are losing.

The bottom line: this is a rebalancing, not a correction. For buyers, it means a decision window measured in days, not hours. For sellers, it means pricing right the first time matters more than it has in years.
