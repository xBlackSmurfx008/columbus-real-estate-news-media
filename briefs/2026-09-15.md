The Columbus Downtown Commission is scheduled to vote September 22 on the Roman Catholic Diocese of Columbus's application to demolish its 1908 five-story building at 197 E. Gay St. — and replace it with surface parking.

This is one of the more significant historic-preservation questions downtown Columbus will face in 2026.

The building, constructed in 1908, currently houses the Diocese's administrative offices, Catholic Social Services, and the Our Lady of Guadalupe Center. It sits directly behind Saint Joseph Cathedral — one of Columbus's most architecturally prominent buildings — and next to the Columbus Athenaeum.

The Diocese filed a Certificate of Appropriateness with the city's Planning Division. Columbus Underground reported that an unsafe order was placed on the building following a brick wall collapse earlier this year.

The Downtown Commission has binding authority over these decisions. In August 2026, it denied a similar demolition-to-parking application at 195 S. Washington Ave. without an approved replacement plan. The Diocese's application includes that unsafe order context — but whether it moves the commission remains the open question.

Community opposition is already on the record. Columbus Underground published an opinion piece arguing the Diocese has the financial means to rehabilitate rather than demolish.

For Columbus real estate professionals, this is a signal case: it will show how the commission weighs a documented safety issue against the district's strong preference for development over surface parking. The outcome sets precedent for other aging structures in the historic district.
