## Lead 1 — Bayer Announces $2.2 Billion Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Campus in New Albany

**Summary:** Bayer announced on October 2, 2026 that it will invest $2.2 billion in a new pharmaceutical manufacturing campus at the New Albany International Business Park, creating approximately 600 permanent jobs and roughly 1,500 construction positions. The announcement adds to a growing cluster of major employers — including Intel's semiconductor investments — that have been driving rapid economic and housing demand growth in the New Albany–east Columbus corridor. The facility is planned in two phases, with the first drug-substance manufacturing module expected online in 2031 and a second drug-product module in 2034.

**Key details:**
- Investment: $2.2 billion pharmaceutical manufacturing campus.
- Location: New Albany International Business Park, New Albany, Ohio (northeast Columbus metro).
- Jobs: ~600 permanent positions, ~1,500 construction jobs.
- Timeline: First module operational 2031; second module 2034.
- Focus: Oncology, cardiovascular, and renal care drug substance and drug product manufacturing; flexible modular design with advanced automation.
- Supporting infrastructure: Ohio Life Science Training Center (opening summer 2027), backed by over $30 million from JobsOhio, the City of New Albany, and The New Albany Company, will train biomanufacturing operators and technicians.
- Incentives: Bayer will pursue Ohio Job Creation Tax Credits and JobsOhio assistance; final terms to be disclosed after agreement execution.
- Existing ecosystem: New Albany International Business Park already hosts Amgen, Hims & Hers, American Regent, and Pharmavite.
- Governor DeWine stated the project "firmly solidifies Ohio's place among global biomanufacturing leaders."

Market observers have attributed much of the east-side demand spike to the "Intel Effect" from the broader Silicon Heartland semiconductor investment. This Bayer announcement represents another large-employer anchor in the same corridor; the housing demand and workforce housing implications — particularly for the 1,500 construction workers and 600 permanent employees — are a reporting angle not addressed in the news coverage found.
