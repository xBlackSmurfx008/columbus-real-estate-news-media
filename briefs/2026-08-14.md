### LinkedIn (announcement)

**Mercy on Main: A Former NFL Player Is Building 65 Affordable Apartments in Olde Towne East**

A Cincinnati developer with deep central Ohio ties is proposing a five-story, 65-unit affordable housing building at 907 East Main Street in Columbus's Olde Towne East — one of the city's most watched neighborhoods for gentrification pressure.

The developer is Kingsley + Co., founded by Chinedum Ndukwe — a Notre Dame alum who played safety for the Cincinnati Bengals and Oakland Raiders before pivoting to real estate development. The project is named Mercy on Main, after Ndukwe's grandmother Mercy Okoronkwo.

**By the numbers:**
- 65 units (8 studios, 29 one-bedrooms, 18 two-bedrooms, 10 three-bedrooms)
- All income-restricted: 7 units at ≤30% AMI, 10 at ≤50%, 25 at ≤60%, 23 at ≤70%
- $22.8M total development cost (~$350,800/unit)
- ~$14.7M from Ohio's 9% Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (OHFA, May 2026) — the most selective affordable housing finance tool in the state
- $3.25M in city of Columbus HOME funds
- City planning review: August 20

The 9% LIHTC award is significant. Ohio's Housing Finance Agency distributed $39.1M in credits to 25 projects statewide in May — each award is the result of competitive scoring. Landing one for a five-story building in an east-side Columbus neighborhood shows the financing is real and committed.

The path has not been friction-free. Columbus Underground describes the project as "contentious," which typically reflects organized neighborhood opposition at the commission level. The August 20 city review will be the first major public airing of those concerns.

For Columbus operators watching the affordable housing pipeline: this is the kind of project that shows where tax credit money is flowing in 2026 — toward inner-ring neighborhoods with established transit and walkable retail, not exurban greenfields.
