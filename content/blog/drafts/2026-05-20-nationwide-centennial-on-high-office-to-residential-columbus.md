# Nationwide's Downtown Columbus Tower Becomes 148 Apartments

One of downtown Columbus's most recognizable office towers is getting a new identity — and a new purpose on most of its floors.

Nationwide Realty Investors has announced it will convert Two Nationwide Plaza — the 18-story tower at 280 N. High St. — into a mixed-use building called The Centennial on High. The project will add 148 apartments across 15 of the building's floors, while preserving approximately 75,000 square feet of office space to keep existing tenants in place. The Downtown Commission unanimously approved the conversion. First residents are expected to move in by mid-2027.

The name is deliberate. 2026 marks Nationwide's 100th year in business, and the company is using the conversion of its own former headquarters tower to mark the occasion — a rare piece of civic storytelling built into a real estate project.

---

## What the Building Is

280 N. High St. was completed in 1982 as Two Nationwide Plaza, one of three office towers developed by Nationwide in the heart of downtown Columbus. The building has housed law firms, trade associations, and financial services tenants for more than four decades. It sits mid-block on High Street between Broad and Gay Streets — one of the most active pedestrian corridors in Columbus, directly across from Columbus City Hall.

Unlike many suburban office conversions, this is a core urban asset. The High Street address and its proximity to the Short North, the Arena District, and Columbus State Community College place it in the center of downtown demand — which makes the economics of adding residential meaningfully different from converting a half-vacant suburban office park. ## The Conversion Plan

Nationwide Realty Investors' plan keeps the building in mixed-use operation throughout the conversion process. The residential floors — 15 of the tower's 18 stories, including a newly created penthouse level — will hold 148 apartments ranging from one to three bedrooms. Each residential floor is configured for five units, and each unit will include a private balcony.

The remaining floors are being retained as office space, maintaining approximately 75,000 square feet of commercial occupancy. Two anchor office tenants — law firm Carpenter Lipps LLP and the Ohio Farm Bureau — have committed to staying in the building through and after the conversion.

The building's amenity program features a fitness center, club room and lounge, and a signature outdoor courtyard: a sunken space along High Street positioned roughly 15 feet below sidewalk grade, incorporating a small pool. The below-grade courtyard creates a protected outdoor amenity on one of Columbus's most trafficked pedestrian streets without consuming rooftop or street-level space.

The Downtown Commission's unanimous approval clears the primary local design review hurdle. Construction financing terms and the project's general contractor have not been publicly disclosed. [needs source]

## Why Now — and Why This Building

The timing of this conversion is not accidental. Downtown Columbus's office market is improving — vacancy dropped a full percentage point from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026, one of the largest quarterly improvements among major U.S. metros, according to Axios Columbus — but the improvement is being driven by tenants consolidating into the best buildings, not by net new demand. A 1982-vintage mid-rise competing for tenants against newer Class A product faces a different leasing dynamic than it did a decade ago.

The partial-conversion model addresses that directly: Nationwide keeps revenue from anchor tenants who value the High Street address and building quality, while converting the floors where renewal risk is highest into residential units with a longer demand runway. It is a risk management play as much as a development bet.

The downtown residential market also offers a more favorable entry point right now than the broader multifamily market. While Greater Columbus multifamily vacancy reached a record high of 10.6% in Q4 2025, a conversion at 280 N. High — delivering 148 units to the top of the building rather than as ground-up construction — carries a different cost basis and absorption profile than a new development requiring full lease-up from zero. The building's existing infrastructure — MEP systems, elevator cores, parking — means conversion economics are more predictable than a ground-up project. ## A Model for Downtown Columbus Landlords

The Centennial on High is the most prominent test yet of the partial-conversion model in downtown Columbus — and the industry will watch it closely.

Full office-to-residential conversions require a building to be entirely vacated, which is both operationally difficult and financially risky during the conversion period. Partial conversions — keeping income-producing office tenants on lower floors while converting upper floors to residential — solve both problems but require design creativity, especially around shared circulation, mechanical systems, and building code compliance for dual-use occupancy.

What Nationwide Realty Investors is doing at 280 N. High is exactly what other downtown Columbus landlords with partially-occupied towers have been watching for: a proof of concept that a major operator can successfully navigate the partial-conversion design and approval process. If the Centennial leases well through 2027 and the office tenants remain stable, it will establish a replicable template.

Columbus's office market has roughly a dozen mid-rise downtown towers built between 1975 and 2000 that face the same generational obsolescence pressures making the Centennial's conversion story compelling. Not all of them will pursue residential conversion — location, floor plate geometry, parking ratios, and existing tenant obligations all factor in — but the Centennial gives the market a live benchmark. [→ *Internal link: CRE News May 6 Pointe at Polaris suburban office deal — for the contrast with suburban vs downtown office performance*]

## What to Watch Next

The path from Downtown Commission approval to first residents involves several visible milestones:

**Building permits**: NRI will need to file for permits with Columbus Building and Zoning Services. The permit filing, once visible in the city's public database, will confirm the construction timeline and likely reveal the GC.

**Construction activity**: Renovation work on an occupied high-rise is visible. Changes to the High Street facade — particularly the sunken courtyard construction — will be among the earliest physical indicators of the project moving forward.

**Pre-leasing**: Residential pre-leasing typically begins 6–9 months before delivery. A mid-2027 delivery means pre-leasing could start as early as fall 2026. The rental rate at which NRI prices these units will be a bellwether for downtown Columbus apartment demand in the post-supply-surge period.

**Office lease renewals**: How Carpenter Lipps and Ohio Farm Bureau's leases are structured through and after the conversion will influence the building's NOI profile — and whether other downtown landlords see the partial-conversion model as financially viable.

CRE News will track building permit filings and pre-leasing announcements as they enter the public record.
