Three big investors just started building a nearly 1-million-square-foot warehouse in Columbus.

Trident Capital Group and O'Connor Capital Partners announced the closing of a joint venture with Clarion Partners and broke ground on Phase III of the Rickenbacker Industrial Center. The new building is at 1669 Rohr Road on Columbus's south side. It will cover 959,579 square feet when complete, according to [Connect CRE](https://www.connectcre.com/stories/960k-sf-speculative-industrial-development-in-columbus-breaks-ground/).

Delivery is targeted for the first quarter of 2027.

## What They Are Building

The building is spec industrial. That means no tenant has signed a lease before construction started. The developers believe Columbus's logistics market will fill the space before the paint dries.

Spec construction at this scale is a confidence bet. The investors are putting capital on the line based on their read of Columbus demand — not a signed lease agreement.

The specs are designed for modern big-box users:

- 40-foot clear heights (standard for e-commerce fulfillment and distribution)
- 104 dock doors
- 238 trailer parking stalls
- 336 car parking spaces

The 57.30-acre site sits within a designated Qualified Opportunity Zone, which offers capital gains tax benefits to investors. The project also carries a 15-year, 75% property tax abatement, according to [Connect CRE](https://www.connectcre.com/stories/960k-sf-speculative-industrial-development-in-columbus-breaks-ground/). Those two incentives reduce the cost of holding the building during lease-up.

JLL's Dan Wendorf, Brian Marsh, and Joseph Davis are handling leasing and marketing the space to tenants.

## Part of a Larger Campus

Phase III is not a standalone bet. It is the third piece of the Rickenbacker Industrial Center, a 230-acre master-planned industrial complex, according to [O'Connor Capital Partners](https://oconnorcp.com/properties/rickenbacker-industrial-center/).

O'Connor and Trident have been building at Rickenbacker for several years. Phase I completed construction on a 1.06-million-square-foot building. Phase II followed. Phase III — at 959,579 square feet — continues the campus build-out with a new equity partner, Clarion Partners.

Clarion Partners is a core real estate investment manager. Core capital typically comes from pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, and insurance companies. It is patient capital that prioritizes stable, long-term returns over rapid appreciation.

When core-fund money backs a spec industrial project, it signals something important: institutional investors see Columbus industrial as a durable, not just a fast-moving, opportunity.

## Why Rickenbacker

Rickenbacker International Airport is the only all-cargo international airport in Ohio. That single fact has shaped the corridor for decades.

The airport provides direct air freight access to global markets. The south Columbus location puts tenants within reach of I-270, US-33, and the broader central Ohio highway network. Companies that need to move goods fast — e-commerce operators, pharmaceutical distributors, electronics suppliers — have been drawn to Rickenbacker for years.

Major tenants at Rickenbacker include Amazon, Target, and a range of national third-party logistics firms. The industrial vacancy rate in the Columbus metro remains tight relative to Sun Belt markets. [Columbus Real Estate News previously reported](/blog/columbus-new-albany-spec-industrial-fully-leased-2026) on spec industrial in New Albany being fully leased in mid-2026 — a signal that Columbus industrial demand has been absorbing supply as fast as it arrives.

## What to Watch

JLL's leasing team will begin marketing the space immediately. Q1 2027 delivery gives prospective tenants roughly five months to evaluate the site and negotiate terms.

The building's size — nearly 1 million square feet — means it could be leased to a single large occupier or divided among multiple tenants. At 40-foot clear heights with 104 dock doors, the target profile is e-commerce fulfillment, consumer goods distribution, or industrial manufacturing with a significant logistics component.

For Columbus operators, the Phase III groundbreaking is the latest in a string of institutional moves into the metro's south-side logistics corridor. When developers build spec at scale and institutional capital signs on before delivery, it reflects a consensus read on the market: Columbus industrial demand is real, and it is not slowing down.
