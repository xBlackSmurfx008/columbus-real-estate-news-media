### LinkedIn (posted when article goes live)

The YMCA of Central Ohio has agreed to sell the 9-acre Jerry L. Garver branch site at 1827 Livingston Ave. to Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority after closing the facility this past May — and CMHA plans to replace it with 216 affordable apartments for southeast Columbus.

Here's what the proposal looks like so far:

📐 **The project:** Nine three-story walk-up apartment buildings totaling 216 units, plus a clubhouse and pool. At ~24 units per acre across 9 acres, the density is moderate — a garden-apartment scale that fits the surrounding Livingston Avenue corridor.

💰 **Who it serves:** The 70–76% AMI band is workforce housing — not deeply subsidized, but well below Columbus's rising market rents. Think essential workers, teachers, and nurses who earn too much for Section 8 but can't compete in today's rental market.

📅 **Timeline:** CMHA hasn't announced a development schedule or filed plans. Watch for a Franklin County deed transfer and a city zoning application as the first public milestones. A rezoning from institutional to residential use will likely be required.

🏗️ **Context:** CMHA is executing one of its most active development slates in recent memory — Cobblestone Manor in Grove City (82 senior units, opened April 2026), AspireCOLUMBUS downtown (82 units), The Falls (220 units), and 27 scattered-site homes across the Near East and West Sides. Garver would add a significant cluster to the southeast corridor.

Southeast Columbus has seen less affordable housing investment than the Near East or West Sides. If this project advances, it puts 216 workforce units in a neighborhood that lost its only full-service YMCA last spring.
