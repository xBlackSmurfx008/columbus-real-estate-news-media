## NEW LEAD 1 — DOJ/RealPage Rent-Fixing Case: Columbus-Area Landlord Struck Deal
**Volume: MODERATE | Distinct sources: 3+ (CW Columbus, CBS News, Hoodline)| Sentiment: MIXED (tenant advocates: outraged; landlord industry: defensive)**
**Status: NEW — not in prior listener briefs**

CW Columbus published an article headlined "Columbus landlord strikes deal with DOJ over alleged rent-fixing scheme," reporting that Cortland Management — which operates 14 apartment communities in the Columbus metro area, including properties in Sunbury, Reynoldsburg, and Blacklick — agreed to cooperate with the U.S. Department of Justice in its amended RealPage lawsuit. The DOJ suit alleges that major national landlords used RealPage's algorithmic pricing software to share nonpublic competitor data and coordinate rent increases across markets.

**Source:** CW Columbus — "Columbus landlord strikes deal with DOJ over alleged rent-fixing scheme" ([cwcolumbus.com](https://cwcolumbus.com/news/local/columbus-ohio-department-of-justice-landlords-face-doj-lawsuit-over-alleged-rent-fixing-scheme-attorney-general-yost)); CBS News — "U.S. Justice Department sues RealPage, alleging it enabled price-fixing on rents" ([cbsnews.com](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/realpage-lawsuit-price-fixing/)); Yahoo News — "Justice Department sues landlords operating in Ohio for collaborating to keep rents high" ([yahoo.com](https://www.yahoo.com/news/justice-department-sues-landlords-operating-120000626.html))

**Verification limits:** The CW Columbus article confirmed the Columbus-area Cortland communities but did not confirm how many units or which specific complexes are involved. Whether the DOJ cooperation agreement affects current lease pricing at those 14 properties is not confirmed. Cortland's settlement terms (cease using competitors' data, provide records, testify in civil litigation) are from the DOJ filing but should be cross-checked against the court document.

**Suggested reporting question:** "Which Cortland Management complexes near Columbus are covered by the DOJ deal, and are current Columbus-area renters owed anything under the settlement?" This connects to the rental registry conversation (CAA litigation) already active in this market and could be a strong stand-alone story with a local harm angle.
