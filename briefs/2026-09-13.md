## Social posts (pending human approval before publication)

### LinkedIn (1,200–2,200 chars)

Columbus is getting its first large-scale affordable assisted living community — and it's in Northland.

Chicago-based Evergreen Real Estate Group broke ground in late August on Green Oaks of Walnut Creek at 4000 Westerville Road. When it opens in fall 2027, it will offer 120 units — 67 studios and 53 one-bedrooms — designed for seniors across a range of income levels, including residents who qualify for Ohio Medicaid.

Most assisted living facilities in Ohio are private-pay. Monthly costs typically run $3,500–$6,000 or more — beyond Medicaid reimbursement rates and beyond what most working-class senior Ohioans can sustain on fixed incomes. This project is financed differently: Ohio Housing Finance Agency bonds (up to $39 million) and a 4% Low-Income Housing Tax Credit allocation, structures that allow below-market rents while supporting a care model.

The Douglas Company (Holland) holds a $22 million construction contract. WJW Architects (Chicago) designed the building. Completion is targeted for November 2027.

Green Oaks of Walnut Creek is one of three Evergreen communities that broke ground simultaneously — the others are in Grove City and near Fairborn. Developers and OHFA describe the trio as among the first purpose-built affordable assisted living projects constructed in Ohio, which says something about how underserved this category has been in a state with a rapidly aging population.

Two things to watch: the facility's operator (not yet publicly named) and Ohio Department of Medicaid enrollment confirmation. In purpose-built affordable assisted living, the operator determines whether the model actually works for residents.

For Columbus investors and housing advocates, this tests whether LIHTC and bond tools can scale to the regulatory complexity of assisted living at the Columbus metro level.
