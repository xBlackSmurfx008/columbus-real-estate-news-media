## 2. Source and image-policy compliance

**Sourcing discipline was strong.** Across the month's briefs: multiple duplicate drafts were correctly
caught and discarded before publish (Franklin County property-tax explainer, Discovery District $96M,
Merchant Building/North Market Tower, Mercy on Main ×2, Let Me Roll It, Rickenbacker Phase III); a
fabricated "prior coverage" internal link was caught and corrected on the Aug 23 Zone In Phase 2 draft
before it shipped; an OHFA "financing approved" secondary-source claim (Barthman Family Homes, Aug 22)
was independently checked against the primary OHFA document, found to be a draft application rather than
an approval, and correctly held as a monitor item instead of published; and every Zillow/FRED market-data
refresh that hit a missing or 404'd upstream file explicitly left the field unchanged rather than
fabricating a value (documented this way on at least 8 separate days).

**Image-policy compliance is the month's clearest production gap.** Higgsfield (the image-generation
credential) returned `IMAGES_OFF`/unauthenticated on effectively every day sampled across the month —
**no live article published in August shipped with a real rights-cleared photograph.** This followed the
then-current "images never block publication" policy correctly (no stock placeholders, no paid
generation, no fake imagery were substituted), so it is a compliance pass on the no-fabrication rule but
a 0% real-image-coverage month. Layered on top of that: a broken-hero-image bug (image files committed to
the repo under `frontend/public/images/heroes/` but serving HTTP 404 on the live site) was first flagged
Aug 15 against ~17 live articles dated back to Aug 3, and was still unresolved for the Aug 18–20 cohort as
of the Aug 21 brief — a suspected Vercel deploy/CDN routing issue, not a missing-file issue, that was
never confirmed fixed within the August record.

**One governance/code gap surfaced Aug 23 and was not confirmed closed:** `publish-article.mjs` still
inserts `status: 'live'` directly, contradicting the owner's 2026-08-21 CLAUDE.md policy requiring a
human to inspect and approve before anything goes live (a separate script, `publish-batch.mjs`, does
match the documented policy). The session that found this worked around it by using `publish-batch.mjs`
instead, but flagged that running `publish-article.mjs` as currently written risks an unreviewed live
publish until a developer fixes the script or the documentation pointer.

## 3. Workflow reliability and held work

The single largest recurring failure, present in nearly every brief from **Aug 7 through Aug 31**:
**`DATABASE_URL` was absent from the routine's session environment on roughly half of August's daily
runs**, blocking `publish-article.mjs`, `recent-articles.mjs` (the duplicate guard), and
`refresh-market-data.mjs` at the point of the day's first session. A second, later same-day session
usually had DB access and recovered most of the backlog, but this two-session pattern cost editorial time
and created real duplicate-drafting risk — which materialized: the Mercy on Main story was independently
re-drafted by two different DB-blind sessions (Aug 27 and Aug 31) before being correctly recognized as
already-published. This gap was raised as an explicit owner ask in essentially every brief and every
weekly SEO report since Aug 7, and was still open at month's end (raised a fourth consecutive time in the
2026-09-04 CMO directive).

**A full site outage occurred Aug 23.** Mid-session, the production Neon database began rejecting all
queries with HTTP 402 ("data transfer quota exceeded"); the public site returned HTTP 200 but rendered no
articles ("Articles are being loaded. Check back shortly.") and all ~79 live article URLs 404'd. Root
cause, diagnosed same day: `getArticleBySlug()` never matched its intended fast path (article IDs carry a
date prefix the public slug doesn't), so nearly every article-page render fell through to a full
`SELECT *` scan including every live article's full body, and the homepage/index/topic/area pages made
the same mistake. A code fix (explicit column selection, corrected slug lookup) was committed the same
day to stop the same quota being re-burned, but **restoring service required raising the Neon plan — an
owner action outside this routine's authority — and no later August brief in the available record
confirms that plan increase happened.**

Other recurring, smaller reliability gaps documented across the month: the FRED mortgage-rate fetch fails
via plain Node `fetch`/`https` in this cloud environment (HTTP 503 from the proxy layer) on at least three
separate days, worked around ad hoc each time rather than fixed in the script; the Gmail connector
supports draft creation only, never sending, so every "email" step all month produced an unsent draft;
and Telegram alerting — the owner's stated preferred channel from roughly Aug 14/21 onward — was never
configured (`TELEGRAM_NOT_CONFIGURED` recurs through month-end), meaning both the DATABASE_URL gap and
the Aug 23 outage were caught by a human reading a brief after the fact, not by a proactive alert.

(`CREN_VERIFIED_AUDIENCE_AND_MEDIA_KIT_BASELINE_2026-08-29.md`), and a 50-name first-advertiser target
list (`FIRST_ADVERTISER_TARGET_LIST_2026-08-29.md`) covering apartment operators, developers, homebuilders,
lenders, and adjacent local services, with a published rate card ranging from a $99/month directory
listing to a $3,500 founding 90-day bundle (`sales/rate-card.md`).

**None of this has been executed yet.** `crm/prospects.md`, `crm/partners.md`, and `crm/outreach-log.md`
are all still empty as of this review — zero prospects logged, zero outreach attempts, zero partnerships.
The infrastructure and target list are ready; sales execution has not started.

Given the verified audience baseline (104 pageviews / 30 days, 1 real subscriber), the media kit's own
"sales accuracy rules" correctly caution against selling scale that doesn't exist yet. The realistic
near-term opportunity is not the $3,500 bundle but the lowest-cost, no-guarantee tier (a $99/month
directory listing, or a single article in-content block) with one or two of the already-planned prospects,
run as an honest pilot to produce one real case study before broader outreach. Several target-list
prospects (NRP Group, Crawford Hoying, CASTO, M/I Homes) are also active subjects of August's own news
coverage — any pilot outreach to them should route through a separate track from the newsroom to hold the
CLAUDE.md requirement that editorial coverage stay independent of sponsor relationships.

## 6. CREN property-inquiry opportunities (kept separate from sponsor/advertiser revenue)

CREN's own four lead-capture funnels — FSBO seller (`/sell/your-home`), investment-property seller
(`/sell/investment-property`), capital partner (`/invest/deploy-capital`), and renter
(`/rent/find-a-home`) — are live, and the Sept 4 audit found the copy itself strong ("you"-first,
concrete numbers, e.g. "roughly $18,000 that stays in your pocket" on the seller page). But **0 real
property inquiries have ever been generated, all-time**, as of the most recent verified figures available
to this review.

The routine's own diagnosis, cross-checked against the article template's source
(`frontend/app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`), is specific and verifiable: every article page's only conversion
surface is one generic, identical, topic-blind "Stay Informed" newsletter block — not a link to any of
the four funnels, and (per Section 4) built on a promise the site currently cannot keep. Readers of an
apartment-opening story are never routed to the renter funnel; readers of a market-report story are never
routed to the seller funnel. This is a distinct, editorially-neutral lever — it requires no sponsor
relationship or paid placement, only linking existing traffic to existing, already-approved copy — and is
the most concrete, low-risk experiment available for next month.
