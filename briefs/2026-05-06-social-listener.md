Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Negative / sobering**  
Frame: **Threat** — three distinct data points now converge into the clearest downtown office and residential absorption correction story in Central Ohio; first time a hard transaction price anchors the narrative

*Updated from May 3–5 briefs — KeyBank transaction is new to this run.*

The KeyBank Building at 88 E. Broad St., a 21-story downtown office tower, sold for $5.1 million at a two-day online auction. **2. Zone In Phase 2 Approved — Area Commission Rift Now Structural**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Divided / frustrated (neighborhood voices) / confident (city, developers, affordable housing advocates)**  
Frame: **Threat + Content idea** — Columbus City Council approved Phase 2 of Zone In (covers 43% of city parcels); multiple area commissions formally refused to endorse it; the rift between the city and neighborhood governance bodies is no longer a hearing-room dispute — it's an implementation divide

*Phase 2 passage is confirmed from this run; Zone In May 7 public hearing is tomorrow — outcome not yet available.*

Columbus City Council voted to approve Phase 2 of the Zone In initiative, the most significant update to city zoning in over 70 years. Phase 2 covers 43% of city parcels (~66,000 properties) and focuses on commercial/mixed-use zones. Specific district-level standards — building heights, transitions, building types — are being developed in 2026. Multiple area commissions refused to endorse the plan, with Clintonville's area commission the most vocal, citing concerns about tree canopy loss, accessibility impacts, and neighborhood character. WOSU headlined a "rift between city leaders and neighborhoods" in December 2025; that rift has hardened in the months since. Pro-housing groups (Columbus New Liberals, affordable housing developers) are actively engaged in favor. Zone In Phase 3 — addressing single-family residential districts — is now scheduled for 2027.

> "Multiple area commissions are refusing to endorse the next step in the city's Zone-In plan." — Columbus Underground ([columbusunderground.com/we-asked-for-responses-to-the-citys-zoning-proposal-heres-what-we-heard-bw1](https://columbusunderground.com/we-asked-for-responses-to-the-citys-zoning-proposal-heres-what-we-heard-bw1/))

**3. $500M Bond Implementation — Net Displacement Concern Is Now Documented**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Alarmed / skeptical (advocates); Confident (city officials)**  
Frame: **Threat** — affordable housing advocates have now published data showing that displacements at three Columbus properties removed more deeply affordable units than $1 billion+ in investment created; this is the credibility risk the bond program must answer

*New to this run — Matter News and Columbus Underground published accountability framing.*

Columbus voters approved a $500M affordable housing bond in November 2025. Mayor Ginther unveiled the spending framework in February 2026: $175M for land acquisition near transit, $150M for affordable unit construction/preservation, $125M for shelter and stability, $50M for innovation. But Matter News and Columbus Underground have now raised a structural challenge: displacements at Colonial Village, Latitude Five25, and Galloway Village alone removed more deeply affordable units from the market than more than $1 billion in cumulative public and private investment created. Housing advocates told Columbus Navigator and WOSU that when community members hear Columbus's affordability definitions, the reaction is: "That is not affordable for me." Columbus defines "affordable" relative to AMI bands that can exceed what extremely low-income households — a 53,000-unit deficit at the extreme end — can actually pay.

> "Displacements at Colonial Village, Latitude Five25, and Galloway Village alone have removed more deeply affordable units from the market than were created by more than a billion dollars in investment." — Matter News ([matternews.org/community/development/new-affordable-housing-investments-may-leave-columbus-most-vulnerable-properties-and-tenants-behind](https://matternews.org/community/development/new-affordable-housing-investments-may-leave-columbus-most-vulnerable-properties-and-tenants-behind/))

> "When community members hear the city's affordability definitions, they often say, 'That is not affordable for me.'" — Columbus Navigator ([columbusnavigator.com/columbus-affordable-housing-plan](https://www.columbusnavigator.com/columbus-affordable-housing-plan))

> "Columbus has a particular lack of units for households that earn less than $52,000 a year." — Columbus Navigator (same source)

**4. Rental Registry Passed 8-1; Lawsuit Clock Ticking**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated (landlords); Validated (tenant advocates)**  
Frame: **Threat (landlord audience) + Opportunity (tenant and policy audience)** — Columbus City Council voted 8-1 to pass the rental registry on April 20; Columbus Apartment Association confirmed legal challenge intent; no suit filed yet as of this scan

*Confirmed from this run; lawsuit status unchanged from May 5 brief.*

Columbus City Council voted 8-1 on April 20, 2026 to create a citywide rental registry, requiring landlords to register properties, pay $15/unit annually (capped at $1,500 per complex), and submit to common-area inspections every three years. Individual unit inspections are triggered only when a property enters "conditional" status for repeated code violations. The Columbus Apartment Association and its attorney Dimitri Hatzifotinos have stated clearly they will challenge the ordinance in court, arguing it conflicts with state law and creates duplicative administrative burden. No suit has been filed as of this scan.

> "We plan to challenge it in court." — Dimitri Hatzifotinos, Columbus Apartment Association attorney, NBC4 ([nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-oks-rental-registry-to-hold-landlords-accountable](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-oks-rental-registry-to-hold-landlords-accountable/))

> "It doesn't fit with state law, so it creates more administrative burden." — Dimitri Hatzifotinos (same source)

> "Columbus OKs rental registry to hold landlords accountable." — NBC4 / AOL (April 2026; same source)

Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / worried**

*Updated from May 4–5 briefs — peer-city price comparables are new to this run.*

Active tariffs remain in effect: 10% on timber/lumber, 25% on imported cabinets, vanities, and upholstered furniture (effective October 14, 2025). Multiple developers named to local media sources cite "several local developments on hold due to higher cost environment." NBC4's unnamed developer quote ("we don't know from any given day what those costs are really going to look like") remains the most direct verbatim on record; it's unnamed and attributed to NBC4, not to the individual.

**7. Ohio Data Center Ballot — 413K Signatures Needed by July 1; New Albany Watch**  
Volume: **LIGHT** | Sentiment: **Mixed (rural supporters vs. CRE / tech community)**  
Frame: **Threat** — if the ballot initiative qualifies in July, a November uncertainty window opens for large land deals in New Albany and the Columbus tech corridor; institutional buyers will price that uncertainty

*Confirmed from this run — signature drive active as of April 30, 2026.*

Ohio Residents for Responsible Development needs 413,488 valid signatures from at least 44 of Ohio's 88 counties by July 1, 2026 to put a constitutional ban on data centers exceeding 25MW aggregate power demand on the November 2026 ballot. The Ohio Ballot Board unanimously authorized the petition drive on April 2. The organizers are primarily from Brown and Adams County (rural southwestern Ohio) and originated in opposition to a local data center project. Columbus-area implications: a November ballot measure would introduce land-deal uncertainty in New Albany through election day, potentially cooling large-parcel acquisition near Intel's Ohio One campus.

> "413,488 valid signatures are required to get the initiative certified for the 2026 ballot." — Ballotpedia ([ballotpedia.org/Ohio_Prohibition_of_Data_Center_Construction_Amendment_(2026)](https://ballotpedia.org/Ohio_Prohibition_of_Data_Center_Construction_Amendment_(2026)))
