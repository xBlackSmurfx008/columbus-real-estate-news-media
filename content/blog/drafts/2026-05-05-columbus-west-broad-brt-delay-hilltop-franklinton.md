# Columbus West Broad BRT Faces 16-Month Delay: What Investors Need to Know

The West Broad Street BRT corridor — Columbus's most closely watched transit infrastructure investment for Franklinton, Hilltop, and Downtown real estate — is running at least 16 months behind schedule. Engineering firm HNTB disclosed the delay to COTA's board of trustees in late April 2026, citing revised traffic projections that forced significant redesign work on a project that was already 90 percent complete.

For Columbus real estate operators and investors, the practical question isn't about commute times. It's about timelines. Properties and development projects that have been underwritten to benefit from BRT-era station access now need a new set of assumptions.

---

## What Changed: The HNTB Report to COTA's Board

In April 2026, HNTB — the engineering consulting firm leading design on the West Broad Street BRT corridor — told COTA's board of trustees that the project schedule is delayed approximately 16 months, WOSU Public Media first reported on April 23.

Separately, CK Construction Group, the project's construction manager at risk, is projecting an 11-month delay to the planned December 2028 public opening of the corridor.

The gap between those two figures — 16 months versus 11 months — reflects the difference between design-phase delay and construction-phase impact. HNTB's estimate describes how far behind the design schedule has fallen; CK Construction's figure reflects the downstream effect on the projected opening date. COTA has not yet issued an official revised schedule as of early May 2026.

Columbus Underground reported that changes to the Downtown Broad Street segment — including possible adjustments to a planned two-way bike facility on the south side of the street — may also be part of the resolution process. ## Why ODOT Revised the Traffic Numbers

The root cause traces to May 2025, when the Ohio Department of Transportation issued updated traffic projections for the West Broad corridor. Those projections roughly doubled the anticipated traffic volumes that HNTB had been working with.

ODOT issued the revised projections in direct response to Governor Mike DeWine's March 2025 order directing state employees to return to the office full-time. The mandate reintroduced peak-hour traffic volumes that had been materially reduced during and after the pandemic — and that the original corridor design had not fully accounted for.

The result: a 90-percent-complete design required substantial rework. That's the mechanical explanation for the delay. The underlying cause is a state-level policy decision unrelated to transit — a return-to-office order — producing structural consequences for a multi-year infrastructure project.

---

## The West Broad Corridor and What's at Stake for Columbus Real Estate

The West Broad Street BRT is one of two primary LinkUS corridors — the other being East Main Street — being developed by COTA with federal support. The project received a $42 million federal grant.

The corridor spans 9.3 miles from Rockbrook Crossing in Prairie Township to Washington Avenue in Downtown Columbus. It includes 17 bus rapid transit stations, five miles of dedicated bus lanes, integrated bike lanes, and sidewalk improvements throughout. Construction was originally targeted to begin in 2026, with a public opening projected for December 2028.

For real estate operators and investors, those station locations are not just transit infrastructure — they are site-specific development triggers. Properties within walking distance of planned BRT stations have attracted investor interest and development attention based on a 2028 activation assumption. A 16-month design delay, combined with an 11-month projected opening delay, stretches that assumption materially.

---

## Franklinton and the Hilltop: The Neighborhoods Most Directly Affected

The West Broad Street BRT has always been as much a Franklinton-and-Hilltop story as a transit story. Both neighborhoods sit directly along the corridor and have seen significant development activity in anticipation of improved connectivity.

Franklinton — Columbus's near-west neighborhood along the Scioto River — has absorbed a wave of mixed-use and residential development over the past several years. [Internal link: prior CRE News coverage of Franklinton development.] The corridor's BRT stations through Franklinton were a central underwriting thesis for operators seeking transit-connected tenants at below-downtown pricing.

The Hilltop, further west along the corridor, was cited by Mayor Ginther in his 2026 State of the City address as part of nearly $765 million in public and private investment flowing into historically disinvested neighborhoods over the past decade. Transit access has been central to the city's pitch to the Hilltop as an investable neighborhood. A delayed BRT opening doesn't cancel that thesis — but it lengthens the timeline for when transit-dependent residents will see meaningful service improvements, and extends the horizon for TOD-dependent property value appreciation along the corridor.

---

## What Investors Should Recalibrate Now

The BRT delay does not change the long-term fundamentals of the West Broad corridor. Franklinton and the Hilltop remain active development markets. The 9.3-mile route still represents one of the most significant transit investments in Central Ohio history. What changes is the hold-period math.

**If you're in a 3–5 year hold near a planned BRT station:** The corridor's public opening may now extend to 2029 or beyond, depending on COTA's final revised schedule. If your underwriting assumed BRT activation during your hold period, verify that assumption once COTA publishes a formal update.

**If you're evaluating land near planned station areas:** Your carry is longer than previously modeled. Factor in additional months of holding costs before transit-driven demand triggers are fully in place.

**If you're a Franklinton or Hilltop retailer or parking operator near the corridor:** Construction along West Broad will eventually bring significant street-level disruption — but that disruption start date is now pushed back. Use the additional lead time to plan contingency operations.

**If you're tracking the schedule for business planning:** COTA's board will receive a revised timeline later in 2026. CRE News will cover that update as soon as it is formally issued.

---

## What Comes Next

COTA has not published a formal revised timeline as of early May 2026. The agency is evaluating both HNTB's 16-month design delay and CK Construction Group's 11-month opening delay projection, and working through potential design changes to the Downtown Broad Street segment.

The East Main Street BRT corridor — the other primary LinkUS corridor — is on its own separate timeline and is not affected by the West Broad delay.

CRE News will monitor COTA board meetings and report when a formal revised schedule is issued. Operators and investors with material exposure to the corridor should monitor the [LinkUS West Broad page at COTA](https://linkuscolumbus.com/westbroad/) and WOSU and Columbus Underground coverage for updates as the design process concludes.
