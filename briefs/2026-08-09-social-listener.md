**1. RealPage Rent-Fixing Settlement — Columbus Renters May Be Owed Money, and Few Know It**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Negative (toward landlords/software) / Frustrated / Curious**  
Frame: **Threat + Content idea** — a $360M consumer class-action settlement and a DOJ antitrust case against RealPage and six major landlords (all operating in Columbus) are generating conversation among renters who overpaid; claims are open now through January 29, 2027, but awareness among Columbus renters appears low

This is the highest-urgency signal in today's scan and a direct-harm story for Columbus renters.

The DOJ sued RealPage and six major corporate landlords — Greystar Real Estate Partners, Blackstone's LivCor, Camden Property Trust, Cushman & Wakefield/Pinnacle, Willow Bridge Property Company, and Cortland Management — for using algorithmic pricing software to share nonpublic data and keep rents artificially high. All six operate properties in Central Ohio: Cortland, Cushman & Wakefield, and Greystar manage at least 16 apartment complexes in the Columbus metro. CW Columbus reported that at least one Columbus landlord has already struck a separate deal with the DOJ over the alleged scheme. Ohio is one of 13 affected states.

A parallel $360M consumer class-action settlement is open for claims now, with a deadline of January 29, 2027. Eligibility is based on whether your landlord used RealPage's software — not whether you personally used it. Claims are free to file.

Volume: STEADY — CW Columbus (cwcolumbus.com), NBC4, Yahoo News, CBS News, ProPublica, and lawhighlights.com all confirmed active coverage. The consumer claims story is circulating broadly in tenant-advocacy channels. Direct Reddit/social access blocked; volume is likely higher than indexable.

**3. Columbus Apartment Association vs. City — Rental Registry Legal Battle Heating Up**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Negative (landlord community) / Positive (tenant advocates)**  
Frame: **Threat** — Columbus City Council passed the citywide rental registry ordinance 8-1 on April 20, 2026; the Columbus Apartment Association has threatened a legal challenge, arguing the registry conflicts with state law and duplicates existing Franklin County requirements; no lawsuit filed as of this scan, but the conflict is active and community conversation is split sharply along landlord/tenant lines

The ordinance requires every residential rental in Columbus to register annually with the Department of Building and Zoning Services. The fee: $15/unit, capped at $1,500 per apartment complex. Common area inspections every 3 years; individual unit inspections only if a property is placed in "conditional" status.

The Columbus Apartment Association's attorney Dimitri Hatzifotinos is quoted: *"It doesn't fit with state law, so it creates more administrative burden."* (Source: National Today / nationaltoday.com, confirmed)

NBC4 headline confirmed: *"Columbus OKs rental registry to hold landlords accountable"* — indicating wide public awareness.

Scioto Title (sciototitle.com) confirmed coverage of the ordinance as a significant compliance change for landlords. RL Property Management (rlpmg.com/blog/columbus-rental-property-registry/) confirmed active guidance being issued to property owners.

Volume: STEADY — multiple distinct institutional voices (CAA, city council, tenant advocates, property managers) active in the conversation. Direct quote access limited; landlord social communities not accessible this scan.

Sources: WOSU (wosu.org/politics-government/2026-04-15/columbus-city-council-proposes-rental-registry-to-combat-problem-landlords), NBC4 (nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-oks-rental-registry-to-hold-landlords-accountable/), National Today (nationaltoday.com/us/oh/columbus/news/2026/02/23), Scioto Title (sciototitle.com, confirmed), RL Property Management (rlpmg.com, confirmed)
