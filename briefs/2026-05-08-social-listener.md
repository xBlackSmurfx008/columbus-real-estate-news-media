**1. Near East Side — Museum Groundbreaking Voices Surface Gentrification Tension**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Positive + Concerned (bittersweet)**  
Frame: **Threat + Content idea** — the Poindexter Village Museum groundbreaking drew multiple named community voices who explicitly tied the celebration to anxiety about rapid neighborhood change; "sustaining this history as it's changing" is the through-line; cultural investment and market-rate pressure are arriving on the same corridor simultaneously

The $25.9M Poindexter Village African American Museum and Cultural Learning Center broke ground May 6 on North Champion Avenue between East Long Street and Mount Vernon Avenue in the King-Lincoln Bronzeville neighborhood. Ohio History Connection has raised $14.5M of its $25.9M goal; CMHA added $500K at the event, on top of its previous $1M commitment. Designed by Moody Nolan (the country's largest Black-owned architecture firm), opening expected 2028. Key real estate implication: the same near-east-side corridor is simultaneously seeing Opportunity Pointe demolition begin (May 6, Cleveland Ave), continued CSCC campus expansion, and growing transit infrastructure investment. The museum is both a cultural anchor and a potential gentrification accelerator in a neighborhood that has historically been affordable. The project director's own words surface the tension at the very groundbreaking — she used the word "changing," not "improving."

> "This is a huge milestone. The community has worked over a decade for this effort. We also are looking forward to the work that needs to be done on the Near East Side to sustain this history as it's changing." — Shelbi Toone, project director, Ohio History Connection, at the groundbreaking ([wosu.org/news/2026-05-06/ohio-history-connection-breaks-ground-on-poindexter-village-african-american-museum](https://www.wosu.org/news/2026-05-06/ohio-history-connection-breaks-ground-on-poindexter-village-african-american-museum))

> "I see Poindexter Village as being one of the things that was done right." — Nancy Jones, former Poindexter Village resident, at the groundbreaking ([abc6onyourside.com/news/local/ohio-history-connection-breaks-ground-on-poindexter-village-african-american-museum-in-columbus](https://abc6onyourside.com/news/local/ohio-history-connection-breaks-ground-on-poindexter-village-african-american-museum-in-columbus))

> "The Poindexter Village African American Museum will connect the history of public housing, urban renewal and African American heritage through immersive exhibits, interactive displays and powerful storytelling. This museum will be a place where all Ohioans can learn from the stories of resilience, creativity and community that have defined the Near East Side and the greater African American community for generations." — Megan Wood, Executive Director and CEO, Ohio History Connection ([knoxpages.com/2026/05/06/ohio-history-connection-breaks-ground-on-25-9m-poindexter-village-african-american-museum-in-columbus/](https://www.knoxpages.com/2026/05/06/ohio-history-connection-breaks-ground-on-25-9m-poindexter-village-african-american-museum-in-columbus/))

**2. Affordable Housing Gap — Structural Deficit Confirmed at Scale; No Single Columbus Outlet Owns the Accountability Story**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Alarmed**  
Frame: **Threat** — the 2026 COHHIO Gap Report confirms Columbus has only 27 affordable units per 100 ELI renter households and a 53,000-unit deficit; data is now circulating across multiple outlets but no Columbus CRE outlet has assembled the gap data, the $500M bond accountability question, and the net displacement documentation in a single piece

*Carry from May 3–7 scans; COHHIO Gap Report 2026 URL confirmed.*

COHHIO/NLIHC "Gap Report 2026" (March 5, 2026): Ohio has 37 affordable units per 100 ELI renter households statewide; Columbus specifically has 53,000 fewer affordable units than needed, with only 27 units available per 100 ELI renters. Full-time Ohio worker must earn at least $22.51/hour to afford a modest 2-bedroom apartment. Columbus $500M affordable housing bond is the city's headline response; the accountability question — whether it produces net new deeply affordable units or merely slows depletion — remains unanswered by any single Columbus outlet. OHFA's FY 2026 Ohio Housing Needs Assessment confirms the structural nature of the gap.

> "Ohio has a shortage of affordable rental units for extremely low-income renters. There are only 37 affordable units available for every 100 ELI renter households across the state." — 2026 COHHIO/NLIHC Gap Report ([cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/](https://cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/))

> "Columbus has a deficit of more than 53,000 affordable rental units for those in the sub-30 percent income category." — Assessment (NBC4/AHACO) ([nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/assessment-shows-net-loss-of-affordable-rental-housing/](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/assessment-shows-net-loss-of-affordable-rental-housing/))
