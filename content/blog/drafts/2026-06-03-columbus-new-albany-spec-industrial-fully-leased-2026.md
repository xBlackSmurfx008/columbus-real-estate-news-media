In April, before the doors were open, VanTrust Real Estate announced that New Albany 300 was fully leased.

The building — a 300,400-square-foot speculative industrial facility at 12550 Jug Street NW in the New Albany International Business Park — was still under construction at the time. Its June 2026 delivery date was weeks away. The tenant's name was never made public. But the deal was done.

VanTrust's response was immediate: break ground on New Albany 302, a 302,400-square-foot spec building at the same site, targeting a December 2026 delivery. It is the developer's seventh speculative project in New Albany and its tenth overall in the park.

In Columbus industrial real estate right now, that is what demand looks like.

## New Albany 300: The Building That Leased Before It Opened

New Albany 300's specs are competitive without being exceptional: 36-foot clear heights, modern dock configuration, 15-year 100% tax abatement. It is a well-executed spec building in a well-run master-planned park.

What makes it notable is the timing. Pre-delivery full lease-up on a 300,000-square-foot industrial building means that a tenant — or a group of tenants — was sufficiently confident in the space, the location, and the economics to commit before taking occupancy. That requires underwriting the site without seeing the finished product, and it suggests a tenant who had been watching the park for some time and was ready to move when the opportunity aligned.

Leasing was handled by Beau Taggart of KBC Advisors and Mike Hurd of Cushman & Wakefield. [needs source: quote from leasing brokers]

What drove the pre-delivery commitment is the same factor that has been reshaping industrial real estate in New Albany since 2022: Intel.

## The Intel Effect

In 2022, Intel announced plans to construct a $20-billion-plus semiconductor fabrication campus in Licking County — directly adjacent to the New Albany International Business Park. The project, called Ohio One, was described at announcement as the largest economic development deal in Ohio history. At full buildout, it would employ 3,000-plus workers and anchor a broader semiconductor supply chain across central Ohio.

The Intel campus has not yet opened. Construction timelines have shifted. But the supply chain Intel requires does not wait for the fab doors to open. Tooling suppliers, materials companies, specialty logistics operators, and precision-manufacturing vendors need to be positioned before the facility starts ramping production. They need space now.

The New Albany Company — the master developer of the New Albany International Business Park — has described VanTrust's spec buildings explicitly as "ideal for chip plant suppliers." That framing is not accidental. It reflects a deliberate positioning of the park as a supply chain staging area for Intel's Ohio One campus, less than a mile away across the Franklin-Licking county line.

VanTrust has read that signal correctly. Pre-delivery lease-up is the result. [*internal link: New Albany data center and industrial market coverage*]

## The Spec Cycle as Market Intelligence

When VanTrust broke ground on New Albany 300, it was speculative. There was no tenant, no letter of intent, no signed lease. The developer was betting on the market.

That bet paid off before the building was finished.

The business logic of the immediate 7th spec start is straightforward: if a building leases before it delivers, the developer has confirmed that supply is still below demand at the clearing price. Starting the next building immediately minimizes the gap between current lease demand and future deliverable supply. Every day that passes after New Albany 300's lease-up without a new spec building in the ground is a day where tenant demand is chasing inventory that does not yet exist.

VanTrust has run this pattern ten times in the New Albany park. Each time a building leases quickly, they start the next one. The market has not punished them for it yet.

New Albany 302 — designed by RED Architecture and being constructed by Elford Construction — delivers in December 2026. It is already being marketed.

## Columbus Industrial's Market Floor

VanTrust's New Albany activity is the most granular data point available on a market that has been posting broad-based strength for several years.

Columbus's industrial market closed 2025 with 8.8 million square feet of positive net absorption — ranking among the top five U.S. markets by absorption volume. More than 5.2 million square feet are currently under construction across the Columbus MSA, reflecting sustained developer confidence even as deliveries have remained elevated.

The Columbus market's industrial performance is driven by several converging factors:

**Logistics centrality.** Columbus sits at the intersection of I-70 and I-71, within overnight drive of more than 50 percent of the U.S. population. That geographic position has attracted every major national distribution and logistics operator: Amazon, Walmart, Target, FedEx, ODW Logistics, and dozens of regional operators. In 2025, more than 8 million square feet of leases tied to distribution were executed in the Columbus MSA.

**Data center adjacency.** New Albany International Business Park is not just a logistics park. Meta's Prometheus data center — the world's first requiring more than one gigawatt of power — is under construction there. EdgeConneX is converting a 524,525-square-foot warehouse and planning a 700,000-square-foot new facility. Microsoft has committed $420 million to a New Albany data center. These hyperscale tenants generate co-location and infrastructure demand that spills into adjacent industrial.

**ODW and Rickenbacker.** ODW Logistics is adding an 864,000-square-foot automated facility at the Lockbourne/Rickenbacker campus, equipped with autonomous mobile robots and expected to hire 200–300 workers. The Rickenbacker submarket — anchored by the Rickenbacker International Airport and its cargo infrastructure — continues to attract distribution and logistics users that cannot be served from the I-270 belt.

**Tight vacancy.** As Columbus continues to absorb, land availability and extended entitlement timelines constrain supply responses. The conditions that drive pre-delivery lease-up — tight vacancy in the relevant submarket — are structural in New Albany. They are unlikely to resolve before 2028 even with aggressive spec construction.

## What New Albany International Business Park Offers

New Albany International Business Park has been growing since the early 2000s. Over 40 technology, industrial, and data center facilities now operate within the park, and 28 more are on the drawing board or under construction.

The park's core competencies as an industrial location:

- **15-year 100% tax abatement** on qualifying improvements — among the most competitive in the Ohio industrial market, compressing cash-on-cost and effective tax burden for both tenants and investors
- **Interstate access**: Direct connection to I-270 (outer belt) and Route 161 (National Road corridor), with I-70 and the full Columbus logistics network accessible within 15 minutes
- **Fiber and utility infrastructure**: Built to data center specs, which means industrial tenants benefit from over-provisioned power, redundant connectivity, and high-speed data access
- **Master plan predictability**: The New Albany Company's long-running master plan means developers and tenants can underwrite adjacent parcels with confidence about surrounding uses — a meaningful difference from opportunistic suburban industrial parks

The 15-year abatement is the most talked-about feature. On a 300,000-square-foot industrial building, a 15-year full abatement can represent millions of dollars in tax savings over the hold period — savings that compound into yield calculations for buyers and inform cap rate expectations for sellers.

## The Merchant Building Parallel

Tuesday's delivery of New Albany 300 arrives in the same week that the $430 million Merchant Building at Columbus's North Market is approaching its Q4 2026 opening. The contrast is instructive.

Downtown Columbus multifamily is navigating a supply overhang: 10.6 percent vacancy, 9,093 units delivered last year versus a historical average of 3,848. The Merchant Building's lease-up — when it begins — will be a bellwether for whether downtown demand can absorb premium new product even in a soft market.

New Albany industrial is in the opposite condition. Demand is absorbing supply before the buildings are complete. VanTrust cannot build fast enough to stay ahead of it. [*internal link: Columbus downtown apartment market analysis*]

The divergence between downtown multifamily and suburban industrial is one of the defining features of the Columbus CRE market in 2026. Different asset classes, different demand drivers, different cycles — handled by operators who understand which narrative applies to which zip code.

## What Columbus Operators Should Watch

**Track VanTrust's New Albany 302 lease-up.** If New Albany 302 reaches full lease-up before its December 2026 delivery — as New Albany 300 did — that confirms the pattern is structural, not cyclical. An 8th spec start would follow within months.

**Intel construction milestones are the underlying demand signal.** Ohio One construction progress, supplier announcements, and hiring timelines at the Intel fab are the most forward-looking indicator of sustained industrial demand in the New Albany corridor. Watch Intel's Ohio One project page and related announcements.

**Land.** VanTrust's 10-project track record in the park means their preferred parcels are largely committed. Adjacent parcels in the Jug Street / Clover Valley Road corridor and along the Route 161 frontage are worth watching for future spec starts from VanTrust or competing developers. [needs source: available parcel inventory in New Albany International Business Park]

**The 15-year abatement expiration pipeline.** The earliest New Albany International Business Park buildings are now approaching the tail end of their 15-year abatement periods. As those abatements roll off, cap rate adjustments and potential portfolio rebalancing by early investors could create acquisition opportunities for operators seeking stabilized assets at repriced yields.

**Rickenbacker as the overflow valve.** If New Albany land is constrained, Rickenbacker remains the most logical alternative for large-format industrial users who need southwest Columbus access and cargo airport infrastructure. Watch for spec starts in the Rickenbacker submarket as a secondary indicator of Columbus industrial demand depth.
