The Italian Village Commission has approved Merus's proposal to build 428 apartments across five buildings on the seven-acre site at 274 E. First Ave. that until recently housed the State Library of Ohio and the Ohioana Library. Groundbreaking is tentatively scheduled for early 2027, with the first phase expected to deliver in roughly two years.

## The Site: A Public Institution's Footprint Becomes Residential

The warehouse-scale building at 274 E. First Ave. sits on the eastern edge of Italian Village, where the neighborhood's Victorian residential fabric begins to give way to the Near East Side's broader commercial and industrial mix. For decades, the facility held two state-level institutions: the State Library of Ohio, which serves as the professional research library for state government and maintains Ohio's official document collection, and the Ohioana Library, an organization cataloguing Ohio-connected literature since 1929.

Both have since relocated. City staff classified the existing structure as "non-contributing" to Italian Village's historic character — a designation meaning demolition does not face a preservation barrier under Columbus's Historic Resources Commission review standards. That classification cleared a significant procedural hurdle for Merus.

Merus is a Cincinnati-based developer expanding its footprint into the Columbus market. The firm had not previously announced major projects in Franklin County prior to this proposal.

## Five Buildings, Seven Acres

The commission-approved plan calls for five apartment buildings and a parking structure on the seven-acre parcel, according to [Columbus Underground's coverage of the approval](https://columbusunderground.com/italian-village-development-approved-bw1/). Three of the buildings stand three stories tall; two reach four stories. The parking garage occupies the eastern edge of the site, attached to one of the four-story structures. Surface parking lots are also included in the plan.

The buildings are described as "architecturally distinct" from one another — a design strategy used in large multi-building residential complexes to break up the visual mass of development along a streetscape.

The unit count merits a note: earlier proposal-stage coverage from [NBC4](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/redevelopment-plan-for-former-state-library-site-returns-to-city-review/) and Hoodline cited 419 units, while the figure confirmed at the commission approval stage is 428. The discrepancy likely reflects plan revisions between initial filing and final commission submission. Direct commission meeting minutes were not available to this publication at time of writing; the 428-unit figure is sourced from search-retrieved journalism summaries of the approval meeting.

## Italian Village's Development Trajectory

Italian Village — a roughly 160-acre neighborhood of Victorian-era row housing and mixed-use corridors north of the Short North — has absorbed significant multifamily investment since 2020. The Lusso and Stella projects along the neighborhood's western edge added several hundred units and established a ground-floor commercial pattern. Hoodline's 2026 coverage documented 260 additional apartments rising near the East Fifth Street corridor on the southern boundary.

The Merus proposal is larger than those individual projects and occupies a site not previously in the residential development pipeline — a seven-acre parcel that was, until very recently, a functioning public institution. That makes the Italian Village Commission's approval one of the more consequential land-use decisions in the neighborhood in recent memory.

The site's location along East First Avenue also gives the project above-average street connectivity. First Avenue links Italian Village to the Short North's southern end and, via Columbus's one-way arterial grid, to Interstate 670. The development site is also within reasonable distance of the Columbus Commons green space and the Nationwide Arena district — both employment and amenity drivers for multifamily absorption.

## Zone In Phase 2 Context

The commission approval comes as Columbus advances the second phase of its Zone In zoning overhaul — a rewrite of the city's zoning code that has not been comprehensively updated in more than 70 years. Phase 2 is expected to reclassify parcels covering roughly 43 percent of the city and go before City Council for a final vote in the fourth quarter of 2026, according to [Columbus Underground's timeline reporting](https://columbusunderground.com/timeline-strategy-laid-out-for-next-phases-of-zoning-reform-bw1/).

Italian Village operates under an additional review layer — the Italian Village Commission and Historic Resources Commission — that functions in parallel with the base zoning code. How Zone In Phase 2's new districts interact with those overlay reviews has not been publicly spelled out. Developers and property owners in the neighborhood should watch for any Zone In guidance that addresses historic overlay districts.

## What's Still Unknown

Several facts remain unresolved in the public record:

**Affordability mix.** The sources reviewed for this article describe no income restrictions, market-rate pricing, or affordable-unit set-asides. Whether the project participates in Columbus's $500 million Affordable Housing Bond program, uses tax increment financing, or applies for Low-Income Housing Tax Credits is not publicly documented at this stage.

**Phase scope.** Whether "first phase" implies a subsequent phase on the same parcel — and what that would entail in terms of unit count or building type — was not specified in available coverage.

**Design details.** No renderings, materials palette, or streetscape treatment were available through sources retrieved for this report.

CRE News sought comment from Merus and requested the Italian Village Commission meeting minutes to verify the approved unit count and any attached conditions. Neither had responded as of publication.

## What to Watch Next

The first verifiable checkpoint after a commission approval is the building permit application, which will appear in Franklin County's parcel and permit database when Merus files it — typically six to twelve months ahead of a groundbreaking. If groundbreaking holds to early 2027, a permit filing would be expected by late 2026.

Watch also for any City Council vote on Zone In Phase 2 in Q4 2026 that could affect the underlying zoning framework for this stretch of First Avenue, and for any public announcement of financing terms that would clarify whether the project carries affordable units.
