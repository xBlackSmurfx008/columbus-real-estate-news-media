### 3. Columbus housing affordability — 53K-unit deficit, $27.50/hr wage-to-rent gap — LOUD, negative/frustrated

**Summary:** The Columbus housing affordability conversation continues to gain traction across multiple outlets and dimensions. The core facts in active circulation: (1) Columbus has a deficit of 53,000 affordable rental units; (2) renters need $27.50/hr to afford a two-bedroom in Columbus, vs. the ~$19.59/hr average renter wage — a $7.91/hr gap; (3) home prices have surged 51% since 2020; (4) nearly half of Ohio renters are spending more than they can afford. New DataPoint this run: the COHHIO 2026 Gap Report (full publication) is now actively cited in social-adjacent outlet coverage alongside the Ohio Capital Journal's July 24 reporting. NewsBreak published "Columbus Rent Crisis 2026: The Numbers Look Affordable, But Thousands of Renters Are Still Being Left Behind" — a piece that explicitly challenges the "normalizing market" narrative circulating from broker blogs and the Columbus REALTORS July report. CRE Daily published "Housing Affordability Worsens As Columbus Home Prices Surge." These two pieces signal that a counter-narrative to the agent/seller "balance" framing is actively forming and gaining audience.

**Volume signal:** Loud — multiple distinct outlet types (advocacy, commercial RE trade, national news aggregators) now publishing Columbus affordability-critical content simultaneously with broker market-normalization content.
