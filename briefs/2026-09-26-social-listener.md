## Lead 1 — Franklinton: Grateful Development Partners Revises 40-Unit Design for 610 W. Town St.

**Summary:** Hoodline reported on September 25, 2026 that Grateful Development Partners presented a revised design for a 40-unit apartment building at 610 W. Town St. in Franklinton — the former Glass Axis studio site — at the East Franklinton Review Board. Architect Jonathan Grubb of Archall Architects presented the updated design, which preserves the building's 1928 brick facade while adding three stories of apartments set behind it. New design elements include corrugated metal panels and color accents described as nodding to Franklinton's industrial character. The project includes a 48-car rear parking lot. The East Franklinton Review Board reviewed the design conceptually but did not take a formal vote. Applicants plan to return for final approval. Board sign-off is required before construction can begin under Columbus City Code Chapter 3323.

## Lead 2 — Gahanna: Como Development Flex-Industrial Park Clears Planning Board, Heads to Council

**Summary:** Hoodline reported on September 26, 2026 that Como Development (principal: Leland Vogel, Galena-based) received unanimous approval from Gahanna's planning commission for a 36,000-square-foot flex-industrial park at 1675 Eastgate Pkwy in Gahanna. The project, dubbed Gahanna Flex Park, would replace a vacant four-acre lot with three separate 12,000-square-foot buildings, each divided into eight 1,500-square-foot tenant spaces, for a total of 24 small-business units. Target tenants include contractors, automotive service shops, print shops, and local office, retail, and service operations. Additional features include 49 parking spaces and a 12,000-square-foot enclosed storage area with a 10-foot chain-link fence. The development still requires Gahanna City Council approval; the council held a first reading of the construction agreement, with the final vote timeline unclear.

**Key context:**
- This is a light-industrial/commercial development rather than residential — notable contrast in a Columbus-area market dominated by residential pipeline coverage.
- The Eastgate Pkwy corridor east of Taylor Station Road is an emerging suburban flex-commercial corridor.
- The project targets the small-business/contractor end of the market, not large-bay industrial or warehouse, distinguishing it from the Rickenbacker logistics transactions covered in recent Colliers weekly reviews.
