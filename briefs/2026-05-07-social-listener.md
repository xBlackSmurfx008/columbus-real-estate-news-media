**1. Intel New Albany — 2030–2031 Delay Inverts the Investment Thesis**  
Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / alarmed**  
Frame: **Threat** — yesterday's brief characterized New Albany as "the safest equity play in the entire Columbus housing market"; today's confirmed 2030–2031 delay timeline means landowners, homebuilders, and school districts who bought in ahead of Intel now face a 4–6 year uncertain hold with capital tied up; the investment thesis must be restated

⚠️ **Reverses prior scan framing.** The May 6 brief described New Albany luxury demand as LIGHT-volume and flagged "distinct-author social discussion not confirmed." This run confirms the Intel delay to 2030–2031 is now well-documented across multiple distinct named officials and outlets. Intel's Ohio One campus in New Albany — originally announced in 2022 with promised production start by 2025–2026 — is now on the following schedule per a filing with Ohio regulators: Mod 1 construction complete in 2030, production start 2030–2031; Mod 2 construction complete in 2031, operations 2032. Ohio Senator Bernie Moreno and others have publicly asked Intel for delay accountability. One Ohio senator characterized the delays as a "potential fraud" against taxpayers. Intel spent $1.4 billion in Ohio in 2025 and as of its last public statement does not anticipate further delays — but that was also its posture before the 2030 announcement.

**7. Housing Bond Net Displacement — Affordable Housing Gap Documented at Scale**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Alarmed / skeptical (advocates)**  
Frame: **Threat** — 266,000-unit affordable housing shortfall confirmed by COHHIO at April 20–21 Housing Ohio conference; 422,000 extremely low-income renters in Ohio; Columbus ratio: 27 affordable units per 100 ELI renters; net displacement from three properties alone exceeds $1B+ in cumulative investment

*Carry from May 3–6 with COHHIO conference data added.*

The Housing Ohio conference (April 20–21, Hyatt Regency, ~800 attendees) produced the clearest public documentation yet of Ohio's structural housing gap: COHHIO director Amy Riegel stated that 10,000 Ohio residents will potentially lose housing due to federal funding pullbacks. The statewide shortfall stands at 266,000 affordable units. Columbus specifically has 53,000 fewer affordable units than needed for extremely low-income households, with only 27 units available per 100 ELI renters. To rent a "modest" two-bedroom apartment in Ohio, a full-time worker must earn at least $22.51/hour. The $500M Columbus affordable housing bond is the city's headline response; the accountability question — whether the bond will produce net new deeply affordable units or merely slow the depletion — remains unanswered by any single Columbus outlet.

> "Ten thousand people across Ohio will potentially lose their housing because the supports will not be there any longer." — Amy Riegel, COHHIO executive director, Hoodline ([hoodline.com/2026/04/columbus-housing-summit-sounds-alarm-as-working-ohioans-edge-toward-homelessness](https://hoodline.com/2026/04/columbus-housing-summit-sounds-alarm-as-working-ohioans-edge-toward-homelessness/))

**8. Ohio Data Center Ballot — National Attention Growing; July 1 Deadline Active**  
Volume: **LIGHT → STEADY** | Sentiment: **Mixed**  
Frame: **Threat** — signature drive for July 1 now tracked by a national outlet (MultiState, May 7); if the amendment qualifies, a November uncertainty window affects New Albany and Columbus tech-corridor land deals

*Updated from May 6 — MultiState (national policy tracker) published May 7 coverage of state-level data center ballot trends, explicitly tracking Ohio.*

Ohio Residents for Responsible Development needs 413,488 valid signatures across 44 of 88 Ohio counties by July 1, 2026. The petition was certified by AG Dave Yost on March 26. As of April 30, organizers reported county leads in 46 counties; volunteer-only signature drive. The amendment would ban data centers exceeding 25MW aggregate power demand. On May 7, 2026, national policy tracker MultiState published an overview of state-level data center ballot measures, citing Ohio as a leading case. Columbus implication: if the amendment qualifies in July, a November uncertainty window opens for institutional land acquisitions in New Albany and the Columbus tech corridor through election day.
