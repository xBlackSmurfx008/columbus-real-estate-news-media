# Downtown Columbus Gets a Grocery: The Peninsula Phase 2 Breaks Ground with Giant Eagle Market District

For years, people who lived or worked downtown were told Columbus was growing — then reminded at dinnertime that the nearest full-service grocery store was a 20-minute drive. That changes this spring.

Construction is underway on The Peninsula Phase 2, a mixed-use tower rising at the corner of Broad and Belle Streets — the edge where downtown Columbus meets Franklinton. When it opens, the ground floor will house a 20,000-square-foot Giant Eagle Market District, making it the first full-service grocery store in the immediate downtown core. Above it: approximately 250 apartments.

The building is being developed by the Daimler Group and Flaherty & Collins. Downtown Columbus, Inc. has been involved in the project.

---

## What Is Being Built

The Peninsula Phase 2 is a 14-to-15-story mixed-use building [height to be confirmed with developer before publish] anchored at Broad and Belle Streets — the western gateway between Columbus's central business district and the Franklinton neighborhood.

The residential component carries approximately 250 apartments above an attached parking garage. Below them, filling the ground floor and a portion of the first level, the Giant Eagle Market District will offer fresh groceries, ready-to-eat meals, and beer and wine in roughly 20,000 square feet.

Construction started this spring and is expected to wrap between 2027 and 2028, according to multiple local news reports. A specific groundbreaking date was not confirmed at press time — CRE News has reached out to Daimler Group for confirmation [needs source].

The development is the second phase of The Peninsula, the broader mixed-use project that already added a residential building, office space, and parking to the Broad and Belle area over the past several years.

---

## Why Downtown Columbus Has Waited This Long

Anyone who has tried to grocery shop within walking distance of Columbus's central business district knows the answer: there wasn't anywhere to go.

Downtown Columbus has been identified as a grocery desert — a term used by city planners and food-access advocates to describe areas where residents lack convenient access to fresh food — for more than a decade. The designation has been a recurring topic in discussions about downtown residential growth, with developers and city officials alike noting it as a factor limiting downtown's appeal to full-time residents compared to surrounding neighborhoods like Short North, Italian Village, and German Village, where multiple grocery options exist within a few blocks.

[internal link: prior CRE News coverage of downtown Columbus residential density growth]

The 2024 LinkUS transit levy and the Zone In zoning overhaul have both framed downtown and corridor growth as a priority — but the baseline question of where people buy food remained unanswered. Peninsula Phase 2 answers it directly.

---

## The Giant Eagle Market District Anchor

Giant Eagle's Market District concept is the company's higher-end grocery format, positioned above standard Giant Eagle stores in product variety, prepared-food options, and store design. Comparable Market District locations in the Pittsburgh and Cleveland markets have worked as urban neighborhood anchors in mixed-use settings.

At 20,000 square feet, the Downtown Columbus location will be a compact urban format — smaller than a full-size suburban Market District but sized appropriately for a ground-floor mixed-use activation. Offerings confirmed across multiple sources include fresh produce, packaged groceries, ready-to-eat meals, beer, and wine.

The announcement of Giant Eagle as the tenant was made in September 2025 by Downtown Columbus, Inc., with Daimler Group's Bob White Jr. and Mayor Andrew Ginther both cited as having made statements at the announcement. [Full quote text available via ABC6 source link; to be pulled before publish.]

For downtown operators and multifamily owners, a confirmed grocery anchor at this location is a material amenity upgrade for the entire downtown submarket — not just for Peninsula Phase 2 residents.

## The 250-Unit Residential Component

Above the grocery floor, approximately 250 apartments will constitute the residential program. Details on unit mix — studio, one-bedroom, two-bedroom — have not been publicly released at this stage. The building also includes an attached parking garage.

The residential count makes Peninsula Phase 2 one of the larger single-phase apartment deliveries in the immediate downtown-Franklinton area this cycle. For comparison, Phase 1 of The Peninsula added a residential building with a different unit count to the same corridor; the combined Peninsula project is now among the more consequential mixed-use efforts reshaping Broad Street west of High.

[internal link: CRE News coverage of Franklinton development pipeline]

At the market level, Peninsula Phase 2 will add units to a downtown submarket where multifamily vacancy remains below 5% and average rents have risen approximately 4.2% year-over-year through Q1 2026, according to market aggregators. [Confirm with Columbus REALTORS® data before final publish.]

## What It Means for Franklinton's Gateway

Broad and Belle is not just a downtown address — it is Franklinton's front door.

Franklinton has been the subject of sustained redevelopment attention for over a decade, from nonprofit-led affordable projects in the neighborhood's interior to larger mixed-use development near the Scioto Peninsula. The Peninsula project itself sits at the eastern edge of Franklinton, physically connecting the neighborhood to downtown and to the Scioto riverfront.

Peninsula Phase 2 at the Broad-Belle corner reinforces the mixed-use character of that connector. For Franklinton-based operators and residents, a ground-floor grocery at that corner means a walkable amenity that the broader neighborhood — not just the building's 250 residents — can use.

The Franklinton Board of Trade has previously cited The Peninsula as a catalyst fueling growth further west into the Franklinton neighborhood core. Phase 2 extends that argument.
