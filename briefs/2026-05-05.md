### 1. Columbus West Broad BRT Faces 16-Month Construction Delay

- **What**: Engineering firm HNTB disclosed to COTA's board of trustees in April 2026 that the West Broad Street LinkUS BRT corridor is approximately 16 months behind its original construction schedule, due to revised ODOT traffic projections issued in May 2025 after Gov. DeWine's return-to-office order doubled anticipated traffic volumes along the corridor. Construction manager at risk CK Construction Group separately projects an 11-month delay to the planned December 2028 public opening. COTA has not issued a formal revised schedule. Columbus Underground reports that changes to the Downtown Broad Street segment design may also be in scope.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Investors and developers who priced transit-oriented development premiums into properties near the 17 planned BRT stations on this 9.3-mile corridor — especially in Franklinton and the Hilltop — now face extended hold timelines before those premiums are activated. Anyone in a 3–5 year hold with a 2028 BRT open assumption needs to recalibrate.
- **Source(s)**: [WOSU — West Broad Street transit corridor could see 16-month delay (April 23, 2026)](https://www.wosu.org/politics-government/2026-04-23/west-broad-street-transit-corridor-could-see-16-month-delay-in-projected-timeline), [Columbus Underground — COTA: LinkUS Construction Delay, Changes to West Broad Design Possible](https://columbusunderground.com/cota-linkus-construction-delay-changes-to-west-broad-design-possible-bw1/)

### 2. Lincoln Avenue Communities Breaks Ground on 321-Unit Landmark on Scioto Affordable Housing

- **What**: Lincoln Avenue Communities (LAC) broke ground in April 2026 on Landmark on Scioto, a 321-unit affordable housing community on a 44.5-acre site in Columbus. Units target residents earning up to 60% AMI, with two-, three-, and four-bedroom layouts. Amenities include a fitness center, pool, clubhouse, and outdoor spaces. LAC will dedicate approximately 7 acres to the City of Columbus for a future public park. Leasing is expected to begin summer 2026, with full completion in 2028.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: One of the largest single affordable housing groundbreakings in Central Ohio in years and addresses the chronic undersupply of family-sized (3BR/4BR) affordable units — directly relevant to affordable housing operators and CMHA-aligned investors watching the Scioto corridor.
- **Source(s)**: [PR Newswire — Lincoln Avenue Communities Breaks Ground](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lincoln-avenue-communities-breaks-ground-on-new-affordable-housing-development-in-columbus-ohio-302737402.html), [WhatNow Columbus](https://whatnow.com/columbus/real-estate/321-unit-affordable-housing-project-breaks-ground-in-columbus/), [MultifamilyBiz](https://www.multifamilybiz.com/news/11362/lincoln_avenue_communities_breaks_ground_on_321uni...), [YieldPro](https://yieldpro.com/2026/04/scioto/)

### 4. Columbus Zone In: Phase 3 Zoning Overhaul Advancing Through 2026

- **What**: Following City Council's June 2025 passage of Zone In Phase 2 — covering 43% of the city's parcels (~66,000 properties) — Columbus is drafting Phase 3 zoning districts. Phase 3 passage is targeted for 2026, with full implementation continuing into 2027. The Zone In overhaul is the most significant rewrite of Columbus's zoning code in over 70 years.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Phase 3 will unlock by-right development changes and rezoning opportunities across the remaining portion of Columbus not yet covered by Phases 1 and 2 — directly affecting land pricing and entitlement timelines for developers watching mid-ring and suburban submarkets.
- **Source(s)**: [Columbus Underground — Next Phase of Zoning Reform Will Cover 43% of City](https://columbusunderground.com/next-phase-of-zoning-reform-will-cover-43-of-city-bw1/), [10TV — Columbus City Council greenlights next phase](https://www.10tv.com/article/news/local/columbus-city-council-approves-phase-two-zone-in-initiative/530-777ab85d-46d5-4974-89fa-ad20ee8f27fd)

## Social posts

### LinkedIn

Columbus real estate operators: if you're holding or underwriting near the West Broad Street BRT corridor — Franklinton, the Hilltop, anywhere along that 9.3-mile stretch to Prairie Township — your timeline just shifted.

In late April, COTA's engineering firm HNTB disclosed to the board that the West Broad Street LinkUS corridor is approximately 16 months behind schedule. Construction manager CK Construction Group separately projects the December 2028 opening will slip by 11 months.

The cause: ODOT doubled its traffic volume projections for the corridor in May 2025, after Gov. DeWine ordered state employees back to the office full-time. The change came when HNTB's design was already 90 percent complete — forcing significant redesign work.

What this means for operators and investors on the corridor:

→ Franklinton TOD thesis is intact, but the timeline is longer. If you're in a 3–5 year hold near a planned BRT station, the transit activation may no longer land during your hold period.

→ The Hilltop connectivity story is extended. Residents waiting for improved service will wait longer than the original 2028 projection.

→ Land near station areas: extend your carry assumptions. Holding costs are now longer than modeled.

→ Construction disruption along West Broad hasn't started yet. The delay gives operators more lead time to plan contingencies for ground-floor retail and accessible parking.

COTA has not issued a formal revised schedule as of early May 2026. That update is expected before the COTA board later this year.
