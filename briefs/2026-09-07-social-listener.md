**2. Columbus market softening — buyers getting breathing room, sellers still ahead**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Cautiously optimistic (buyers) / mixed (sellers)** | Frame: **Market narrative shift — content opportunity**

Multiple data points surfaced in today's scan point to a market in transition: homes are taking longer to sell, some sellers are reducing prices, and inventory is building toward levels not seen in nearly six years. The 30-year fixed rate is 6.65% (Aug 20, 2026). The regional median closed at $350,000 in July, up 2.3% YoY on 3,083 closed sales.

This is a new narrative for Columbus — the story all year has been tight supply and rising prices. The "finally some breathing room" framing is appearing in multiple brokerage and national outlet coverage, which means it is reaching community conversations.

Representative data points (search snippets, multiple outlets):

No verbatim community/Reddit quotes surfaced from multiple distinct authors (Reddit not accessible; it is a federal holiday). Volume rated STEADY; evidence is outlet-driven rather than community-forum-driven.

**3. Zone In Phase 2 — NIMBY/YIMBY fault lines deepening at neighborhood level**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Mixed — YIMBY enthusiasm citywide, organized skepticism neighborhood-level** | Frame: **Content opportunity + tension signal**

The Zone In Phase 2 city-council approval covers 43% of Columbus parcels (~66,000 properties) and could unlock ~88,000 new units. But neighborhood-level reactions are not uniformly positive. Clintonville residents — whose neighborhood is inside the Phase 2 area — have publicly questioned whether the process is driven by developer influence rather than community need.

Representative quote (WOSU Public Media reporting, search snippet — confirmed named outlet):

Representative quote (Rob Dorans, Columbus City Council President, Columbus Navigator, confirmed prior CREN sourcing):

**4. Affordable housing wage/rent gap — "the numbers look fine, our paychecks don't"**
Volume: **LIGHT-to-STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / resigned** | Frame: **Persistent content gap**

Columbus rents average $1,366/month, making the city appear affordable by national standards. But the structural mismatch is sharp: workers need to earn **$27.50/hour** to afford a two-bedroom apartment in Columbus, while the average renter earns **$19.59/hour** — a gap of nearly $8/hour. Ohio rent is outpacing wages, per a 2026 analysis cited by multiple outlets.

The community conversation around this gap is present but diffuse — it surfaces in advocacy reporting, rental-data roundups, and newsbreak-style local coverage rather than in concentrated Reddit threads or social posts. It is a real pattern in the data with a low community-conversation volume signal today (Labor Day).

Representative data points (search snippets):

> "Workers need to earn $27.50 per hour in Columbus to afford a two-bedroom apartment, yet the average renter earns just $19.59 per hour." — ApartmentList / Steadily 2026 analysis

> "Ohio rent continues to outpace wages, increasing financial insecurity." — Ohio Animal Advocates citing statewide housing research, 2026

**Content gap (persisting from Sep 6 scan):** The "Columbus is technically affordable but I'm still struggling" question remains the most underserved community question in public discourse. A CREN explainer built around the $27.50/$19.59 figure, layered onto the COHHIO 2026 Gap Report's 53,000-unit deficit, would be genuinely differentiated coverage.

**5. Ohio Brownfield Program — Sep 11 deadline generating policy attention**
Volume: **LIGHT** | Sentiment: **Neutral / interested (policy/investor audience)** | Frame: **Time-sensitive content peg**

The FY 2027 Ohio Brownfield Remediation Program application window closes September 11, 2026 — four days from today. COCIC (Central Ohio Community Improvement Corporation) is the lead entity for Franklin County and is actively scheduling discovery meetings. The current round has approximately $120 million available (authorized by HB 96, Ohio's biennium budget). Since 2012, COCIC has worked on Franklin County brownfield sites totaling $28.9M in prior grants across 6 sites.

Social volume is light — this is a professional/government audience topic, not a consumer conversation. However, the proximity of the Sep 11 deadline makes it a time-sensitive story peg for CREN's investor and developer audience.
