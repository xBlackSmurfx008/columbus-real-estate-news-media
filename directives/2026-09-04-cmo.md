   `crm-live-smoke-duplicate`, with `@example.com` emails. The only real

2. **The "+5 contact messages" were smoke tests.** All carry sources

   "Production CRM sync test. Safe to ignore."
3. **All-time leads: 2, both test records** (source `rent-find-a-home-crm-test`).
   Real leads ever: 0.
4. **The "first affiliate clicks" were not organic placement wins.** All 8
   all-time clicks are on `/resources`, several in identical-second pairs
   (2026-08-27 02:05:36 x2, 02:10:04–05 — automated), with the site itself as
   referrer. Treat affiliate revenue as unproven, not started.
5. **The publish pipeline is healthier than the 08-31 review implied.** Verified
   today: every article Sep 1–4 is `live`, zero stuck drafts in the database.
   The daily two-session pattern recovers when session 1 lacks `DATABASE_URL`.
6. **The stranded "Mercy on Main" story I ordered published was a duplicate**
   of a story live since Aug 2 — which the Aug 25 session-2 brief had already
   flagged. It was correctly never published. The waste was drafting it twice
   (Aug 27 and Aug 31), both by sessions running without `DATABASE_URL` and
   therefore blind to the duplicate check.

**The real scoreboard, all-time: 1 subscriber (the owner), 0 leads, 0 real
contacts, 0 proven affiliate clicks.** The KPI script's smoke exclusion misses
`crm-*` sources and `@example.com` emails, so the weekly report has been
presenting our own tests as growth. Principle 5: ignore psychology and you
market blind — we have been reviewing blind.

## What worked / what stalled

**Real and working: Google organic search on article pages.** Referrer-host
breakdown of `page_views` (queried today): google.com sent 24 views / 22
visitors in the last 7 days and 57 views in the last 30, plus a Bing/Yahoo/
DuckDuckGo/MSN trickle. Articles are indexed and pulling Columbus searchers.
The daily trend shows what pulls them: pageviews spiked to **50 on Aug 27 —
the Sugar restaurant's opening day**, for a story published Aug 25 — then
decayed to 8 (Sep 3) and 4 (Sep 4 so far). Timely stories published *before*
their event are our one proven traffic engine (principle 8: find demand and
position yourself in front of it).

**Real and working: the funnel pages themselves.** Audited today. The copy is
strong — `/sell/your-home` leads with relief and a specific number ("Skip the
6% fee... roughly $18,000 that stays in your pocket"), `/rent/find-a-home` is
"you"-first and free-to-you (principles 4, 9, 15, 18 all pass).

**Stalled: nothing routes the real traffic to those pages.** 0 funnel-page
views this window. Organic visitors land on articles, and the article
template's only conversion surface (verified in
`frontend/app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`, the `blog-cta` section) is one generic
"Stay Informed" newsletter block — identical on every story, never
topic-matched to the four funnels. Well-written offer pages nobody sees are
inventory, not marketing (principle 13: the offer isn't landing because it
isn't placed where people are).

**Stalled — and a trust problem: the newsletter promise is false.** That same
CTA block on every article promises "market data... delivered every Tuesday."
No email delivery integration exists (`cren-website` skill, Known Issues: "No
email delivery yet"), there is 1 real subscriber, and no newsletter has ever
been sent. Every organic reader who subscribes gets silence. Principle 23
(never sacrifice long-term trust) makes this the copy fix that outranks all
copy improvements.

**Stalled: week-over-week traffic fell** — 77 pageviews vs 119 last window
(and last window's number included smoke-test noise). With no retention loop
(no working newsletter, no social channel activated) every reader must be
re-won from search, so traffic follows the news calendar down (principle 16:
a loyal customer is worth more than any funnel — we currently retain no one).

## Directives (prioritized)

### P1 — Make the KPI report tell the truth
- Why: principles 5 and 17 (you cannot listen through noise). KPI gap: the
  snapshot above reports +8 subscribers / +5 contacts / +2 leads; queries show
  all 15 records are our own CRM tests. Two consecutive CMO reviews nearly
  built strategy on them.
- What the CTO builds: (a) purge or flag all test records in `subscribers`,
  `contacts`, `leads`, and `affiliate_clicks` — anything with source matching
  `crm-*`/`*smoke*`/`*test*` or an `@example.com` email; (b) extend
  `kpi-report.mjs` exclusions so such records can never appear in totals
  again, and route all future smoke tests through a flagged source convention;
  (c) add to the report what this review had to hand-query: referrer-host mix
  and a daily pageview trend line, so weekly reviews see channel and momentum,
  not just totals.
- Definition of done: the next weekly KPI run shows the real scoreboard (today
  that is 1 subscriber, 0 leads — painful and true); a deliberately seeded
  `crm-test` record does not appear in it; the report prints referrer mix and
  daily trend.

### P2 — Replace the false newsletter CTA with an honest, topic-matched next step
- Why: principle 23 (the false Tuesday-newsletter promise), principle 13/8
  (0 funnel-page views while articles took ~60 views this window), principle
  16 (no retention loop exists). The funnel copy already passes the sales
  skill — the missing piece is honest routing from where readers actually are.
- What the CTO builds: (a) remove or truthfully reword the "delivered every
  Tuesday" promise everywhere it appears until real email delivery ships; if
  the newsletter is the strategy, wiring delivery (e.g. Resend) and sending
  the first digest is part of this directive — no promise before capability;
  (b) make the end-of-article block topic-matched: rental/apartment/lifestyle
  stories → `/rent/find-a-home`; market-data and home-sale stories →
  `/sell/your-home`; development/investor stories → `/invest/deploy-capital`
  or `/sell/investment-property`; neighborhood stories may use the existing
  area-follow instead. One block after the body, "you" language, one specific
  outcome sentence (borrow the funnel pages' own lines), no popups; (c) log
  the block's clicks to `activation_events` so article→funnel movement is
  measurable.
- Definition of done: no live page promises anything we cannot deliver this
  week; every article renders one topic-matched block; the KPI report shows
  article→funnel clicks; at least one real (non-test) funnel-page view chain
  from an article appears in next week's snapshot.

### P3 — Publish ahead of the date, every time: build the coverage calendar into the pipeline
- Why: principle 8 and the clearest number we have: Aug 27 (Sugar opening day)
  drove 50 pageviews — 65% of this whole window's traffic in one day — because
  the story was live two days early. Search demand spikes on event dates;
  coverage that lands late misses the spike entirely. Known dates are already
  sitting in our published stories: Downtown Commission vote on the 1908 Gay
  St. demolition (Sep 22), Zone In Phase 2 comment close (Oct 24), Dublin
  Christkindlmarkt opening (Nov 21).
- What the CTO builds: (a) a coverage-calendar file or table the newsroom
  routine reads and writes — every dated milestone extracted from published
  and drafted stories, with its publish-by date (event minus 2 days) — and a
  line in the daily brief showing what is due; (b) internal links from the
  highest-traffic existing articles to their follow-ups when a calendared
  story publishes (the Sugar article at 6 views this week should point to
  the next Park Street story the day it exists); (c) owner dependency, fourth
  consecutive ask: put `DATABASE_URL` into the session-1 routine config —
  blind sessions cost us duplicate drafting twice this window (Mercy on Main,
  Corrections item 6).
- Definition of done: the calendar exists with the three dates above and is
  echoed in each daily brief; the Sep 22 Downtown Commission vote has updated
  coverage live no later than Sep 20; next week's snapshot shows google.com
  referrals at or above this week's 24 views.
