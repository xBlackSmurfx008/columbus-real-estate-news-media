**1. Rental Registry Passes — Landlords Mobilizing, Tenants Claiming Win, Legal Challenge Incoming**
Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Polarized** (landlords: resistant/angry; tenants: relieved/vindicated)
Frame: **Threat** (for operator/landlord audience) | **Content idea** (the fight is now legal, not just political)

*New to this run — not covered in Apr 30 or May 1 briefs.*

Columbus City Council voted to pass a rental registry requiring all residential landlords to register units, pay a $15/unit annual fee (cap: $1,500/complex), and submit common areas to preventative inspections every three years. The Columbus Apartment Association's attorney has signaled intent to challenge the ordinance legally, noting that landlords already submit information to the Franklin County rental registry. The Buckeye Institute published a report arguing the registry will lead to higher rents. Interfaith tenant-advocacy group BREAD backed the bill. This is a multi-voice, actively contested community fight with a legal proceeding now likely.

> "The current complaint-based system of code enforcement is not adequate to address the issues of today's housing market." — Councilmember Nick Bankston, proposing the registry (WOSU, April 15, 2026 — wosu.org/politics-government/2026-04-15/columbus-city-council-proposes-rental-registry-to-combat-problem-landlords)

> "The Columbus Rental Registry Will Lead to Higher Rents." — Buckeye Institute, research report headline (buckeyeinstitute.org/research/detail/the-buckeye-institute-columbus-rental-registry-will-lead-to-higher-rents)

> "A lawyer representing the Columbus Apartment Association said the information is already submitted to the Franklin County rental registry and plans to challenge council's action." — NBC4 (nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-oks-rental-registry-to-hold-landlords-accountable/)

Coverage sourced from: WOSU (April 15, 2026), NBC4, ABC6, Buckeye Institute, BREAD, nationaltoday.com (February 23, 2026 hearing), Columbus Mennonite (community voice) — multiple distinct outlets and organizations.

**4. "Ohio Is Too Expensive" — Affordability Frustration Runs Louder Than the "Hot Market" Headline**
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / resigned**
Frame: **Threat** | **Content idea** (the gap between "Columbus is booming" trade coverage and what residents are actually experiencing)

*Carries forward from Apr 30 affordable-housing theme; reframed here with the state-legislative angle and individual-resident voice now active.*

Ohio House Democrats' affordability bill package (introduced April 2, 2026) is circulating in Columbus community discussions as the mid-May legislative calendar approaches. Individual residents are on record saying they cannot make home-buying math work at current prices and 6.25% mortgage rates. The Affordable Housing Alliance of Central Ohio's executive director has issued an explicit "heading toward California" warning that has circulated across multiple outlets. Columbus has a 53,000-unit affordable rental deficit per COHHIO's 2026 Gap Report.

> "'Ohio is too expensive.' Ohio House Democrats plan to introduce series of affordability bills." — Ohio Capital Journal headline, April 2, 2026 (ohiocapitaljournal.com/2026/04/02/ohio-is-too-expensive-ohio-house-democrats-plan-to-introduce-series-of-affordability-bills/)

> "A $200,000 home is $2,000 a month in mortgage, interest and insurance, and that is almost impossible without some kind of assistance to help families buy that home." — Columbus city official, quoted in coverage of down payment programs (nationaltoday.com, April 8, 2026)

> "If the pace of affordable housing development doesn't escalate radically, come 2040, we're going to have a housing market that looks a lot more like California than it does Columbus." — Executive Director, Affordable Housing Alliance of Central Ohio (multiple outlets, 2026)

Coverage sourced from: Ohio Capital Journal, nationaltoday.com, COHHIO Gap Report (cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/), CRE Daily, NBC4, Stacker, AHACO — multiple distinct outlets.

**6. Data Center Backlash — Columbus-Area Resident Voices Going Statewide, Ballot Deadline Tightens**
Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / alarmed**
Frame: **Threat** (July 1 signature deadline now 8 weeks away; New Albany land market in political crosshairs)

*Rolls over from May 1 brief (Theme 1) — updated with Spectrum News 1 April 24 coverage of residents escalating to statewide action.*

Spectrum News 1 reported April 24 that Columbus-area residents opposing AI data center expansion are "taking the fight statewide." A multi-speaker public panel aired April 22. The statewide petition (Ohio Prohibition of Data Center Construction Amendment) requires 413,488 valid signatures by July 1 — now eight weeks away. Real estate-specific concerns cited in community discussions: rising water and electric bills, farmland being bought out, residential property value impacts, and noise. Columbus is projected to become the #2 Great Lakes data center hub. The New Albany hyperscale land market (Meta, Vantage $2B/192MW, Microsoft, Google 85-acre acquisition, EdgeConneX) sits directly in the middle of this political fight.

> "Many have seen the industry's impact in rising power and water bills, and data centers have drawn criticism for years — namely, that they use too much water, and don't deliver many permanent jobs in exchange for the generous tax breaks they've traditionally received." — Signal Ohio, "Ohio's data center boom is running into political resistance" (signalohio.org/ohio-data-center-boom-faces-political-resistence/)

> "Your electric rates go up. The farmland is getting bought out." — Columbus-area resident, characterizing local data center impact (indexed search summary, Signal Ohio / Statehouse News Bureau, 2026)

> "Proposed data centers are roiling otherwise sleepy public hearings and raising difficult questions about electricity, water, and noise." — The Statehouse News Bureau (statenews.org/section/the-ohio-newsroom/2026-02-26/ohio-towns-are-pushing-back-against-data-centers-to-varying-degrees-of-success)
