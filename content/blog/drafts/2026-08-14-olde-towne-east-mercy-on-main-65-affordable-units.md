# 65 Affordable Apartments Planned for Olde Towne East, City Review Set

**Date:** August 14, 2026 | **Category:** Development | **Area:** Olde Towne East

---

A Cincinnati developer with central Ohio roots is proposing Mercy on Main, a 65-unit affordable housing building at 907-917 E. Main St. in Olde Towne East. The $22.8 million project secured a competitive 9% state tax credit award in May and has a Columbus planning review scheduled August 20.

## What Mercy on Main Would Build

The five-story building at 907-917 E. Main St. would add 65 income-restricted apartments to one of Columbus's most actively gentrifying neighborhoods. The unit mix leans toward smaller households: eight studios, 29 one-bedroom units, 18 two-bedroom units, and 10 three-bedroom units, [as first reported by Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/five-story-building-proposed-in-olde-towne-east-bw1/).

All 65 apartments would be income-restricted. Seven units would be reserved for households earning no more than 30% of area median income, 10 units at up to 50%, 25 units at up to 60%, and 23 units at up to 70%. The 70% AMI tier — sometimes called the workforce housing band — serves households that typically earn too much for public housing but too little for market-rate rents in a neighborhood where average one-bedroom apartments in Olde Towne East listed above $1,200 a month as of mid-2026, [according to Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/olde-towne-east-columbus-oh/apartments/).

## Financing: Tax Credits and a City Grant

The project won a reservation through Ohio's competitive 9% Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) program — one of the most selective sources of housing finance in the state. The [Ohio Housing Finance Agency awarded more than $39.1 million in 9% LIHTC](https://ohiohome.org/news/releases/2026/9percent-taxcredits.aspx) to 25 Ohio developments in May 2026, creating or preserving 1,468 apartments statewide.

Mercy on Main's share of that award is roughly $14.7 million in federal tax credit equity — about 65% of the $22.8 million development cost. The remaining financing includes a $4 million permanent mortgage and $3.25 million from the city of Columbus's HOME Investment Partnerships program, which directs federal community development funds to affordable housing.

At $22.8 million for 65 units, the implied per-unit cost is approximately $350,800. That tracks with the typical range for tax-credit-financed construction in Columbus, which runs higher than surface-comparable market-rate projects because of prevailing wage requirements and a complex financing stack.

## The Developer: Hometown Roots, NFL Career, Second Act

Mercy on Main is named for Mercy Okoronkwo, the grandmother of Kingsley + Co. founder and president Chinedum Ndukwe, [according to NBC4](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/retired-nfl-player-behind-affordable-housing-proposal-in-columbus-olde-towne-east/).

Ndukwe grew up in central Ohio, played college ball at Notre Dame, and spent seven years in the NFL with the Cincinnati Bengals and Oakland Raiders. After retiring from professional football, he founded Kingsley + Co. in Cincinnati, where the firm has completed two previous 9% LIHTC developments — Victory Vistas and Blair Lofts. Mercy on Main would be the company's third 9% LIHTC project and its first in Columbus.

Ndukwe has cited his central Ohio upbringing as part of his motivation for expanding to Columbus. The project's name ties that personal thread directly to the building.

## Olde Towne East: A Neighborhood Worth Fighting Over

Olde Towne East sits east of Downtown Columbus, roughly between Livingston Avenue to the south and Franklin Park to the east. The neighborhood has some of the city's most intact Victorian-era housing stock, and its proximity to the Short North and German Village has made it one of the more closely watched — and gentrifying — east-side corridors.

That trajectory is part of why affordable housing proposals here carry particular weight. Median home values in [Olde Towne East](/areas/olde-towne-east) have risen faster than the Columbus average over the past several years, and longtime residents face rising rents that the city's broader housing supply crunch has intensified.

The Near East Area Commission and the Olde Towne East Neighborhood Association both had early discussions with Kingsley + Co. about the project, [per Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/five-story-building-proposed-in-olde-towne-east-bw1/). Columbus Underground has also described the path to approval as "contentious" — language that typically reflects organized neighborhood opposition at the commission level, though the specifics of that opposition are not fully on the public record ahead of the August 20 review.

## What Comes Next

The city of Columbus has scheduled August 20 for a review of the preliminary site plan. That hearing is a procedural step — it does not constitute final approval — but it gives neighbors and commissioners a formal opportunity to weigh in on the building's design, parking, and block-level impacts.

Kingsley + Co. still needs final city approval to break ground. Tax-credit projects typically close financing and begin construction 12 to 18 months after a credit reservation, which would put a groundbreaking in 2027 or 2028 if the project advances without major delays.

For Columbus's [broader affordable housing debate](/topics/local-politics), Mercy on Main is a small-stakes test of a large-stakes question: whether competitive tax credit dollars can hold ground in the city's most gentrified corridors, or whether the economics of east-side land inevitably push lower-income housing further from the center.
