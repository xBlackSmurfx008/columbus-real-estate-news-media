### 1. Fall Market Reality Check — Buyers Are More Selective, But It's Still a Seller's Market (Barely)
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Mixed (cautious optimism among sellers; frustrated-but-engaged among buyers) | **Frame:** Content idea + investor signal

The Columbus market entering September 2026 is not the aggressive seller's market it was in 2023–2024. Inventory is up approximately 10.8% year over year, median days on market has stretched to roughly 40 days, and approximately 26% of active listings have had at least one price reduction. Buyers have become materially more selective — agents are describing a "short, selective fall market" rather than a broad September surge.

That said, the region still carries only 2.4 months of supply, which Columbus REALTORS characterizes as a seller's market. The distinction matters for how owners should price: sellers who chase the spring-peak comp are sitting; sellers pricing to the current comp are moving.

National sentiment around Columbus remains positive. The metro added 102,000 projected jobs through 2030, 60% of Midwest NAR survey respondents plan a purchase within two years, and 20% of Columbus demand is now from out-of-state buyers. That base demand is keeping the market from truly softening even as buyer tolerance for overpriced listings has collapsed.

> "For sellers this fall, there are buyers out there, but they're no longer chasing every house." — *The Mancini Group 2026 Central Ohio Housing Market Forecast, confirmed in search index (themancinigroupsells.com)*

> "Central Ohio will have a fall market in 2026 — but a short, selective fall market rather than a big September boom." — *The Mancini Group 2026 Central Ohio Housing Market Forecast, confirmed in search index*

**Content gap:** "Is it still a seller's market in Columbus? What September 2026 data actually shows." — A clear-eyed market snapshot separating the data from the narrative. Both buyers and sellers are confused by conflicting signals; a plain-English explainer indexed with "Columbus real estate fall 2026" would earn strong search traffic. Ideal for investor and active buyer audiences.

### 2. Broad & Gould East Side Condos — First Visible $500M Bond Project Breaks Ground
**Volume:** Steady (specific project news; not a viral moment) | **Sentiment:** Cautiously positive (affordable ownership) / watchful (is this a pattern or a one-off?) | **Frame:** Content idea + watchdog opportunity

On August 27, 2026, the City of Columbus broke ground on the Broad & Gould Condominiums at the southeast corner of East Broad Street and South Gould Road. The four-story building will contain 23 two- and three-bedroom condominiums — 950 to 1,300 square feet — with expected sale prices of approximately $199,000.

The city committed $3 million from the $500 million Affordable Housing Bond program plus $1.1 million in homebuyer assistance, making this one of the first publicly traceable deployments of bond funds since the Mayor's February 2026 framework announcement.

The Broad & Gould project matters because it represents affordable-ownership housing rather than rental — a rare product that builds generational equity for buyers at or below area median income. At $199,000, these condos price below the central Columbus median ($320,000+) and give first-time buyers a credible path.

Community conversation is watching whether this represents the city's intended pattern of investment — smaller projects with ownership outcomes — or whether the bulk of bond capital will flow toward institutional rental development, which is faster to execute but does not build resident equity.

> "23 two- and three-bedroom condos ranging from roughly 950 to 1,300 square feet, with homes expected to sell for about $199,000" — *NBC4 / WCMH-TV, confirmed in search index (nbc4i.com)*

**Content gap:** "Broad & Gould is Columbus's first visible bond investment. Here's how to track where the rest of $500M goes." — A story anchored to the groundbreaking that teaches readers what to look for: the city's bond investment tracker, what types of projects are eligible, and how community members can flag misspending. Strong accountability angle; builds loyal readership among affordability-focused audiences.

### 3. New Construction Wave — Downtown and Near-South Has 10+ Projects Active Simultaneously
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Positive (urbanists/developers) / Cautious (renters tracking displacement risk) | **Frame:** Content idea + opportunity for map-based coverage

The downtown and near-south Columbus pipeline is unusually dense right now:

- **Stone Ridge at High Street** (NRP Group): 150 units, 45 W. Barthman Ave. (South Side, ~3 miles south of downtown). Broke ground August 27, 2026.
- **Brewery District residential complex**: 6-story, 106 units, pool deck on second floor, parking garage.
- **Scioto Peninsula mixed-income tower**: 7-story, 137 units (mix of affordable and market rate), Capital and Starling streets. Completion expected summer 2027.
- **AspireColumbus**: Former United Way HQ, $29M, 5-story, middle- and low-wage households + disability-accessible units.
- **Opportunity Pointe** (Downtown Commission-approved): 166 units, start expected next summer, completion fall 2027.
- **Lofts at 40 Long** (Woda Cooper): $65.7M, 121-unit affordable complex, 7-story historic conversion, work begun October 2025.

North Market's 32-story tower and the $600M Capitol Square redevelopment are in the pipeline but not yet under construction.

No single project dominates conversation, but the cumulative pipeline is substantial — and it raises a legitimate question for the community about sequencing: all of these units will hit the market at roughly the same time (2027), which could produce a lease-up competition that temporarily drives concessions, then stabilize rents once absorbed.

### 4. New Albany Premium Market Holding, But Land Speculation Has Cooled
**Volume:** Light | **Sentiment:** Neutral to cautiously optimistic | **Frame:** Investor-facing content idea + threat (for buyers who over-paid 2022–2024 on speculation)

The Intel production timeline (2030–2031) is established; no new news on the delay itself. What is new is more nuanced: the speculation window has definitively closed, but the underlying housing market driven by construction-phase workers and executive relocation demand remains strong.

Key data points confirmed in search-indexed sources:
- New Albany median home price: approximately $625,000 (2026)
- In June 2026, New Albany led Franklin County in high-end transactions, with the top residential sale at $3.2 million.
- Land that appreciated 10x between 2022 and 2024 is now "priced for patient capital rather than quick flips."
- Current demand in the northeast corridor is described as driven by Intel construction-phase employment (on-site workers) and executive relocation, not speculative anticipation.

The community of buyers being displaced from inner Columbus by price appreciation and looking eastward toward New Albany, Pataskala, and Westerville is growing — and their price ceiling hits at approximately $450,000–$500,000, well below the New Albany median. This displacement cascade is not yet well-covered locally.
