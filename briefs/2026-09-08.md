### LinkedIn (1,200–2,200 chars)

Ohio is putting $120 million on the table to clean up contaminated properties — and the deadline is Thursday.

The Ohio Brownfield Remediation Program's current application round closes September 11 at 5 p.m. For Columbus operators and developers with eligible sites, the clock matters: the program runs through a county-level structure, and Franklin County's designated lead — COCIC, the Central Ohio Community Improvement Corporation — is already scheduling discovery meetings.

COCIC's track record in prior rounds: $28.9 million across six Franklin County sites. Three of those are Columbus addresses — the Eastland Mall site ($2 million for asbestos abatement and demolition of eight structures), Weinland Park Apartments ($1.6 million, now in development for 186 units across two seven-story buildings), and Lofts at 40 Long ($1 million to remediate the historic downtown YMCA building).

Two things worth noting:

First, the data center angle. Ohio has about 200 data centers, more than half in the Columbus region. Brownfields have emerged as a preferred site type for data center developers looking for large, contiguous parcels with existing infrastructure. Aligned Data Centers has facilities on two former Ohio brownfield sites — Coshocton County and Erie County. Whether that pattern reaches Franklin County is open.

Second, the cleanup-to-construction gap. Brownfield grants fund assessment and remediation, not construction. A site that receives cleanup funding still needs a full capital stack to put a shovel in the ground. Cleanup removes the contamination barrier; it doesn't close the deal.

If you have a Franklin County site that may be eligible, contact COCIC at [email protected] to schedule a discovery meeting. September 11 is the gate for this round.
