**3. ⚠️ COHHIO 2026 Gap Report: 45% of Ohio Renters Cost-Burdened; Unhoused Population May Grow 10,000**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / Alarmed**  
Frame: **Threat + Content idea** — Per the COHHIO 2026 Gap Report (released this cycle), 45% of Ohio's 1.58 million renters are paying more than they can afford in rent. Of the state's 422,098 extremely low-income (ELI) renters, 73% spend more than half their income on housing — up from 66% five years ago. Separately, Columbus Underground/Ohio Capital Journal reporting indicates Ohio's unhoused population may grow by 10,000 in 2026. These numbers are the demand side of the housing crisis and are the accountability frame for the $500M affordable housing bond (May 10 brief, Theme 6): if 73% of ELI renters are already severely burdened and the bond's $150M preservation bucket allocation hasn't been announced, the question of who actually benefits from the bond is more urgent than ever. Columbus home prices are up 51% since 2020 (CRE Daily). Rents up ~3.1% YoY. A Columbus Underground headline this scan — "Report Shows Rents Are Down in Columbus — Are They Really?" — signals community confusion: market-wide data shows modest declines in some segments while individual renters experience continuing pressure. This disconnect is a high-SEO content gap.

> "Of Ohio's 422,098 extremely low-income renters, 73% of these households spend more than half of their income on rent — up from 66% five years ago." — COHHIO 2026 Gap Report ([cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/](https://cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/))
