## Executive Thesis

Columbus Real Estate News should not try to beat Zillow, Apartments.com, Realtor.com, Redfin, or CoStar at national listing inventory. CREN should win a narrower and more defensible position:

CREN should become the trusted Columbus housing intelligence layer: local reporting, clear market data, neighborhood context, apartment and service-provider verification, and sponsor-safe advertising for people already making Columbus housing decisions.

The business should emulate:

- Zillow/Realtor.com/Redfin: intent capture, data transparency, calculators, saved searches, and consumer decision paths.
- Apartments.com/Zumper/Apartment List/RentCafe: renter self-service, rich apartment profiles, current availability, fast mobile workflows, and property-manager dashboards.
- The Real Deal/Inman/Bisnow/Urbanize: authority with real estate professionals, advertising around high-intent industry readership, events, sponsored content, and brand studio services.
- 6AM City/Block Club Chicago/Axios Local: newsletter habit, local identity, neighborhood pride, transparent ad products, and audience-first trust.
- ATTOM/Cotality/ICE/Yardi Matrix/HouseCanary/Cherre: source-labeled data, quality controls, update cadence, exportable intelligence, and paid professional data products.

CREN should avoid:

- Unlabeled native advertising.
- Unverified traffic, subscriber, open-rate, or lead claims.
- Generic AI content that does not add Columbus-specific value.
- Fair-housing-risky neighborhood language.
- Referral-fee or capital-introduction products without counsel.
- Self-service profile edits without proof, version history, and review controls.

## What People Most Want From This Category

The pattern across major real estate portals, rental marketplaces, professional media, local newsletters, and property-data companies is consistent:

1. Accurate, current inventory and facts.
   Users want listings, rents, availability, fees, photos, school/transit/location context, and market metrics that show source and freshness.

2. Less search fatigue.
   Renter and buyer products increasingly emphasize matching, quizzes, saved preferences, alerts, and curated shortlists instead of endless undifferentiated cards.

3. Clear local context.
   National portals can show inventory, but users still need local interpretation: what changed, what is planned, what is delayed, what a rent or price means, and what official source supports it.

4. Mobile-first utility.
   The highest-traffic real estate sites are heavily mobile. CREN's key paths should be fast on phones: search, subscribe, contact, claim profile, advertise, compare apartments, save area, and submit correction.

5. Trustworthy data methodology.
   Successful data brands state update cadence, metric definitions, geography, source, limitations, and whether data is direct, modeled, estimated, or user-submitted.

6. Self-service for professionals.
   Property managers, agents, vendors, advertisers, and sponsors expect dashboards for profile edits, assets, leads, reporting, invoices, proof, and support.

7. Transparent commercial relationships.
   Audiences tolerate advertising when it is labeled, relevant, useful, and separated from editorial judgment.

8. Outcome reporting for advertisers.
   Sponsors want proof: impressions, clicks, leads, call/email clicks, profile views, newsletter placement dates, geography, creative used, and next-step recommendations.

9. Community identity.
   Neighborhood-focused local media succeeds when readers feel the coverage belongs to their area, not just a citywide commodity feed.

10. Human review where stakes are high.
   The strongest model is automation plus governance: AI can collect, summarize, validate, and alert, but humans approve legal pages, sponsored claims, corrections, fair-housing-sensitive copy, and capital/investment language.

## Patterns To Turn Into CREN Product Requirements

### Consumer Requirements

- Search should start with intent: rent, buy, sell, invest, advertise, list a business, or research an area.
- Area pages should show last refreshed date, source notes, top articles, rental/buyer/seller paths, services, and official-data context.
- Apartment pages should show address, manager/owner, verified source, last verified date, unit mix, rents, fees, concessions, photos, pet/parking policy, tour/apply links, contact path, and fair-housing-safe copy.
- Renter tools should include a short matching quiz, rent budget calculator, move-in cost checklist, fee glossary, saved shortlist, and alerts.
- Buyer/seller tools should include price-band reality, affordability calculator, "sell or rent" guide, renovation/payoff guide, and agent contact request with disclosures.
- Every data card should show source, period, geography, update date, and limitation.
- Every sponsored item should be labeled before click and on destination.

### Advertiser Requirements

- Advertisers need products they understand: newsletter text ad, email banner, sponsored article, area sponsor, apartment spotlight, enhanced directory listing, market report sponsor, event sponsor, and custom brand studio.
- Each product needs specs, price or quote basis, inventory calendar, due date, approval workflow, labeling rule, reporting metrics, refund/make-good policy, and prohibited claims.
- Advertisers need a dashboard to update profile info, upload assets, approve insertion-order terms, see campaign status, see reporting, and respond to review flags.
- Sales needs a CRM view with advertiser category, geography, package fit, contract status, renewal date, policy risk, last contact, and next action.

### Editorial Requirements

- CREN must build trust by explaining Columbus-specific changes faster and more clearly than national sites.
- Core beats should be: apartments, development, neighborhood market shifts, housing affordability, public records/permits, policy/zoning, agents/brokers, home services, commercial corridors, and investment property.
- Story format should include "What changed", "Why it matters", "Source", "Who is affected", "What to verify", and "Next date to watch".
- Sponsored and affiliate content must never buy conclusions, rankings, corrections outcomes, or newsroom source treatment.

### Data Requirements

- Build one canonical metric layer for home values, list prices, inventory, days on market, rents, permits, apartment supply, population, household income, taxes, and transit.
- Store source, license, permitted use, collection date, refresh cadence, geography, metric definition, confidence level, and public/private visibility.
- Create a market-data QA job that flags mismatched values across homepage, market-data page, articles, embeds, newsletters, and social snippets.
- Publish only metrics whose source and methodology are approved for public use.

## Backend Profiles CREN Should Support

### Reader and Member Profile

Purpose: personalization, saved content, newsletters, alerts, and trust.

Required fields:

- Email, name, role, password hash or auth identity, status.
- Preferred areas, topics, buyer/renter/seller/investor intent.
- Saved articles, saved areas, saved apartment profiles, saved service providers.
- Newsletter cadence, opt-in source, consent text version, privacy version, terms version.
- Export/delete/correction request status.

Self-service:

- Update name, interests, preferred area, newsletter preferences, saved items, password/session settings.
- Request data export, deletion, or correction.

### Agent and Broker Profile

Purpose: professional directory, advertising, lead routing if approved, and sponsor packages.

Required fields:

- Legal name, public display name, brokerage, license number, license source URL, service areas, specialties, languages, bio, headshot, website, phone/email routing preferences.
- Claimant authority, proof status, fair-housing certification version, lead-disclosure version, paid/sponsored status.
- Compliance flags and reviewer notes.

Self-service:

- Update factual profile fields, photos, links, service areas, specialties, and lead preferences.
- Upload proof and request verification.
- View lead/reporting metrics if permitted.

Human gate:

- License/credential changes, disputed claims, sponsored ranking language, superlatives, guarantees, or fair-housing-sensitive copy.

### Apartment Community Profile

Purpose: apartment intelligence, renter service, and apartment advertising.

Required fields:

- Property name, legal owner, manager, address, area, parcel ID if available, property type, unit count, unit mix, rent ranges, availability source, fees, concessions, deposit, pet policy, parking, accessibility features, amenities, utilities, tour/apply links, photos, floor plans, last verified date.
- Claimant authority, proof records, source URLs, feed source, paid/sponsored status, fair-housing certification version.

Self-service:

- Update availability, rents, concessions, fees, policies, photos, floor plans, tour links, contact routing, and lead preferences.
- Upload proof and mark data source.
- View profile views, clicks, leads, and stale-data reminders.

Human gate:

- First publication, ownership/manager changes, fees/concessions that could mislead, fair-housing-sensitive copy, and disputed availability.

### Property Manager and Landlord Profile

Purpose: rental listing review, service directory, and owner/tenant contact clarity.

Required fields:

- Legal business name, public name, portfolio type, license/registration if applicable, service geography, tenant/prospect contact path, complaint/escalation path, authority proof, insurance/licensing notes, fair-housing certification.

Self-service:

- Update business facts, service areas, portfolio types, listing relationships, photos/logos, contact rules, and proof.

Human gate:

- Claims about screening, guaranteed approval, eviction policy, rent-setting, or protected-class language.

### Developer and Builder Profile

Purpose: development tracker, new-home/new-apartment sponsorship, and source records.

Required fields:

- Company, legal entity, projects, project stage, approvals, permits, unit counts, site plans/renderings, public-record links, media contact, sales/leasing contact, project status history.

Self-service:

- Submit project updates, renderings, public-record links, availability, press contacts, and sponsorship interest.

Human gate:

- Project status, unit counts, approvals, timelines, public incentives, and renderings not already public or approved.

### Vendor and Service Provider Profile

Purpose: find-a-pro directory and sponsor-safe service guides.

Required fields:

- Legal business name, display name, category, service areas, credentials, licenses, insurance, proof, public contact path, limitations, dispute contact, owner/claimant authority, lead-routing consent.

Self-service:

- Update factual fields, upload proof, select service areas, see status, buy/inquire about packages, respond to disputes.

Human gate:

- Credential claims, guarantees, pricing promises, awards, "best" claims, and regulated services.

### Advertiser Profile

Purpose: paid media operations.

Required fields:

- Legal advertiser name, billing contact, campaign contact, category, landing pages, creative assets, substantiation files, insertion-order status, policy acknowledgements, payment/invoice status, refund/make-good notes.

Self-service:

- Submit campaigns, upload assets, approve terms, see flight dates, view performance, request renewal, request cancellation/refund review.

Human gate:

- Ad approval, sponsored article publication, claim substantiation, prohibited categories, fair-housing-sensitive ads, and refund/make-good decisions.

## Sales and Advertising Plan

### Initial ICPs

- Apartment communities and property managers with vacancies, lease-ups, concessions, or new amenities.
- Home service providers: inspectors, movers, lenders, title, insurance, staging, renovation, HVAC, roofing, landscaping, photographers, cleaning, junk removal.
- Agents and teams focused on Columbus neighborhoods.
- Developers, builders, commercial brokers, and consultants needing visibility around projects.
- Local banks, credit unions, law firms, CPA firms, and wealth/investment groups serving homeowners or real estate investors, subject to compliance review.

### Offer Ladder

- Free: basic listing, correction path, newsletter subscription, area alerts.
- Entry paid: newsletter text ad, email banner, enhanced directory listing.
- Mid paid: apartment/community spotlight, area sponsor, service-guide sponsor, sponsored article with label, market pulse sponsor.
- High paid: founding sponsor bundle, quarterly report sponsorship, event sponsorship, custom brand studio package.
- Future professional: paid data/report access, investor briefing, apartment intelligence, CRM/reporting dashboard.

### Sales Motion

1. Build a verified media kit using only confirmed traffic, subscriber, open-rate, click-rate, geography, and lead metrics.
2. Create one-page product sheets for each package with specs, price range, label rule, due dates, and reporting.
3. Build target account lists by category and geography: apartments, PMCs, agents, builders, vendors, banks, title, law, insurance, and developers.
4. Use editorial calendar to sell around high-intent themes: lease-up season, tax season, school-year moves, spring listing season, permit/development updates, year-end market outlook.
5. Start with managed sales before full self-service so CREN can learn proof, claims, and reporting needs.
6. Move simple newsletter/display products into self-service only after policy, checkout, refunds, asset review, and reporting are stable.

### Revenue Milestones

- Month 1: verified media kit, first sponsor-safe products, 25 warm prospects, 5 discovery calls.
- Month 2: 3 to 5 founding advertisers, first sponsored newsletter placements, first enhanced directory listings.
- Month 3: apartment spotlight pilot, campaign reports, renewal calendar, audience survey.
- Month 4 to 6: Columbus State of the Market event, service-guide sponsorships, neighborhood packages, profile claim portal beta.
- Month 7 to 12: paid data/report product, apartment intelligence package, advertiser dashboard, repeatable renewals.
