### 1. Edwards Companies Shelves $600M Capitol Square Renaissance — Downtown's Largest Mixed-Use Project Paused

- **What**: Edwards Companies announced it is shelving the Capitol Square Renaissance, a roughly $600 million mixed-use redevelopment covering approximately 10 acres south of East Broad Street in downtown Columbus. The project would have delivered more than 1,000 residential units, nine restaurants, nearly 200,000 SF of office space, and multiple parking decks. CEO Jeff Edwards stated: "While we remain confident in the long-term trajectory of downtown Columbus, today's market conditions indicate that now is not the right time for this project." The project had secured up to $33 million in state tax credits and received local approval in December 2025 for a 12-story, 218-unit apartment building at 33 S. Fourth St. Whether that component still proceeds independently is unconfirmed. [needs source] The Preston Centre (former PNC Tower conversion) was already completed as part of the corridor effort.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: This is the largest single downtown Columbus development pause of the current cycle. A local real estate expert told WOSU that developers recognize downtown is "saturated with product" and need to pause to let existing supply absorb. [expert name needs attribution before publish] For Columbus operators, the signal is clear: the near-term downtown delivery stack — Merchant Building (174 units + hotel), Centennial on High (148 units) — is arriving first, and the absorption test is now live. The Capitol Square shelving is a data point on where institutional development risk tolerance sits in spring 2026.

### 3. Columbus Industrial Market Q1 2026: Vacancy at 7.2%, Six Consecutive Years of Rent Growth

- **What**: Columbus's industrial market closed Q1 2026 with vacancy at 7.2%, down from 9.7% at end-of-2024, per JLL and NAI Ohio Equities reporting. The market posted approximately 9.4 million SF of positive net absorption through Q3 2025, with momentum continuing into 2026. Asking rents have climbed for six consecutive years. Tenant demand skews toward Class A modern logistics; 3PL, advanced manufacturing supply chains, and data center logistics are the primary demand drivers. Polaris and Worthington leasing activity helped offset losses in other submarkets.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: While downtown mixed-use faces a pause, Columbus industrial fundamentals remain among the tightest in the Midwest. For Columbus industrial investors and developers, six straight years of rent growth and a vacancy below 8% justify continued Class A development near I-70, I-71, and the Intel corridor in New Albany — even as the city's urban core recalibrates.
- **Source(s)**: [Columbus Industrial Market Dynamics Q1 2026 — JLL](https://www.jll.com/en-us/insights/market-dynamics/columbus-industrial), [Columbus Industrial Market Snapshot Q1 2026 — NAI Ohio Equities](https://industrialpropertyteam.com/columbus-industrial-market-snapshot-q1-2026/), [Columbus Industrial Market Accelerates Amid Mega-Projects — REBusinessOnline](https://rebusinessonline.com/columbus-industrial-market-accelerates-amid-mega-projects-strategic-growth/)

### 5. Columbus Multifamily Permitting: 9,548 Units in 2025, #9 Nationally, Record Total

- **What**: Columbus ranked #9 nationally for multifamily permitting in 2025, issuing 9,548 units — its highest total on record and a 36% year-over-year increase, per GlobeSt and AHACO reporting. The metro issued permits at approximately 42 units per 10,000 residents. The Building Industry Association of Central Ohio (BIACO) notes the region needs roughly 14,000 new homes annually; current construction is delivering approximately half that total.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Record permitting confirms a wave of supply completing in 2026–2027. The gap between 9,548 permitted and 14,000 needed — paired with the Capitol Square pause — is the clearest argument for continued suburban and mid-ring development outside the downtown core. Operators in Franklinton, Linden, and near the Intel corridor in New Albany carry the most attractive forward-looking supply/demand ratios.
- **Source(s)**: [Record Surge in Multifamily Permits Pushes Columbus Into Top 10 — GlobeSt](https://www.globest.com/2025/10/01/record-surge-in-multifamily-permits-pushes-columbus-into-top-10-metros/), [Columbus Ranks 5th Nationally in Multifamily Permits — AHACO](https://www.ahaco.org/ahaconews/columbus-ranks-5th-nationally-in-multifamily-housing-permits), [Report: Columbus Metro Among Areas With Highest Rate of Apartment Permits — NBC4](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/data-desk-ohio/report-columbus-metro-among-areas-with-highest-rate-of-apartment-permits-issued/)
