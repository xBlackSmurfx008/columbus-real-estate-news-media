#### 5. Rent Control Battle — Petition vs. State Preemption
**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Polarized (advocates: urgent; industry: opposed)
**Frame:** Threat (if you cover landlords) / Opportunity (if you cover tenants/advocacy)

Columbus Coalition on Rent Control has filed signatures toward a citizen ordinance (needs 5,218 valid signatures). Ohio REALTORS are leading opposition and have previously killed statewide rent control via H.B. 430 (2022). The Steadily and Hemlane explainer pages are high-traffic destinations for both landlords checking legal exposure and tenants checking rights — indicating genuine public search demand on this topic. Multiple distinct advocacy voices (Jonathan Beard, Joe Motil, Tyrone Thomas, Noel Williams/B.R.E.A.D.) are active in public forums.

**Quotes (verbatim from indexed excerpts):**
- *"When the group collects 5,218 valid petition signatures from registered Columbus voters, council can choose to either adopt the proposed ordinance and pass it into law, or put the issue on the ballot for a vote."* — Columbus Free Press
- *"Ohio REALTORS have testified that rent control policies decrease housing supply, increase housing prices and, most notably, hurt the people the policy is supposed to help."* — Ohio REALTORS statement, 2026
- *"In June 2022, the Ohio General Assembly passed H.B. 430, which had an amendment prohibiting cities from enacting rent control."* — Hemlane / Steadily, 2026

**Content gap → explainer:** "Can Columbus Actually Pass Rent Control? A Plain-English Explainer on the Petition, State Law, and What Could Actually Change." — high civic interest, zero neutral explainers in local CRE press.

**Quotes (verbatim from indexed excerpts):**

- *"The group that wants Ohio voters to abolish property taxes almost certainly won't make the fall ballot, even though the deadline to submit signatures is more than two months away."* — WOUB, Apr 24 2026
- *"Ohio townships would be hard pressed to continue to operate fire/EMS services, road and bridge improvements, police protection and pay their debts should the property tax be eliminated."* — Butler County Auditor statement, 2026

**Content gap → explainer:** "Ohio's Property Tax Abolishment Push Explained: What It Would Mean for Columbus Homeowners (and Why It Probably Won't Happen)" — deflates panic, serves search intent, educationally durable even after deadline passes.
