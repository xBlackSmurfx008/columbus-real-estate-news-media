A new bar opened in Weinland Park last month. It has vinyl records, cocktails under $12, and zero televisions.

Let Me Roll It opened at 132 E. 5th Ave. in Weinland Park, with a soft open in mid-July and a grand opening in late July 2026. The bar comes from Conor Stratton — who co-owns the Ace of Cups music venue in North Columbus — and his partner Sarah Fleming, as first reported by [WhatNow Columbus](https://whatnow.com/columbus/restaurants/new-cocktail-bar-let-me-roll-it-opening-near-italian-village-from-ace-of-cups-owner/).

The space was most recently a hookah lounge. Stratton and Fleming rebuilt it from the floor up.

## What the Bar Is

The concept is built around the 1970s. The aesthetic, the music format, and the philosophy all trace back to that era.

Records and cassettes provide the soundtrack. There is no streaming service, no playlist algorithm, no background music from a phone. Someone puts a record on and it plays all the way through.

The name is a nod to the Beatles — specifically the Paul McCartney track "Let It Be" and a shared love of music between Stratton and his father, according to [Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/new-cocktail-bar-launching-in-weinland-park-we1/).

The bar program focuses on classics: Old Fashioneds, Martinis, Negronis. Most drinks stay under $12. Seasonal and house cocktails fill out the menu. The goal is a neighborhood bar — the kind where you know the bartender and you actually talk to the person next to you.

No televisions. No sports scores scrolling across the walls. Stratton told [614NOW](https://614now.com/2026/food-drink/ace-of-cups-owner-opens-new-cocktail-bar-concept-downtown) the focus is on conversation and community.

## The Used Kids Records Partnership

One detail sets Let Me Roll It apart from other cocktail bars.

The bar partnered with Used Kids Records, the long-running Columbus record shop, to stock its opening vinyl collection. The arrangement goes further: customers can trade records for drinks or gift cards, according to [ThisWeekNews](https://www.thisweeknews.com/story/entertainment/dining/2026/08/06/let-me-roll-it-bar-weinland-park-columbus/91181318007/).

That exchange program turns the bar into a neighborhood anchor. It gives regulars a reason to come back. It connects the bar to Columbus's local music and record culture. And it creates a kind of community loop — used records circulate, the bar's collection grows, and customers feel ownership over the space's sound.

Used Kids Records has been a fixture of the Short North / University District area for decades. The partnership with Let Me Roll It is a vote of confidence in Weinland Park as a destination, not just a pass-through.

## Why Weinland Park

The bar's location is worth noting.

Weinland Park sits north of downtown Columbus, east of the Short North, and south of the University District. It borders Italian Village — one of the city's most active residential development corridors — and it sits within walking distance of the OSU campus.

It is not Short North. That is partly the point.

Short North has some of the highest commercial rents in the Columbus market. Operators who want a neighborhood feel — and prices that match — are looking north and east. [Columbus Real Estate News has covered](/blog/italian-village-419-apartments-state-library-columbus) the Italian Village corridor's ongoing residential buildout. Commercial operators are following.

Stratton's decision to anchor in Weinland Park rather than a pricier corridor reflects both economics and intent. He runs a music venue in North Columbus. He knows how neighborhood bars work. A drinks-under-$12, no-TVs concept needs a neighborhood that will support it over time — not a tourist corridor that turns over.

## What It Signals

New operators choosing transitional neighborhoods over established ones is a pattern Columbus real estate watchers track closely.

In [Franklinton last August](/blog/columbus-s-franklinton-gets-a-rotating-theme-cocktail-bar), The Shapeshifter opened a rotating-theme cocktail bar in a renovated garage. In [Clintonville in 2025](/blog/clintonville-s-harvest-bar-kitchen-reopens-after-two-fires-in-two-years), Harvest Bar + Kitchen survived two fires to reopen. In Weinland Park now, a known local operator is putting capital and a concept into the neighborhood.

Each of these is a small signal. But they add up. Commercial operators invest where they think foot traffic will grow. Landlords in those corridors notice. Rents follow.

Let Me Roll It is a cocktail bar. It is also a data point.
