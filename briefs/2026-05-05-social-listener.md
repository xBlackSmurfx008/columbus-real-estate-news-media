**1. Columbus Homelessness Hits Record 2,556 — COHHIO Warns of 68% Spike by 2028**  
Volume: **LOUD** | Sentiment: **Alarmed / urgent**  
Frame: **Threat** — the affordability crisis is producing documented human outcomes; the 2028 homelessness forecast lands exactly when the housing pipeline peaks; investors and policy watchers can no longer treat these as separate conversations

*New to this run.*

The COHHIO Gap Report 2026 and Axios Columbus coverage of January's Point-in-Time count confirm Columbus's homeless population hit a record 2,556 individuals — a 26% increase since 2020. Of those, 455 lived outside; 2,101 were in shelters or transitional housing. The Housing Ohio Conference (April 20–21, ~800 attendees at the Hyatt Regency) issued a formal alarm: Central Ohio has only 26 affordable units for every 100 extremely low-income households. A WOSU-reported regional modeling study predicts a 68% increase in unsheltered homelessness by 2028. The eviction filing rate in Central Ohio is 8.5% of renters — highest in the state. Multiple distinct institutional voices: Community Shelter Board, COHHIO, WOSU, Axios Columbus, Spectrum News 1, Yahoo News.

> "The key drivers of homelessness are rising rents and lowering vacancy rates." — Community Shelter Board officials, quoted by Axios Columbus ([axios.com/local/columbus/2026/01/29/point-in-time-homeless-count-2026-ohio](https://www.axios.com/local/columbus/2026/01/29/point-in-time-homeless-count-2026-ohio))

**2. Franklinton Displacement Irony — BRT Delay Extends Speculation Window Before Transit Arrives**  
Volume: **STEADY** | Sentiment: **Frustrated / alarmed (long-time residents)**  
Frame: **Threat** — the 16-month BRT delay (today's morning story) has a shadow side: it extends the window for land speculation and displacement in Franklinton before the transit benefits that were supposed to trigger community protections arrive

*New framing today, though Franklinton displacement coverage is ongoing.*

Today's top story — the West Broad BRT 16-month delay — lands on a neighborhood where displacement fear is persistent and multi-voiced. NBC4 and WOSU have both published the "tale of two Franklintons": development and affordability sparring in central Ohio's oldest community. New businesses, upscale apartments (Gravity Project, Land Grant), and the New City Homes 50-home scattered-site program north of West Broad are already reshaping eastern Franklinton's land values. COTA spokesperson Jeff Pullin confirmed the ODOT traffic-analysis trigger but offered no revised community benefit plan. The irony: the delay gives speculators more runway to acquire near station areas before transit arrives — the opposite of the anti-displacement-through-speed argument that transit-equity advocates typically rely on.

> "Two decades after the completion of the flood wall, Franklinton is seeing explosive development, and gentrification now threatens to displace the neighborhood's families." — NBC4 Columbus ([nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/franklinton-residents-seek-modernization-but-fear-gentrification/](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/franklinton-residents-seek-modernization-but-fear-gentrification/))

> "COTA was made aware of a requirement from ODOT to provide an updated traffic analysis due to the state's return to the office orders last year." — COTA spokesperson Jeff Pullin (via WOSU / nationaltoday.com)

The Columbus American Dream Downpayment Initiative (ADDI) is generating consistent search traffic through national aggregator sites — LendingTree, refiguide.org, ark7.com — without any local outlet publishing a "do you qualify?" explainer.
