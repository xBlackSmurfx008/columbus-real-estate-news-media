A new cocktail bar opened in Franklinton this summer. It lives in a renovated garage and changes its entire concept every few months.

The Shapeshifter opened June 17 at 129 McDowell St., as first reported by Hoodline. The bar comes from bartender Theo Lentz, who previously worked at Mouton in the Short North before that bar closed in 2024.

## How It Works

The Shapeshifter has no permanent menu. The cocktail list, decor, and atmosphere shift every few months. Drinks cost $10 to $20.

The bar opened with a menu called "Greatest Hits" — the most popular cocktails from Lentz's pop-up series. Named drinks include the Macho Matcha Man, Green Thumb, Magenta Moon Rising, and Uncultured Swine. New themes will rotate in.

The space also hosts rotating visual art and live music. The Shapeshifter describes itself as a queer and artists' collective — a community gathering point with cocktails at the center.

## Why Franklinton?

After Mouton closed in 2024, Lentz started hosting themed cocktail pop-ups at bars around Columbus. The events drew a following. When it came time to open a permanent space, Lentz chose Franklinton over the Short North or the Arena District.

That choice is worth reading as a signal.

Franklinton has been transforming since the early 2010s. Artists and small operators moved in first, drawn by low rents and raw space. Developers followed. Today, Franklinton is one of the most active development corridors in Columbus.

The Peninsula project at West Broad and Belle Streets is adding more than 250 apartments, a grocery store, and a parking garage. New City Homes has been filling vacant lots north of West Broad with new single-family houses. The West Broad Street Bus Rapid Transit line — Columbus's first BRT corridor — is planned to serve the neighborhood.

A queer artists' collective in a renovated garage fits Franklinton's pattern exactly: an independent operator taking a chance on a neighborhood that has already done much of its hardest work.

## What to Watch

The rotating-theme concept is a test. Most Columbus bars build a fixed identity and stick with it. The Shapeshifter is betting that a loyal crowd will return specifically because things change.

If that works, expect more independent operators to follow on McDowell Street. That is how Franklinton has grown every time — one unlikely opening at a time, each one making the next one easier to justify.

For property owners and investors in Franklinton, the direction is clear. The neighborhood is not done changing. Every new independent operator that sticks is a data point confirming that the market can support more.
