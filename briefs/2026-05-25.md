The South Side has been Columbus's most-watched underdeveloped corridor for years. In the span of a few months in early 2026, three separate major housing investments have landed within a mile of each other along South High Street — and the commercial infrastructure to make the neighborhood work is already in place.

Here's what's moving, who's behind it, and what it means for Columbus operators.

## Steelton Village: City Council Clears the Way for 455 Units and a $200M Bet

Columbus City Council approved a development agreement on April 13, 2026 with Lotus Company, a Utah-based real estate developer, for the first residential phase of Steelton Village — the ambitious mixed-use project anchored by The Fort, the former Steelton industrial compound turned creative-commercial hub on South High Street.

The first residential phase covers 16.41 acres near 1981 S. High St. and calls for 455 multifamily units. Of those, 279 are targeted at households earning up to 60% of the area median income (AMI). The development also includes a new public park and a connector road linking the project to South High Street.

Lotus Company is committing more than $200 million to this opening residential phase. The City Council vote was timed deliberately: the developer needed a locked-in development agreement before it could competitively apply for Ohio's Transformational Mixed-Use Development (TMD) tax credits — the state's most competitive and lucrative housing incentive program. If Lotus wins those credits, the Phase 1 financial model firms up significantly; if not, the timeline may stretch.

The broader Steelton Village vision, developed by Justin McAllister (Fortner Upholstery) and Columbus developer Kyle Katz, ultimately calls for up to 1,000 residences and 400,000 square feet of commercial space across 26 acres. The commercial core at The Fort is already active and drawing tenants.

## Landmark on Scioto: 321 Units and a 44-Acre Footprint

Less than a mile from Steelton Village, Lincoln Avenue Communities (LAC) broke ground in April 2026 on Landmark on Scioto — the California-based affordable housing developer's first ground-up construction project in Ohio.

The project spans 44.5 acres and will deliver 321 units across 257 one- and two-story buildings, with a mix of two-, three-, and four-bedroom units. All units will be affordable to residents earning up to 60% AMI.

LAC's partner Ohio Capital Corporation for Housing (OCCH) is providing more than $51 million in investor equity, plus a $5 million bridge loan through its lending affiliate. As part of the deal, Lincoln Avenue Communities is dedicating approximately 7 acres to the City of Columbus, which will be converted into a public park.

Leasing is expected to begin in summer 2026, with full project completion scheduled for 2028. U.S. Representative Mike Carey (OH-15), Ohio Housing Finance Agency Executive Director Bill Beagle, and OCCH President and CEO Catherine Cawthon joined Lincoln Avenue Communities for the April groundbreaking.

## NRP Group Opens The Lofton, Eyes a Second South Side Project

Cleveland-based NRP Group, partnering with local nonprofit Community Development for All People, opened The Lofton at 55 Fornof Rd. in May 2026. The 245-unit development offers one-, two-, and three-bedroom units affordable at 30% to 80% AMI — rents running from $365 for a one-bedroom at the 30% AMI level to $1,295 for a three-bedroom at the 80% AMI level. The development includes a first-floor commercial space reserved for a daycare operator, and is already more than half leased since opening.

NRP Group isn't stopping there. The developer has submitted a zoning application to the city for a second South Side project at 45 W. Barthman Ave. — an 8-acre site approximately a mile from The Lofton, proposing 200 apartments across three separate buildings.

Together, the two NRP Group projects add 445 units to the South High corridor. How quickly the city moves on the Barthman Ave. zoning application will be a meaningful signal about the strength of the policy tailwind behind South Side development.

## The Fort as the Commercial Anchor

What separates this development cluster from prior South Side investment cycles is the presence of a functioning commercial anchor — and the tenants to prove it.

The Fort occupies the century-old Steelton industrial buildings at the heart of the corridor. It already houses furniture makers, architects, web designers, a coffee roaster, and other creative tenants. City Winery and Honest Friend Brewing are both actively building out their spaces there as of May 2026.

John Mally, a broker at NAI Ohio Equities who covers South Side transactions, told The Fort's Punch List this week that he expects more business attention to follow the residential investment wave. The commercial and residential build-outs are reinforcing each other in a way the South Side hasn't seen before.

## What This Convergence Means for Columbus Operators

Three concurrent housing investments totaling more than 1,000 units — plus active commercial anchors at The Fort — is a tipping-point signal. Here's what to watch:

**State TMD tax credits.** Steelton Village's Lotus Company is applying for Ohio's Transformational Mixed-Use Development tax credits. These are limited and intensely competitive. The City Council vote was structured to give Lotus the best possible application timing. A win means the Phase 1 financial model locks in; a loss creates uncertainty about pace.

**NRP Group's second project and zoning.** The 200-unit proposal at 45 W. Barthman Ave. still requires a city zoning decision. This is the next significant regulatory approval to watch on the South Side — and the timeline signals how seriously the city is behind the corridor.

**Supply-demand in the near-term.** Three projects delivering 1,000+ units to a single corridor creates meaningful new supply. The Lofton's leasing pace is encouraging, but operators underwriting new product here should model lease-up carefully given concurrent deliveries scheduled for 2026–2028.

**Land pricing.** South Side land along South High Street has historically traded at a discount to comparable sites in Franklinton, German Village, or the Near East Side. As this investment wave becomes visible — and as The Fort's commercial story is told more broadly — that discount is likely to compress. Operators watching this corridor for site acquisitions are on a shorter clock than they may realize.

Columbus's South Side has been "about to happen" for a long time. In early 2026, it started actually happening.
