Two vacant buildings on Cleveland Avenue no longer stand this morning. Crews started demolishing the structures at 145 and 159 Cleveland Ave. on May 6, clearing the site for Opportunity Pointe — a $54 million, 166-unit affordable apartment development steps from Columbus State Community College's main campus.

The demolition was delayed by roughly a day after a miscommunication between contractors, but work is now underway, with full demolition expected to wrap within days and vertical construction to follow shortly after.

## What Is Opportunity Pointe?

Opportunity Pointe is a joint effort between Columbus State Community Partners — the nonprofit real estate development arm of Columbus State Community College — and Woda Cooper Companies, a Columbus-based affordable housing developer with more than 365 properties and 18,000 housing units across 17 states, including 30 properties in central Ohio alone.

The development will consist of two five-story buildings sharing an outdoor amenity space and surface parking lot at the corner of Cleveland Avenue and Long Street, just north of downtown. When complete, it will offer 166 apartments priced across income bands set by the Ohio Housing Finance Agency:

- **One-bedroom:** $365–$995/month
- **Two-bedroom:** $410–$1,175/month
- **Three-bedroom:** $440–$1,295/month

Twenty of the 166 units will be held specifically for Columbus State students — a component that CSCC officials have said directly addresses one of the biggest barriers to student retention: housing cost and proximity to campus.

## The Affordable Housing Case

Columbus's overall apartment market is tightening. Vacancy citywide fell to 3.80% in 2025, and new deliveries are projected at only about 5,150 units in 2026 — down nearly 44% from the year before. The pullback in market-rate construction is squeezing lower-income renters who can't compete for the shrinking pool of available units.

Opportunity Pointe is funded in part through Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC) awarded by the Ohio Housing Finance Agency. The LIHTC program is the primary federal vehicle for financing affordable rental housing, and Ohio's allocation is competitive. Woda Cooper's track record in central Ohio — already managing dozens of LIHTC properties — made the partnership with Columbus State a credible pitch for the credit allocation. [needs prior post on Ohio LIHTC cycle]

## Why Columbus State Is Building Housing

Columbus State Community Partners' involvement in real estate development is deliberate. Community colleges have increasingly recognized that housing instability is among the top reasons students stop attending. A student paying market-rate rent in Columbus — the city's average now runs roughly $1,495/month across unit types — faces a significant budget gap if working part-time or in low-wage employment.

By structuring 20 dedicated student units with below-market rents and co-locating them within walking distance of campus services, CSCC is treating housing as academic infrastructure. The 2024 announcement of the project framed it explicitly around student success outcomes, not just housing supply.

## The Contractor Mix-Up

The demolition at 145 and 159 Cleveland Ave. was originally announced for early May but was pushed to May 6 after a miscommunication between contractors on the project. The delay was procedural rather than a sign of broader project trouble — Columbus State officials confirmed construction remains on schedule, with an anticipated completion date of late 2027.

For a $54 million development involving two institutional partners, a one-day demolition delay is a footnote. But it was noticed locally and prompted coverage from multiple Columbus TV stations, reflecting community interest in whether the project would move forward after years of planning since its first announcement in 2024.

## The Neighborhood Context

The Cleveland Avenue and Long Street corridor sits in a transitional zone between downtown Columbus and the near east side. It is within walking distance of Columbus State's main campus, the Franklin Park neighborhood, and the future COTA rapid transit network that includes the East Main Bus Rapid Transit study corridor.

The area has seen a mix of disinvestment and targeted public investment over the past decade. Vacant commercial buildings, surface parking lots, and underutilized storefronts have coexisted with CSCC's expanding campus footprint. Opportunity Pointe's two-building, mixed-income format — anchored by an institutional partner — represents a bet that the corridor is ready for denser residential investment.

For Columbus operators watching near-east-side acquisition targets, the arrival of a major institutional-backed affordable development signals that city and state resources are actively being deployed in this submarket. [internal link: prior near-east-side coverage when available]

## What Comes Next

Demolition at the Cleveland Avenue site is expected to wrap within days. Construction of the two five-story buildings will follow, with a completion target at the end of 2027. The project team — Columbus State Community Partners and Woda Cooper Companies — have not announced a leasing office timeline, but the student unit allocation is expected to be coordinated through CSCC's housing services.

For the 20 CSCC student units, availability and application details will likely be communicated through the college's student affairs channels.

Columbus State officials have indicated the project is the first of potentially more real estate ventures the college is considering to address campus-area housing need. Whether a second phase or adjacent site follows Opportunity Pointe will depend on LIHTC allocations in future Ohio Housing Finance Agency competitive cycles.
