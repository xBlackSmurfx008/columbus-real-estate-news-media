**1. Ohio's 53,000-Unit Affordable Housing Deficit — COHHIO Gap Report Now in Circulation** — Loud, Frustrated
*Frame: Threat + content opportunity (negative community sentiment surfaces at top per policy)*

The COHHIO (Coalition on Homelessness and Housing in Ohio) 2026 Gap Report is actively circulating in housing advocacy circles. Columbus-specific data: the metro lacks **53,000 affordable units** to meet demand. The ratio of affordable units available to extremely low-income renters sits at just **27 per 100 households** — meaning three-quarters of the city's most vulnerable renters cannot access an affordable unit at all. Statewide, **73% of Ohio's extremely low-income renters** are spending more than half their income on housing.

The report is prompting frustrated, cross-ideological conversation: housing advocates share the numbers to push for policy action; critics of new development note that the supply wave is not reaching the tiers where need is greatest (consistent with yesterday's Class A / affordability gap theme).

**Verbatim (Amy Riegel, COHHIO Executive Director, via Spectrum News 1, March 2026):**
> "The data confirms what we hear all the time — renters across the board are having more trouble paying rent."

**Verbatim (Affordable Housing Alliance of Central Ohio executive director, via search summary):**
> "If we don't escalate that pace radically, come 2040, we're going to have a housing market that looks a lot more like California than it does Columbus."

**Verbatim (Columbus Housing Board, April 2026, via Hoodline search summary):**
> Columbus Housing Board signed off on a cash surge for 4 Ohio affordable projects — an acknowledgment that the pipeline gap is real and requires public subsidy to close.

**Volume signal:** Loud — the Gap Report is being cited across multiple distinct organizations (COHHIO, Affordable Housing Alliance, Columbus Housing Board), news outlets (Spectrum News 1, NBC4, NewsBreak), and advocacy channels. Not manufactured.

**Note:** This is separate from yesterday's apartment-supply-softening-rents theme. The supply wave is Class A; this theme is about the structural deficit in subsidized and deeply affordable tiers. The distinction is important and the community conflates them.

**3. Bexley Commons Tenant Evictions After Rent Petition — Chilling Effect on Tenant Organizing** — Steady, Negative
*Frame: Threat (to community/tenant rights) + content opportunity — underreported local organizing story*

Nearly 70 Bexley Commons tenants in the Columbus area signed a petition demanding a rent reduction following significant rent increases. As of current reporting, that number has **dwindled to fewer than 40 as tenants were evicted**. In a separate but related Columbus case, a court found that a landlord raised rent $200/month within 30 days of a tenant reporting mold to the health department — the judge ruled it retaliatory and reversed the increase plus awarded attorney fees.

This cluster of stories is generating quiet but sustained conversation in tenant and renter communities. The Bexley Commons situation — where organizing participation dropped as evictions proceeded — is a chilling-effect pattern that housing advocates track closely. Direct Reddit/X posts were not accessible this run, but the pattern is consistent with housing-rights forum concerns that routinely appear in r/Columbus and r/Tenant threads nationally.

**Verbatim (Bexley Commons coverage, via NBC4 search summary):**

**Verbatim (NBC4 Columbus, tenant retaliation case):**

**4. Intel Production Delay to 2030–2031 Recalibrates Eastern Suburbs Housing Story** — Steady, Mixed/Concerned
*Frame: Threat (to speculators/buyers banking on Intel timeline) + correction opportunity*

**Important update from yesterday's brief.** Intel's New Albany semiconductor production start has been pushed from the original 2025 target to **2030–2031 — a five-year delay**. Construction is ongoing (9.4M+ work hours logged) but permanent job creation is now deferred by half a decade. This significantly recalibrates the investment thesis for housing in Pataskala, Johnstown, and New Albany that was built on "Intel workers arriving soon."

Despite the delay, housing prices in the Route 161 corridor have already moved: Licking County farmland went from ~$20,000/acre (2021) to ~$45,000/acre (2022), and median prices in New Albany remain elevated. The question now circulating: will values hold through a 5-year production gap, or is eastern suburb housing exposed to a partial correction?

The delay is also notable for workforce housing: 7,000 peak construction workers are still active, but the 3,000 permanent $135K-average-salary jobs won't materialize until the early 2030s. The affordable housing pressure question ("where are we putting everybody?") raised in 2022 shifts from permanent-residents planning to extended-temporary-workers planning.

**Verbatim (Van Steyn Partners, via search summary):**
> Intel's semiconductor production start has been delayed approximately five years — from 2025 to 2030–2031.

**Verbatim (Columbus Ohio Housing Market 2026 summary, via Advantage Lending search summary):**
> The "Intel Effect" [is] maintaining steady upward pressure on home values and rental rates across the region [in 2026] — but [with production delayed] investors banking on permanent job arrivals face an extended wait.

**Volume signal:** Steady — covered by local real estate firms (Van Steyn Partners), national financial media, and regional housing analysts. The framing varies (optimists say "construction demand continues"; skeptics flag the valuation risk).

**CRE News angle:** Yesterday's brief described Intel as "on track for initial production" — this needs correction in any future article. The 5-year delay is the operative fact.

2. **"What happens to Harrison West if the Thrive rezoning goes through?"** → suggested format: neighborhood report. First post–Zone In test case. A piece explaining what 60 units means for parking, the street grid, and precedent for the zone would serve both sides of the debate.

3. **"Is my landlord allowed to raise my rent $500 after I complained?"** → suggested format: short explainer / Q&A. Ohio has no rent control; the Bexley Commons and NBC4 retaliation cases show tenants don't know their actual rights. A piece breaking down what the delay means for housing values, workforce housing demand, and timeline for New Albany/Johnstown would serve investors who made or are considering decisions based on the original 2025 production timeline.
