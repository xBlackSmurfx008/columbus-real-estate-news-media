## Lead 1 — One Twenty Vine: Nationwide Realty Investors Plans 242-Unit Apartment Building on Arena District Parking Lot

**Summary:** Yahoo News (sourcing NBC4i) reported on September 28, 2026 that Nationwide Realty Investors — the real estate development affiliate of Nationwide Insurance — plans to build a 242-unit apartment complex called One Twenty Vine at 154 Vine St. (to be readdressed as 120 Vine St.) in Columbus's Arena District. The development would replace a gravel surface parking lot on the block bounded by Vine, Spruce, Armstrong, and Kilbourne streets, combining nine parcels. The project consists of two seven-story buildings (five floors of wood construction above a two-story podium) framing a shared courtyard with the adjacent Two Twenty Vine building. Building highlights include 56 enclosed first-floor parking spaces, a third-floor skywalk connecting to the neighboring Kilbourne Garage, a leasing office, fitness center, resident lounge, and a pool deck and outdoor gathering areas in the shared courtyard. HP Land Development LTD (a Hamilton Parker affiliate) is a development partner; The Columbus Architectural Studio is the architect. The Columbus Downtown Commission approved the design on September 22, 2026. Building permits are still required; no construction timeline has been announced.

**Why this matters for CREN (downtown development / housing supply angle):**
- A 242-unit infill project replacing a surface parking lot in the Arena District is a directly measurable contribution to downtown Columbus housing supply. It continues the Nationwide Realty Investors pattern of consolidating and densifying the Arena District (Two Twenty Vine, now One Twenty Vine on an adjacent block).
- The wood-over-podium construction on nine consolidated parcels is a notable structural choice for a 7-story downtown building — a cost-efficiency signal. The skywalk connection to the Kilbourne Garage means the project reduces its own parking requirement without eliminating access, a design approach relevant to CREN's Zone In / reduced-parking-minimums coverage.
- Commission member Bob Loversidge's remark — "I'm happy to see another parking lot going away" — reflects the pro-infill consensus now operating in Columbus's downtown design review body, which is worth documenting in aggregate across multiple projects.
