### 1. The Peninsula Phase 2 Breaks Ground: Giant Eagle Market District Anchors 250-Unit Downtown Tower

- **What**: The Daimler Group and Flaherty & Collins are constructing The Peninsula Phase 2, a 14–15-story mixed-use building at the corner of Broad and Belle Streets — at the seam between downtown Columbus and Franklinton. Construction is expected to begin spring 2026 with completion targeted for 2027–2028. The ground floor will house a 20,000 SF Giant Eagle Market District — fresh groceries, ready-to-eat meals, beer and wine — making it the first full-service grocery in the immediate downtown core. The residential portion carries approximately 250 apartments above an attached parking garage. Downtown Columbus, Inc. is involved in the project.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Downtown Columbus has operated as a grocery desert for years — a genuine barrier to full-time residential density and a friction point for every multifamily operator in the core. Peninsula Phase 2 resolves that directly, and the ripple effects are real: nearby operators can now market downtown units to renters who previously hesitated. The Broad-Belle corner is also the first major mixed-use node directly straddling downtown and Franklinton, anchoring the westward expansion corridor that neighborhood investors have been watching.

### 2. Nationwide's Tower at 280 N. High St. Becomes "The Centennial on High" — 148 Downtown Apartments

- **What**: Nationwide Realty Investors (NRI) received Downtown Commission approval to convert Two Nationwide Plaza — an 18-story office building at 280 N. High St. — into a mixed residential-office building. The conversion will create 148 apartments across 15 floors, including a new penthouse level, each residential floor with five units and balconies. About 75,000 SF of office space remains, with anchor tenants Carpenter Lipps LLP and Ohio Farm Bureau retaining space. The building is being renamed The Centennial on High in honor of Nationwide's 100th anniversary year. First residents are projected to move in by mid-2027.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: This is the third major downtown office conversion in recent years (following the Continental Building and the Preston Center), confirming that office-to-residential is no longer a one-off workaround — it's a repeatable Columbus playbook. For operators, the Centennial project demonstrates that institutional-grade investors are willing to carry office exposure alongside residential, keeping ground-floor and mid-building tenancy alive rather than converting entirely.

### 4. Columbus Company Builds Apartments Indoors, Ships Them to Site — Cutting Time and Cost by a Third

- **What**: Connect Housing Blocks, a Columbus-based manufacturer based in the Linden neighborhood, produces fully finished apartment units on a factory production line — framed, insulated, wired, plumbed, floored, and lit before they leave the building. Units are then shipped to site and stacked. The company's EVP of development Bob Lamb told 10TV that the method is approximately one-third faster and one-third cheaper than conventional construction, with 80% less on-site construction waste and 70% fewer site vehicle trips. Connect completed its first major project — a 100+ unit complex in Columbus's Trolley District (east of downtown) — and has two additional projects underway: one on Maryland Avenue on the east side and one in Springfield.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Modular construction has struggled to scale in Columbus partly because of site logistics and municipal permitting familiarity. Connect Housing Blocks is a local proof-of-concept now past its first full project. For Columbus operators struggling with construction cost escalation, this is worth watching as a viable procurement path — especially on mid-size infill sites where conventional construction margins are thin.
- **Source(s)**: [Columbus-based manufacturer assembles apartments indoors — 10TV](https://www.10tv.com/article/news/local/boomtown-ohio/columbus-based-manufacturer-assembles-apartments-indoors-connect-housing-blocks/530-4c4cfa3c-7070-4e2e-bdf6-b9a0e8306020), [Columbus modular apartment complex project nears completion — NBC4](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-modular-apartment-complex-project-nears-completion/), [Officials highlight tiny homes, modular apartment factory — 10TV](https://www.10tv.com/article/news/local/officials-tiny-homes-modular-apartment-factory-affordable-housing-columbus/530-2d584fd3-c978-4474-a7c7-bdc1182b1122)

### 6. Columbus Q1 2026 Housing Market: Prices Up 6.8%, Inventory Rising, Pace Easing Slightly

- **What**: The Columbus metro housing market closed Q1 2026 with 4,847 residential transactions, a 12% increase from Q4 2025. Median home prices reached $287,500 — up 6.8% year-over-year. March alone saw 2,118 closings, flat year-over-year (+0.5%). Homes averaged 47 days on market, up from 43 days last year. Inventory reached 4,067 units — up 3.1% year-over-year — representing 2.8 months of supply. Mortgage rates briefly dipped below 6% in late February before rising roughly 50 basis points through March. New construction permits increased 15% quarter-over-quarter. Multifamily vacancy remains below 5%, with average rents up 4.2% year-over-year.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: Columbus continues to post above-average appreciation while early signs of balance appear — rising days on market, growing inventory. For multifamily operators, sub-5% vacancy and 4.2% rent growth validate continued new supply delivery. For residential investors, the 47-day market is the first meaningful softening signal in three years.

### 8. Columbus $500M Affordable Housing Bond: Implementation Underway After November 2025 Voter Approval

- **What**: Columbus voters approved a $500 million affordable housing bond in November 2025. The bond is structured as: $150M for building and preserving affordable homes, $125M for housing stability programs, and $175M for access-to-opportunity initiatives. As of spring 2026, implementation is underway. The Ohio Housing Finance Agency (OHFA) separately approved financing in April 2026 for four housing developments statewide; Columbus-specific allocations from the city bond are expected to be announced in project-by-project tranches throughout 2026.
- **Why it matters (CRE News frame)**: The $500M bond is the largest single public investment in Columbus affordable housing in the city's history. For operators working in the affordable and workforce-housing tiers — including LIHTC deals, land trust partnerships, and community land bank projects — this is the primary subsidy stack to track. Watch for individual project award announcements as the year progresses.
- **Source(s)**: [Columbus Is Rolling Out A $500 Million Affordable Housing Plan — Columbus Navigator](https://www.columbusnavigator.com/columbus-affordable-housing-plan), [OHFA Board Approves Financing for Four Housing Developments — Ohio Home](https://ohiohome.org/news/releases/2026/april-fundedprojects.aspx), [Housing Initiatives — City of Columbus](https://www.columbus.gov/Government/City-Council/News-Releases-Information/Programs-Initiatives/Housing-Initiatives)
