#### 1. Zone In affordability gap — "luxury not affordable"

**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Frustrated / negative
**Frame:** Content opportunity — high reader anxiety, no clear local explainer

The Zone In Phase 2 public comment period (open through October 24) is generating community-level pushback that goes beyond standard NIMBY concerns. The critique that has traction: Columbus's upzoning reforms and associated investment programs are producing market-rate and luxury units, not affordable ones. Multiple sources across mid-2026 — a think-tank researcher, a statewide coalition report, and a Columbus Underground op-ed — are converging on the same claim.

**Verbatim quotes with sources:**

> "This isn't creating affordable housing." — Aditi Srivastava, tax policy researcher, Policy Matters Ohio, on Ohio Opportunity Zones program. *Spectrum News 1, July 17, 2026.* ([spectrumnews1.com](https://spectrumnews1.com/oh/columbus/news/2026/07/17/program-designed-to-create-opportunities-receives-backlash-))

> "In some cases and in many cases, often creating like luxury apartments." — Srivastava, same source.

> "The Illusion of Affordable Housing in Columbus" — Columbus Underground op-ed headline, undated (search-visible, fetch blocked). ([columbusunderground.com](https://columbusunderground.com/opinion-the-illusion-of-affordable-housing-in-columbus/))

**Supporting data:** COHHIO's *Gap Report 2026* documents a 53,000-unit deficit of affordable rental housing in Columbus. Assessment by Matter News found new affordable housing investments may leave the city's "most vulnerable properties and tenants behind." ([cohhio.org](https://cohhio.org/the-gap-report-2026/))

**Content gap this surfaces:** A Columbus-specific explainer on what Zone In Phase 2 *will and won't* produce affordably — how the public comment process works, what the density reforms actually enable at the unit-price level, and what the 53,000-unit gap means in practice. Zone In Phase 2 comment period closes October 24. That's a timed peg.

#### 2. Franklin County triennial property tax update — homeowner anxiety

**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Mixed-to-frustrated
**Frame:** Content opportunity — practical, time-sensitive

The 2026 triennial reappraisal (not a full reappraisal — a value update) mailed June 9. Informal review sessions run through September 2026, meaning the appeal window is effectively closing now. Multiple real estate blogs and broker posts are active on this topic, which means homeowners are searching. The anxiety is understandable: the 2023 full reappraisal raised Franklin County residential values an average of 43%, and some homeowners in hot neighborhoods are now seeing a further 20–40% increase in the 2026 update. The state average for this update is approximately 9%, but neighborhood-level variation is wide.

**Verbatim quotes with sources:**

> "The Franklin County Auditor launched a website explaining a state-required property value update that occurs every three years between full reappraisals, with residents receiving new assessments in June." — NBC4 WCMH-TV. ([nbc4i.com](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/franklin-county/franklin-county-auditor-launches-website-for-property-value-update/))

> "Informal Property Value Review Sessions run July through September 2026. This is the easiest, no-cost first step if you disagree with your tentative value." — Sam Cooper Realtor blog. ([samcooperrealtor.com](https://www.samcooperrealtor.com/blog/franklin-county-2026-triennial-update-appeal-your-value/))

> "Higher property taxes do not just affect homeowners—they also influence renters, local economies, and housing affordability. Landlords often pass increased costs to tenants through higher rent." — The Free Financial Advisor. ([thefreefinancialadvisor.com](https://www.thefreefinancialadvisor.com/ohio-property-revaluations-are-increasing-2026-tax-bills-across-multiple-counties/))

**Content gap:** A practical guide — "Franklin County 2026 triennial update: what your new value means, how to appeal before the window closes" — timed to the closing of informal review sessions (through September). The informal window closing in September is the peg; missing it means losing the no-cost first-step opportunity. This piece would be genuinely useful and time-sensitive for Columbus homeowners.

#### 3. Brewery District / WWCD demolition — community nostalgia meets density

**Volume:** Light | **Sentiment:** Mixed (divided; some pro-density, some anti-demolition)
**Frame:** Story ready to cover — confirms community interest in peg already on our monitor list

The Historic Resources Commission's 5-2 approval of Arcadia Development's 106-unit, six-story building on the former WWCD/CD102.5/CD92.9 FM site is generating community conversation, primarily on Columbus Underground and UrbanOhio (search-visible, fetch blocked). The tension is between density supporters who see the project as needed housing and residents/radio nostalgia who see it as erasing a local landmark. The 51-space garage is a flashpoint — the Columbus Division of Fire did not support parking changes over emergency vehicle concerns.

**Verbatim quotes with sources:**

> "Demolition and Six-Story Development in Brewery District Approved" — Columbus Underground headline, September 2026. ([columbusunderground.com](https://columbusunderground.com/demolition-and-six-story-development-in-brewery-district-approved-bw1/))

> "Concerns were raised about the size of the new building as well as the demolition of the Chalet building, which is vacant but served as the home of WWCD 92.9 before the local radio station went off the air." — Search snippet summarizing coverage of the HRC vote.

> "Let's Make The Brewery District Great Again" — Columbus Underground op-ed headline (search-visible). ([columbusunderground.com](https://columbusunderground.com/lets-make-the-brewery-district-great-again-we1/))

**Note:** This story was held in Session 2 (today's AM run) because no primary government document (HRC agenda/minutes at columbus.gov) was fetchable. The Columbus Underground headlines confirm the vote is public. This is the next article to draft when a primary source clears.

#### 4. "Ax Ohio Tax" ballot campaign collapses — property tax abolition off the 2026 ballot

**Volume:** Light (peaked July 2026, now quieter) | **Sentiment:** Mixed (relief from municipalities; frustration from property-tax opponents)
**Frame:** News explainer — relatively quiet now but frames upcoming 2027 fight

The citizen initiative to abolish Ohio property taxes constitutionally failed to collect the ~413,487 valid signatures needed (from 44 counties) by the July 1, 2026 deadline. Organizers say they will carry signatures forward and attempt to qualify for a 2027 ballot. The countermovement — Ohioans to Protect Public Services — remains active. For Columbus-area homeowners frustrated by triennial increases, this failure means no ballot relief this fall.

**Verbatim quotes with sources:**

> "The Committee to Abolish Ohio Property Taxes (Ax Ohio Tax) said the campaign had not collected the approximately 620,000 signatures it had hoped to secure ahead of the July 1 filing deadline." — The Statehouse News Bureau / WYSO, July 22, 2026. ([statenews.org](https://www.statenews.org/section/the-ohio-newsroom/2026-07-22/effort-to-abolish-ohio-property-taxes-faces-looming-deadline-for-fall-ballot))

> "Property Tax Abolition Initiative Won't Be on 2026 Ballot" — Ohio Municipal League, 2026. ([omlohio.org](https://www.omlohio.org/m/newsflash/home/detail/39))

> "[The move] would be catastrophic for local emergency services and schools." — Ohioans to Protect Public Services. (Search snippet, primary source: omlohio.org and related coverage.)

**Content gap:** A Columbus-local explainer — "Ax Ohio Tax failed to qualify: what that means for your Franklin County bill in 2026 and what comes next in 2027" — connects the ballot failure to the triennial update anxiety in theme 2. Modest audience, but very high relevance to our investor/homeowner core. Could be combined with theme 2 content.

#### 5. Columbus housing supply softening — buyers noticing more days on market

**Volume:** Steady | **Sentiment:** Curious / cautiously optimistic
**Frame:** Market education opportunity — broker and buyer audiences both active

September data (Yahoo Finance / Bankrate snippets) shows Columbus median listing price at $282,450, 1,551 homes available (up 21.1% YoY), and 45 days average time on market — nine more days than the same month last year. This is generating search traffic from buyers asking whether the market is finally softening. The data is factual but scattered across non-local outlets, and there is no local Columbus-specific synthesis of what these September numbers mean.

**Verbatim quotes with sources:**

> "Homes in Columbus take an average of 45 days to sell, according to September figures. This is one day more than the previous month and nine days more than the same month last year." — Search snippet (Yahoo Finance / Bankrate). ([bankrate.com](https://www.bankrate.com/real-estate/housing-market/columbus-oh))

> "The number of listings on the market grew 3.6% from last month, which is a smaller increase than normal for this time of the year in Columbus." — Search snippet.

> "With only 1.7 months of supply on hand, sellers retain the structural advantage in most price ranges and communities." — Ferrari Home Group, 2026 market forecast. ([ferrarihomegroup.com](https://www.ferrarihomegroup.com/blog/housing-market-in-columbus-oh-2026-insights/))

**Content gap:** Columbus August/September home sales explainer — what the rising days-on-market and inventory-up numbers actually mean for buyers and sellers right now. The Columbus REALTORS monthly report would be the primary source for this piece; it's on the AM monitor list.
