The Pointe at Polaris — the mixed-use campus VanTrust Real Estate built along I-71 next to Polaris Fashion Place — has fully traded out of its original ownership in two separate April 2026 transactions that, taken together, tell a clear story about where Columbus CRE stands right now.

A North Texas investment firm bought the office and retail. A Columbus LLC pairing bought the apartments. VanTrust and its joint venture partner NP Limited Partnership exited the entire campus. And across those three moves, the Polaris submarket absorbed 473 luxury apartment units at $127,273 each and more than 245,000 square feet of fully leased trophy office and retail — with no apparent difficulty finding buyers for either.

Here's what happened, why the pricing matters, and what it signals for Columbus operators watching the suburban office and luxury multifamily markets.

## The Two Deals in Brief

**Office and retail:** DFW LAND, a North Texas real estate investment and development firm, acquired the commercial components of The Pointe at Polaris from the VanTrust/NP Limited Partnership joint venture. The package includes two fully leased buildings totaling 212,366 square feet of Class A trophy office space and 33,071 square feet of retail — 245,437 square feet combined. Newmark Capital Markets arranged both the sale and the acquisition financing. The sale price was not disclosed.

**Multifamily:** On April 29, 2026, the two residential buildings at 8890 Lyra Drive and 8922 Lyra Drive transferred to new LLC ownership. Polaris Lyra Holdings LLC acquired 8890 Lyra Drive for $34.9 million. Polaris Exchange LLC acquired 8922 Lyra Drive for $25.3 million. Combined: $60.2 million for 473 luxury units, working out to approximately $127,273 per unit.

Both transactions closed in April. Both were part of the same development campus. Different buyers, different capital stacks, different strategies — but the same underlying bet on Polaris.

## Why a Texas Firm Is Buying Columbus Office

National headlines about suburban office have not been kind. Remote work gutted demand in many markets. Suburban office parks that once commanded premium rents in gateway cities are sitting at elevated vacancies or trading at distressed pricing.

The Polaris submarket is not that story.

The office component DFW LAND acquired is fully leased — 212,366 square feet of Class A space across two buildings, occupied, rent-paying, stabilized. One of those buildings is a four-story, 145,000-square-foot structure with 35,000-square-foot floorplates that came online in 2021. Large, efficient floorplates at that scale are what corporate tenants occupying premium suburban space typically require, and they stayed.

For DFW LAND — a Texas-based firm with no existing Columbus portfolio — to acquire that asset at a point when many institutional investors are still reducing suburban office exposure, the underwriting had to pencil on fundamentals: Columbus employment growth, corporate tenant durability, Polaris submarket rent stability, and a view that the occupancy profile of this specific building holds through the hold period. Newmark arranged financing, which means lenders also had to underwrite those fundamentals.

The office sale price wasn't disclosed. But the fact of a fully leased Class A acquisition from an out-of-state capital source says more than a per-SF price would.

## The Apartment Trade: $127K Per Unit in Context

The multifamily side of the transaction has a number attached to it: $127,273 per unit for luxury Polaris apartments.

That's a useful data point for Columbus multifamily operators. Per-unit pricing is the primary acquisition underwriting metric — it sets the implied cap rate, the required rent per unit to hit return thresholds, and the comp basis for nearby transactions.

At $127,273 per unit, the Lyra Drive buildings traded at a meaningful discount to new-construction replacement cost for Class A Columbus multifamily, which currently runs $200,000 or more per unit to build depending on construction type and submarket. The buyers — two Columbus-based LLCs — are acquiring stabilized luxury product below replacement cost and betting that the Polaris submarket rent roll supports their basis.

For context on what the rent roll likely looks like: The Pointe at Polaris apartments are among the higher-end units on the north Columbus corridor, with amenity packages and location premiums that support above-market rents for the submarket. At $127K per unit, the implied gross rent multiplier and cap rate are favorable for a stabilized acquisition if occupancy holds.

The April 29 closing date makes this the most recent disclosed luxury multifamily transaction in the Columbus metro — a fresh 2026 comp for anyone underwriting acquisition targets in the Polaris, Powell, or northwest Columbus corridors.

## What the Full Campus Trade Tells Columbus Operators

VanTrust built The Pointe at Polaris across multiple development phases — office, retail, and residential — and stabilized all components before this exit. That's a full development lifecycle executed at scale: develop, stabilize, sell into demand. The fact that three components (office and retail as one package, two apartment buildings as separate transactions) found buyers in a single month without apparent difficulty is the operative signal.

**For office operators:** Columbus suburban office is not homogeneous. Fully leased, large-floorplate Class A product in Polaris attracts out-of-state institutional capital. Distressed, high-vacancy suburban office in weaker submarkets does not. The distinction matters for how operators price their own assets and how they evaluate nearby vacancies.

**For multifamily operators:** The $127,273 per-unit Polaris comp is the highest-profile 2026 acquisition transaction in the immediate corridor. It sets a floor for what stabilized luxury suburban Columbus multifamily trades at in the current rate environment, and it anchors the underwriting conversation for any similar assets that come to market this year.

**For the Polaris submarket broadly:** The simultaneous absorption of a large office and retail package and 473 luxury apartments — by separate, competitively structured buyers — is a liquidity signal. Capital can get in and out of Polaris across asset classes. That kind of transactional fluidity is what makes a submarket institutionally attractive over a long hold period.

Columbus's suburban north corridor continues to draw capital at scale. The Pointe at Polaris transactions are the clearest evidence yet of how that looks in 2026.
