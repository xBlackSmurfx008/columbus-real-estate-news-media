#### 1. Capitol Square Renaissance Shelved — $600M Downtown Columbus Development Paused ⚠️ NEGATIVE LEADS
**Volume:** Loud | **Sentiment:** Alarmed / Concerned
**Frame:** Threat + Accountability Story

Edwards Companies shelved the $600 million Capitol Square Renaissance Project in April 2026, citing "unfavorable economic and market conditions." The development — a 10-acre mixed-use redevelopment south of East Broad Street — would have delivered 1,000+ residential units, nine restaurants, nearly 200,000 SF of office space, and structured parking across multiple phases. The project had already secured up to $33 million in state tax credits and received city council approval in December 2025 for a 12-story apartment tower on the site. Edwards had previously completed portions of the broader Capitol Square footprint (the Preston Centre redevelopment and The Pembroke apartments). The pause is notable precisely because it cleared both regulatory and incentive milestones — two common barriers for downtown development — and was shelved anyway.

**Why it leads:** A $600M, 1,000-unit downtown development paused after clearing city approval and $33M in state incentives signals that financing gaps and construction cost pressures are now sufficient to halt even well-advanced downtown projects. This is the clearest evidence so far that Columbus's downtown mixed-use pipeline is more fragile than the industrial submarket — compare directly to VanTrust New Albany 300's pre-delivery full lease-up this same week (today's 06:33 brief). The divergence is the real Columbus development story of June 2026.
