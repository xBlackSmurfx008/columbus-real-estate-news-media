## Lead 2 — Columbus City Council Ballot Measures (Issues 8 and 9): Governance Restructuring with Implications for Zoning and Development Decisions

**Summary:** WOSU published a September 28, 2026 explainer on two competing ballot measures that would change how Columbus City Council members are elected. **Issue 8** (a grassroots citizen initiative) would restrict voting so that only voters within each of the nine council districts may vote for their respective council member — ending the current at-large system. **Issue 9** (a council-referred proposal) does the same but also expands the council from 9 to 13 members by adding four at-large citywide seats. If Issue 9 passes, redistricting would be triggered in 2028 and the allowable population variance between districts would increase from 1% to 7.5%. Both measures address the November 2026 ballot.

**Why this matters for CREN (governance-adjacent real estate angle):**
- Columbus City Council is the final decision-making body for major rezonings (McCoy Park/NWSL, Zone-In modernization), development agreements ($25M NWSL deal), and capital budget allocations ($30M affordable housing in 2026). Changing how those nine votes are constituted — and who the electorate is for each — is structurally relevant to future contested land-use decisions.
- Under the current at-large system, every Columbus voter can vote for every council seat. Under either Issue 8 or Issue 9, council members representing specific neighborhoods (e.g., the SW side district that includes McCoy Park) would answer exclusively to voters in that district rather than the broader city electorate. This concentrates accountability locally for neighborhood-level development disputes.
- If Issue 9 passes, four new at-large seats would dilute district-focused opposition on any given contentious project, while adding more total votes that could go either way.
- The redistricting trigger in Issue 9 (2028) aligns with the McCoy Park NWSL facility's target launch date (2028) — a coincidence worth noting in any long-form piece about the SW side's political representation.
