Columbus just topped a national leaderboard that city planners have been working toward for years.

A June 2026 rental report by realtor.com, cited by [NBC4](https://www.nbc4i.com/news/local-news/columbus/columbus-leads-nation-in-multifamily-home-permitting-report-shows/), found that Columbus issued 4.3 new multifamily units for every 1,000 residents in 2025. That rate is the highest the city has seen since 2019. It also outpaces every other major metro in the country.

For real estate operators, that number tells a clear story: Columbus is building at a historic pace.

## What the Numbers Mean

Permit counts measure intent. When a developer pulls a permit, they have a site, a lender, and a plan. A rate of 4.3 new multifamily units per 1,000 residents means Columbus is approving housing at a pace that most Sun Belt cities would recognize.

Columbus also ranks 5th nationally in total multifamily permit volume, according to [10TV](https://www.10tv.com/article/news/local/boomtown-ohio/columbus-ranks-5th-nationally-multifamily-housing-permits/530-9675fb62-06e9-4253-93c6-ecc37077f58e). That puts the city in the same company as metros two to three times its size. The per-resident rate shows Columbus punching above its weight. The total volume shows the scale is real.

## Zone In Is Working

The city's effort to rewrite its zoning code is a key driver of the surge.

Columbus updated its 70-year-old zoning laws — a project called Zone In — to allow more residential density along key corridors and near job centers. Phase 2 of Zone In now covers 43% of the city, as first reported by [Columbus Underground](https://columbusunderground.com/next-phase-of-zoning-reform-will-cover-43-of-city-bw1/).

The city projects Zone In can unlock the potential for more than 80,000 additional residential units over the next 10 years. Not all of those units will be built. But removing legal barriers lets projects move forward that would otherwise stall before a variance hearing.

Zone In changes what a developer can propose without needing a special exception. That alone compresses the project timeline — and in real estate, timeline is money.

## The Demand Side

Columbus isn't permitting more housing because building got easier. It's permitting more because the demand is real.

The Regional Housing Coalition — backed by Mayor Andrew Ginther and the 83-member Columbus Partnership — has set a regional target of 200,000 new homes by 2034. That is roughly 20,000 units per year across Franklin County and neighboring communities, as reported by [Columbus CEO](https://www.columbusceo.com/story/business/briefs/2026/08/04/columbus-region-leaders-unite-to-tackle-the-local-housing-crisis/91170007007/).

The math behind that target is sobering. The region has under-built its housing supply by half over the last 15 years. By 2030, projections show fewer homes than families in the Columbus region. An annual income of $90,000 is now required to afford a median-priced home here. The area's median household income is $76,000.

Tre Giller, president and CEO of Metro Development, told NBC4 that diverse housing options across the full market spectrum are essential — market-rate, workforce, and affordable units each serve a different income band.

## National Operators Are Taking Notice

The permit surge is broad, but it's most visible in a few corridors.

Northwest Columbus — near the Dublin-Granville Road corridor and the Worthington School District — has attracted national attention. The NRP Group, which has built more than 60,000 homes nationwide, broke ground last week on 336 market-rate apartments in that submarket. It is the company's first market-rate Columbus project. When a national developer enters a submarket for the first time, it signals confidence in the fundamentals.

Some 278 development projects are moving through Columbus's planning and zoning pipeline so far in 2026, according to Boardwalk AI — a volume that reflects both the city's growth and the Zone In-enabled pipeline.

## Two Gaps Operators Need to Watch

The permit surge is a leading indicator, not a delivered product. Financing conditions, labor costs, and material prices can all slow or stop a project after a permit is issued. Units permitted in 2025 and 2026 are most likely to hit the market in 2027 and 2028.

Two gaps remain even as the headline numbers improve.

First, the new supply skews market-rate and upper-market. The affordable unit gap in Franklin County is estimated at roughly 53,000 units, per the Regional Housing Coalition. The permitting surge is not closing that gap at the same pace.

Second, suburban resistance to density is real. Mayor Ginther's push to extend Zone In-style reforms beyond Columbus's city limits has run into opposition from some Franklin County communities, as [Hoodline reported in April 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/04/ginther-s-big-housing-push-hits-a-wall-in-columbus-suburbs/). Columbus controls its own zoning. It cannot compel its neighbors to do the same.

## What to Watch

The bottom line for Columbus operators and investors: the pipeline is the deepest it has been in years. The 4.3-unit permit rate is a strong leading indicator. Watch for absorption data in 2027 — that will tell us whether Columbus's demand growth can keep pace with the supply coming to market.
