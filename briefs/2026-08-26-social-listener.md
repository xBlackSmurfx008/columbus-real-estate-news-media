### 1. Rental Registry now law — landlords threatening legal action, tenant advocates say it was defanged — LOUD, NEGATIVE/MIXED

**Summary:** Columbus City Council passed the Residential Rental Registry ordinance on April 15, 2026, requiring all rental units to register annually with owner contact info and an affidavit affirming code compliance. The registration fee is $15/unit, capped at $1,500 per complex. The ordinance has been in effect for four months but is still generating active coverage and community debate: tenant advocates note that proactive unit-level inspections were stripped from the final text after legal concerns about warrantless entry, while the Columbus Apartment Association has threatened legal action and remains on record opposing the registry. Feedback from property managers shows the compliance infrastructure (tenant notification, annual affidavit process) is generating practical friction for small-scale landlords particularly.

**Volume signal:** Loud in landlord/property manager channels; Steady in tenant advocacy media.

**Sentiment:** Negative (landlords and apartment associations); Frustrated/ambivalent (tenant advocates — "good first step but weakened"); Curious among individual tenants seeking practical guidance on what rights or benefits they actually gained.

**Representative quotes:**
- "Tenant advocates like BREAD say the proposal is a good first step, but could be strengthened further. However, tenant advocates note that the latest revisions have weakened the measure." — Sciotitle.com / Columbus City Council Rental Registry coverage (sciototitle.com/scioto-blog/columbus-city-council-passes-rental-registry-and-property-inspection-ordinance)
- "Landlord groups, including the Columbus Apartment Association, remain strongly opposed to the registry and have threatened legal action if it is approved." — Scioto Title, same source
- "Columbus Rental Registry Proposal Sparks Debate Between Landlords and Tenant Advocates" — Columbus Today / National Today headline (nationaltoday.com/us/oh/columbus/news/2026/02/23/columbus-rental-registry-proposal-sparks-debate-between-landlords-and-tenant-advocates/) [site proxy-blocked; headline from search snippet]

**Project frame:** Content idea — CREN has not run a tenant-side explainer on the rental registry that answers "what does this actually do for me as a renter?" The unit-inspection removal is the biggest practical gap between what advocates promised and what passed; a piece spelling out the real protections, the limits, and what tenants can actually do now would fill an underserved question in the conversation. Angle: "Columbus Passed a Rental Registry. Here's What It Does — and Doesn't — Protect Renters From." Secondary angle: landlord-side compliance guide for small operators who aren't professional management firms.

### 3. Intel / Silicon Heartland housing pressure in northeast suburbs — STEADY, MIXED/ANXIOUS

**Summary:** Intel's New Albany fab timeline has officially slipped to 2030–31 (first factory) and 2031–32 (second), but demand pressure in the northeast suburbs is already embedded in the market. New Albany median entry price is now ~$600K+. The commuter shed effect (workers priced out of core looking within 40–60 miles) is spreading demand into Licking, Delaware, and Madison counties. Franklin County closings fell 4.6% YoY in Feb 2026 while surrounding counties posted gains: Delaware +13%, Fairfield +20.2%, Madison +21.2%. Nationally, Intel's Silicon Heartland narrative is still generating coverage that brings out-of-state investor inquiries to Columbus-area brokers. Community sentiment has a split: tech-sector workers and investors are broadly positive; longtime east-side residents and housing advocates are expressing concern about displacement.

**Volume signal:** Steady — continuous coverage across tech, real estate, and advocacy outlets. Not breaking news volume but a durable background signal.

**Sentiment:** Mixed — positive among investors and tech workers; Anxious/concerned among east-side communities where displacement fears are loudest; Frustrated among first-time buyers priced out of northeast suburbs.

**Representative quotes:**
- "New residents from tech companies are worsening fears of gentrification and displacement among minority communities in central Ohio." — multiple search aggregations of Lantern / Midstory coverage
- "The massive Intel investment is no longer a future projection but a current economic driver, with the influx of high-income tech jobs creating localized demand pockets, particularly on the northeast side of the city." — theadvantagelending.com/blogs/columbus-ohio-housing-market-2026
- "Proximity to Intel's New Albany campus continues to drive demand and building permit activity, particularly in Licking County." — Rockford Homes market trends report 2026 (rockfordhomes.com/news/central-ohio-real-estate-market-trends-2026/)

**Project frame:** Content idea — the timeline delay (2030-31) is not widely understood in the community; many residents still think Intel opens "in a couple years." An explainer on what the delay means for housing pressure (the demand is already baked in via hiring and construction workforce, even before fab opening) would serve both investor and resident audiences. Pairs well with a map of the commuter shed / price appreciation gradient by county.

### 4. July 2026 inventory surge — highest in a decade, but still a seller's market — STEADY, POSITIVE/CAUTIOUS

Homes selling at ~98.5% of asking price. Supply is 2.4 months, still below the 4–6 months considered "balanced." ### 6. Emergency Housing Vouchers ending late 2026 — LIGHT in public conversation, HIGH STAKES — NEGATIVE/ANXIOUS

**Summary:** HUD announced in March 2025 that Emergency Housing Voucher funding would run out in late 2026, affecting roughly 5,200 households nationwide that haven't transitioned to standard Section 8. Columbus/Central Ohio impact is unclear but proportional to the metro's voucher population. This story has not broken into broad public conversation yet — it lives in housing advocacy media (COHHIO, HUD guidance documents) and property management industry outlets. Columbus's source-of-income protection ordinance (in effect since 2024) means landlords cannot refuse voucher holders, but the EHV program ending means the underlying support disappears for some households regardless of landlord acceptance.

**Volume signal:** Light in public conversation, though the deadline is weeks away and advocacy groups are likely mobilizing outreach.

**Sentiment:** Anxious/alarmed in advocacy media; Unaware in general public conversation.

**Representative quotes:**
- "HUD announced in March 2025 that Emergency Housing Voucher funding would run out in late 2026, affecting roughly 5,200 households nationwide that haven't transitioned to standard Section 8." — Steadily.com, Rent Increase Laws and Regulations in Columbus, OH (steadily.com/blog/rent-increase-laws-regulations-columbus-oh)

**Project frame:** Opportunity — time-sensitive story with a hard deadline. A CREN piece focused on the Columbus/Franklin County impact, how many local households hold EHVs, and what happens when the program ends would serve the advocacy and renter audience and is unlikely to be covered in detail by broker-focused outlets. Reach out to CMHA (Columbus Metropolitan Housing Authority) for local numbers.

### 7. Short North rents declining while suburbs surge — LIGHT, CURIOUS

**Summary:** Short North average rents have declined 3.67% YoY to $1,857/mo (down from $1,928), a countertrend to the broader Columbus market where rents rose 1.4% on average and suburban appreciation is strong. 82% of Short North households rent. This countertrend hasn't generated significant public discussion but is surfacing in rental market trend reports. The likely driver is increased urban apartment supply (construction completions in and near Short North in 2024–2025) absorbing demand at the high end.

**Volume signal:** Light — in market data reports only, not community conversation.

**Sentiment:** Curious — interesting data point for both renters (opportunity) and investors (caution on Short North multifamily).

**Representative quotes:**
- "The average rent in Short North has decreased 3.67% over the past year, moving from $1,928 to $1,857." — RentCafe, Average Rent in Short North, OH: 2026 Rent Prices by Neighborhood (rentcafe.com/average-rent-market-trends/us/oh/columbus/short-north/)

**Project frame:** Content idea — a neighborhood rental market roundup ("Where Columbus Rents Are Rising and Where They're Falling in 2026") using RentCafe/Zillow/Zumper data by neighborhood would be highly SEO-searchable and serve the renter audience that's underserved by the homebuying-focused coverage.
