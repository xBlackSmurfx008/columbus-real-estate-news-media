# Columbus' Weinland Park Gains 260 Apartments as The Guild Nears 2026 Opening

A mixed-use project that spent years in community negotiation is about to deliver on a promise made to Weinland Park residents. Marker Development's *The Guild* — a five-story, 260-unit apartment building at 303 E. 6th Ave. — is on track to open in 2026, bringing the most significant residential addition to the East 5th Avenue corridor in recent memory.

Seventy-eight of the 260 units are priced for households at or below 80% of the area median income (AMI), a meaningful share of affordable inventory in a neighborhood sandwiched between the appreciating Short North to the west and Ohio State University's campus to the north.

## A Name That Carries the Neighborhood's History

The project name is a deliberate callback to what stood here before. The site was formerly home to the Godman Guild Association, a social service organization that served Weinland Park residents for decades before selling the property to Marker Development. By naming the project *The Guild*, the developer signals continuity to a neighborhood that has watched newcomers arrive and long-timers depart.

That community buy-in matters in Weinland Park, and Marker appears to have earned it. The Columbus-Franklin County Finance Authority, which helped structure the deal's financing, noted that Marker and the Finance Authority worked closely with the Weinland Park Community Civic Association over several years to build mutual understanding of how the development would benefit surrounding residents — a process that shaped the unit mix and the affordable set-aside.

## 260 Units, 3,400 Square Feet of Retail, a Parking Garage

The Guild's full footprint spans approximately 365,000 square feet across the five-story structure. Ground-floor retail — roughly 3,400 sq ft — runs along the building's street edge, designed to activate the pedestrian experience on E. 6th Ave. An onsite parking garage rounds out the program.

The 78 affordable units are priced for 80% AMI households, which in the Columbus metro corresponds to roughly $50,000–$65,000 annual household income for a family of two to four. That places The Guild's affordable units squarely in the workforce housing tier: accessible to service workers, teachers, and entry-level professionals — not only the very low-income households served by deeper subsidy programs.

## How the Financing Works — and Why Operators Should Pay Attention

Marker structured The Guild using two mechanisms that Columbus developers are watching closely.

**Capital Lease program**: The Columbus-Franklin County Finance Authority's Capital Lease program lowers project construction costs by allowing the authority to hold title during construction, creating tax advantages that reduce total development cost. The savings create room for pricing a portion of the units below market without eroding project-level returns.

**PACE financing**: Commercial Property Assessed Clean Energy (PACE) financing was used to fund qualifying energy-efficiency improvements in the building. PACE is repaid through a property tax assessment rather than conventional debt service, which frees balance-sheet capacity during lease-up. Several Columbus developers have begun layering PACE onto multifamily projects as a way to hit energy standards without squeezing operating margins.

Together, the Capital Lease + PACE combination enabled Marker to deliver a 30% affordable set-aside in a market-rate-dominant neighborhood without relying on a direct public subsidy stack — Low-Income Housing Tax Credits, HOME funds, or CMHA vouchers. That is a financing playbook that operators pursuing affordability in Franklinton, the Near East Side, or the South Side could adapt for their own projects.

## Weinland Park at an Inflection Point

Weinland Park's story over the past decade has been one of cautious optimism followed by real momentum. The neighborhood's location — walking distance from the Short North's High Street restaurant row, the OSU medical and academic campus, and the growing Italian Village — has long made it attractive to investors. But rezoning battles, community concerns about displacement, and parcels held by longtime nonprofits slowed the development pipeline.

The Guild's delivery in 2026, combined with other projects advancing along E. 5th, suggests the neighborhood is entering a new phase. Marker's project at 303 E. 6th is the most prominent, but it's not alone: a separate 186-unit project at 200 E. 5th Ave. and the Thrive Companies' 4th & 5th mixed-use redevelopment — which adds structured public parking and ground-floor retail — are also active in the same geography. Rental comps will establish quickly once The Guild begins pre-leasing, setting benchmarks for the corridor.

## What to Watch

**Pre-leasing timeline**: Marker has not publicly announced a pre-leasing date. If a 2026 delivery holds, expect outreach to begin in late summer 2026.

**Retail tenants**: The 3,400 sq ft of ground-floor retail is unannounced. Given Weinland Park's demographics — a mix of OSU-area students, young professionals, and long-term residents — neighborhood-serving uses (café, pharmacy, health clinic) align with what community stakeholders have historically requested during engagement sessions.

**Affordability compliance**: Once units are leased, income eligibility for the 80% AMI units will be verified by a compliance firm or CMHA. Prospective affordable applicants should monitor Marker's website and the Franklin County housing waitlist for pre-leasing notices.

**Corridor pricing**: Watch whether The Guild's lease-up pricing sets new ceiling comps for Weinland Park or holds steady relative to Italian Village. The corridor has been historically priced below Short North — if The Guild's market-rate units push toward Short North comp levels, it will signal a repricing event for the neighborhood.
