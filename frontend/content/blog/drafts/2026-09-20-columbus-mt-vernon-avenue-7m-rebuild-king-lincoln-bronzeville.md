# Columbus Launches $7M Mt. Vernon Avenue Rebuild in King-Lincoln Bronzeville

**Published:** 2026-09-20 | **Category:** Neighborhoods | **Area:** Near East Side / King-Lincoln Bronzeville

---

Columbus broke ground September 16 on a $7 million overhaul of the Mt. Vernon Avenue corridor in King-Lincoln Bronzeville, bringing the historic Near East Side neighborhood its first protected bike lane along with new sidewalks, resurfaced roadway, and traffic signal upgrades from Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard to Champion Avenue.

## Groundbreaking on September 16

Mayor Andrew J. Ginther joined city officials and community members Wednesday, September 16, to break ground on the project at the Mt. Vernon Avenue corridor. Construction is led by the Columbus Department of Public Service under the city's [Vision Zero Columbus Action Plan](https://engage.columbus.gov/mtvernonavenuephase1), an initiative targeting a reduction in fatal and serious-injury traffic crashes across Columbus.

The project covers two connected segments. The first runs along Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard from Hamilton Avenue to St. Clair Avenue. The second continues on Mt. Vernon Avenue from St. Clair Avenue east to Champion Avenue — together spanning roughly a mile through the heart of the neighborhood.

## What the $7 Million Builds

Construction, scheduled to run from summer 2026 through summer 2027, will deliver:

- **A protected bike lane** with a concrete barrier separating cyclists from vehicle traffic — the first of its kind in King-Lincoln Bronzeville
- **New sidewalks** and upgraded curb infrastructure along the corridor
- **Resurfaced roadway**
- **Traffic signal upgrades** at several intersections

The bike lane advances Columbus's public commitment to build 25 miles of protected bike infrastructure citywide by 2028, [according to the Columbus Navigator](https://www.columbusnavigator.com/mt-vernon-avenue-protected-bike-lane/). That 25-mile goal is codified in the Vision Zero Columbus Action Plan, which the city adopted to address documented disparities in traffic fatality rates across neighborhoods.

## Why King-Lincoln Bronzeville

King-Lincoln Bronzeville is one of Columbus's historically significant Black neighborhoods, anchored by the Mt. Vernon Avenue corridor that once supported a dense district of shops, entertainment venues, and civic institutions. The neighborhood lost much of that commercial fabric after urban-renewal-era demolitions and freeway construction in the mid-20th century reduced both the physical corridor and the economic base that sustained it.

Public infrastructure investment in the area has been uneven in the decades since. The September 16 groundbreaking follows a period of concentrated attention on the Near East Side: Columbus City Council separately approved $3.6 million for storm sewer, water line, sidewalk, and streetlight improvements along Long Street in the King-Lincoln area, [according to WOSU Public Media](https://www.wosu.org/politics-government/2026-09-16/columbus-launches-street-improvement-project-in-bronzeville-neighborhood). Both projects represent a visible increase in city infrastructure spending within about a half-mile of the historic Mt. Vernon Avenue commercial core.

## Development Context

King-Lincoln Bronzeville adjoins a broader Near East Side corridor where CREN has tracked several significant real estate moves in the past months. Nationwide Children's Hospital purchased a $7.6 million block on East Main Street south of the neighborhood in early September ([CREN, September 2, 2026](/blog/2026-09-02-nationwide-children-s-buys-7-6m-east-main-street-block-no-plans-yet)). Second Baptist Church put 2.4 acres on East Long Street — along the northern edge of the area — out for development-partner proposals for affordable housing ([CREN, September 7, 2026](/blog/2026-09-07-second-baptist-opens-2-4-acres-on-columbus-near-east-side-to-developers)).

Protected bike infrastructure and pedestrian-grade sidewalk rebuilds have been associated with subsequent commercial-corridor reinvestment in urban planning research, though a direct causal relationship between any single street project and near-term property-value changes is difficult to isolate from other neighborhood factors. The City of Columbus has not published a projected economic-impact estimate specific to the Mt. Vernon Avenue Phase 1 project.

## What to Watch

The [project page on Engage Columbus](https://engage.columbus.gov/mtvernonavenuephase1) carries construction updates and any scope modifications through the expected completion in summer 2027. The city has not announced a phase-two street proposal for the remaining Mt. Vernon Avenue blocks east of Champion Avenue toward the 20th–21st Street stretch, where historic commercial storefronts still stand.

Progress on the separately funded Long Street utility improvements — the $3.6 million City Council-approved project — will be the next infrastructure checkpoint for this part of King-Lincoln Bronzeville. No completion date has been published for that project.
