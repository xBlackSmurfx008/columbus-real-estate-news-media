Columbus is in the middle of the most consequential overhaul of its zoning code in more than 70 years — and Phase 2 is where commercial and industrial property owners need to start paying attention.

Phase 2 of the city's Zone In initiative is now in active code-drafting mode. When it's complete, roughly **66,000 parcels — representing 43% of the entire city** — will operate under new zoning districts. The targets this round: commercial strips, industrial corridors, warehouse districts, and major institutional campuses.

City Council is expected to vote on the final Phase 2 code sometime in 2026. The detailed standards — building heights, permitted uses, transitions between zones, design requirements — are being written right now.

If you own or operate commercial or industrial property in Columbus, the window to understand and influence what's coming is still open. Once the code passes, the rules are set.

## What Zone In Phase 2 Actually Covers

Phase 1 of Zone In tackled the city's major transit corridors — roughly 140 miles of roadway, covering about 13,000 parcels. Those changes unlocked capacity for tens of thousands of new housing units along High Street, Broad Street, and other high-traffic arteries. The impact was concentrated: corridors that could now build taller and denser, by right, without variances.

Phase 2 is different in scope and character. City planners have turned their attention to the parcels surrounding and sitting between those corridors:

- **Commercial strips and urban retail zones** — underutilized strip centers, auto-oriented commercial, neighborhood retail blocks
- **Industrial and warehouse districts** — active and legacy industrial land across the metro
- **Major institutional campuses** — hospital complexes, university-adjacent parcels, large corporate campuses

The city has framed Phase 2 under an "Economic and Housing Opportunity" strategy, signaling that the intent is to enable mixed-use redevelopment — housing layered with commercial — in areas historically locked into single-use designations.

The 66,000 parcels in scope are distributed citywide, not concentrated on any single corridor. Their locations span neighborhoods from the Near East Side to Hilliard, from the Olentangy corridor to the industrial zones south of downtown.

## How Phase 2 Differs from Phase 1

The distinction matters for operators. Phase 1 was primarily a housing unlocking exercise along corridors that already had mixed-use character — adding density to places that were already commercial-residential hybrids in practice.

Phase 2 is more contested. It's asking what to do with land that has historically been single-use: industrial warehouses, surface parking-heavy commercial, institutional buffers. The answer will vary parcel by parcel, and the city's approach is to distinguish between:

- Areas **primed for enhanced mixed-use development** — where new districts would enable housing, denser commercial, or mixed-use redevelopment
- Areas targeted for **continued or enhanced industrial and warehouse operations** — where the code would preserve and potentially clarify industrial uses rather than open the door to residential

Which category a parcel lands in will determine whether its as-of-right development capacity expands, stays the same, or narrows. That classification is being made in the code-drafting work happening right now.

## The Community Input Factor

Not every stakeholder has been aligned with the city's pace or approach. WOSU Public Media documented a rift between city planners and some neighborhood representatives earlier in the process, with Area Commissions — the neighborhood advisory bodies with formal standing in Columbus land use decisions — raising concerns about how the new land use map was developed and whether neighborhood character would be adequately protected.

Those concerns have not stopped the process, but they have shaped it. The city extended the Area Commission input window as recently as late 2025, and Luis Teba, the city's Zone In project manager, has continued outreach to commissions and neighborhood groups as the detailed standards are developed in 2026.

For operators, this matters practically: parcels in neighborhoods with active, organized Area Commissions may see different outcomes in the final code than parcels in areas where commission engagement has been lower. Monitoring your local commission's position on Zone In is a concrete early-warning step.

## The Columbus Growth Strategy Connection

Alongside the detailed zoning code work, the city is developing a broader **Columbus Growth Strategy** — a companion land use document intended to guide where density and investment are encouraged citywide. The Growth Strategy and the Zone In Phase 2 code are being developed in parallel, which means the vision document and the enforceable rules will be released in the same legislative cycle.

This is relevant for operators thinking beyond individual parcels. The Growth Strategy will likely identify priority investment corridors, areas targeted for affordable housing density, and zones where industrial preservation is a policy goal. Understanding the Growth Strategy's framework before the code is adopted gives operators a read on the city's long-term intentions for their neighborhoods.

## Timeline to Watch

Here's where the process stands as of May 2026:

| Milestone | Status |
|---|---|
| Phase 2 land use framework (the "what goes where" layer) | Approved by City Council — complete |
| Detailed zoning code drafting (building types, heights, use tables) | Underway — in progress |
| Area Commission outreach and community input | Ongoing |
| City Council vote on Phase 2 zoning code | Target: 2026 (no specific month confirmed publicly) |
| Phase 3 (residential districts) | Target: 2027 |

The practical window — when operator input and advocacy can shape the final standards — is between now and whenever the legislation is introduced for a Council vote.

## What Operators Should Do Now

If you're a Columbus CRE operator or investor with commercial or industrial assets in scope, here's the short checklist:

**1. Confirm your parcel's Phase 2 status.** The City of Columbus Building and Zoning Services maintains Zone In mapping. Identifying whether your parcel is in the Phase 2 area, and which proposed district it falls into, is the first step. [needs source: direct link to Zone In interactive parcel map from columbus.gov]

**2. Attend or monitor your Area Commission.** Commissions that engage actively with Zone In code drafting carry more weight in how the final standards are written. Knowing your commission's position — and showing up to make yours — still matters before the vote.

**3. Track the City Council agenda.** When Phase 2 legislation is introduced, operators get a final public testimony window before the vote. That date hasn't been set, but it's coming in 2026.

**4. Model both scenarios now.** If your parcel could plausibly land in either a mixed-use opportunity district or an industrial preservation zone, run the development math on both outcomes today — before the code determines which lane you're in.

Columbus has moved faster and more boldly on zoning reform than almost any other Midwest city its size. Phase 2 will determine what's buildable on nearly half of Columbus for the next generation. The time to engage is before the rules are written, not after.
