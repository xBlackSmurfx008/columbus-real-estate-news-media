Grove City City Council is taking up a pivotal ordinance tonight — one that would authorize a development agreement with Axiom Ventures for the $65 million Broadway Live project, the most ambitious mixed-use proposal to hit the southwestern Columbus suburb's town center in years.

If approved, Ordinance C-19-26 clears the way for a food hall, apartments, outdoor plaza, and bar to rise at the corner of Broadway and Columbus Street in the heart of Grove City.

For Columbus-area real estate operators and investors watching the suburban mixed-use market, this is worth following closely.

## What Grove City City Council Is Voting On

Tonight's ordinance authorizes the City Administrator to execute a development agreement with Axiom Ventures, LLC — a Columbus-based developer. The agreement is the formal mechanism that binds both parties to the Broadway Live preliminary development plan, which the city approved in an earlier planning phase.

The ordinance had its first reading on May 4, 2026. Tonight, May 18, is the second and final reading before a potential vote.

Key terms of the agreement include:

- The City of Grove City will contribute **$4 million** upon its acceptance of a 125-space public parking facility and the developer's conveyance of approximately 2.19 acres of open space and public plaza land.
- Axiom Ventures bears responsibility for the project's construction timeline and financial performance.
- The agreement includes a timeline provision tied to "timely completion" of the project.
- A conditional precedent — FEMA's approval of a Conditional Letter of Map Revision (CLOMR) — must be satisfied before the project can break ground. Axiom estimates the FEMA process will take six to nine months from the filing date.

The city's $4M contribution is not a blank check. It is explicitly tied to the delivery of public infrastructure: the 125-space parking lot and the open-space land conveyance. That structure keeps the subsidy accountable to a physical deliverable rather than a construction milestone.

## What Broadway Live Includes

Broadway Live, as approved under the preliminary development plan, is a mixed-use development on approximately 5.8 acres at the corner of Broadway and Columbus Street — the symbolic center of Grove City's historic town center.

The project's program:

- **Food hall**: 16,400 square feet, multi-tenant format with bar
- **Residential**: Up to 150 apartments (exact count and unit mix not yet locked per available city documents)
- **Outdoor plaza**: Public open space with a performance stage
- **Ground-floor activation**: Retail-facing frontage along Broadway

The site currently consists largely of surface parking and lower-density commercial uses. The food hall is positioned as the anchor activation element — drawing foot traffic that supports the residential and retail components.

The design reflects a growing pattern in Columbus's suburban ring: towns that built out around the car are now trying to retrofit walkable density into their historical cores, using food-and-beverage as the primary traffic generator. <!-- internal link: Zone In zoning overhaul context → /content/blog/drafts/2026-05-01-columbus-zoning-overhaul-zone-in-operators-guide-2026.md -->

## The FEMA Hurdle: What It Means for Timeline

The most significant near-term uncertainty in Broadway Live's path is a regulatory one that has nothing to do with Columbus or Grove City's local planning apparatus.

Before ground can break, Axiom Ventures must obtain FEMA's approval of a CLOMR — a Conditional Letter of Map Revision — related to the project site's relationship to a mapped flood zone. A CLOMR is FEMA's mechanism for reviewing and conditionally approving proposed changes to floodplain conditions before construction begins.

Axiom has estimated the FEMA review will take six to nine months. The city's agreement anticipates this timeline and conditions the development accordingly.

This is a material detail for operators evaluating the project's risk profile: Broadway Live, even if approved by council tonight, is unlikely to break ground before late 2026 at the earliest — and possibly not until early-to-mid 2027 if the FEMA process encounters delays. The CLOMR is not a discretionary local approval; it is a federal engineering review that follows its own schedule.

## Why This Matters for Columbus-Area CRE Operators

**Grove City's market position is improving.** The southwestern Columbus suburb has historically been overshadowed by the Short North and Clintonville as a target for mixed-use investment, but its population base — and its proximity to jobs along the I-71 and I-270 corridors — has grown substantially. A $65M food-hall-anchored project with 150 apartments in the town center, if delivered, represents a step-change in Grove City's walkable retail and rental stock.

**The public-private structure is replicable.** The $4M participation tied to public parking and open space delivery is the kind of deal mechanics that other Columbus-ring suburbs will watch. If Broadway Live performs — and if the food hall generates the foot traffic its underwriting assumes — it becomes a template for similar town-center activations in Westerville, Canal Winchester, or Dublin. <!-- internal link: West Broad BRT suburban mixed-use context → /content/blog/drafts/2026-05-05-columbus-west-broad-brt-delay-hilltop-franklinton.md -->

**Multifamily supply dynamics matter here.** Central Ohio's April 2026 housing data showed active inventory up 13.4% year-over-year metro-wide, but new multifamily supply in suburban Columbus remains concentrated in the northeast. A 150-unit delivery in the southwest, anchored by a food destination, would be differentiated product in a submarket that has little comparable competition.

**The FEMA path is a watch item, not a red flag.** CLOMR approvals are common in floodplain-adjacent infill development, and Axiom's six-to-nine-month estimate is consistent with typical federal review timelines. The risk is delay, not denial — but operators and investors tracking the project should plan accordingly.

## What Comes Next

If Grove City City Council approves Ordinance C-19-26 tonight, Axiom Ventures will begin — or continue — the FEMA CLOMR process. Council approval is the last major local approval hurdle; the remaining path is primarily federal (FEMA) and then financing and permitting.

A council approval tonight does not guarantee the project gets built — financing conditions, FEMA timing, and market conditions all remain live variables. But it does formally commit both the city and the developer to the terms of the agreement, with the city's $4M public participation on the line.
